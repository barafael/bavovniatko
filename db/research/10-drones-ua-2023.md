# 10 — Ukrainian small drones as the player flies them, 2023

Scope: the small drones Ukrainian units flew at Vuhledar (main Russian assault 6 February 2023, 72nd Mechanised Brigade defending) and south of Mala Tokmachka / Robotyne (breach of 7–8 June 2023, 47th Mechanised Brigade attacking), with a short look at 2024–2025. This file covers what the drones were, how many there were, how they flew, what they dropped, how they cued fires, and how fast they were lost.

Accessed 2026-10-15. English-language sources, Ukrainian outlets paired with Western analysis. Russian claims appear only as Ukrainian or Western outlets report them. **Reported** means a figure a named party states: a count, a manufacturer spec or a stated rate. **Estimate** means a figure the source itself presents as approximate, derived or anecdotal. Manufacturer specs are peacetime figures for a stock aircraft. Where only a republication of a paywalled original could be fetched, the republication is cited and the dependence is flagged.

## 1. Summary

- **The Mavic 3 was the workhorse at both dates.** In mid-2023 Ukrainska Pravda called the DJI Mavic 3 "the most commonly used type of reconnaissance drone in the war" and "the classic" of the Ukrainian battlefield [src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table]. CNAS, drawing on WindtalkerX detections, likewise found the Mavic 3 the most common commercial drone on both sides from October 2022 to March 2023 [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- **Thermal drones were spreading.** The same CNAS data show "a notable uptick" in Mavic 3 Enterprise E/T and Matrice 30T drones in October 2022 – March 2023, because they can fly at night [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- **At Vuhledar (Jan–Feb 2023) the player is a spotter.** Ukrainian officials credited a Mavic 3 and a Matrice M30T with spotting a Russian convoy and passing its coordinates to artillery [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t]. Serhiy Leshchenko said a Matrice handed to the 72nd Brigade two weeks earlier adjusted fire on the 155th Naval Infantry Brigade [src:militarnyi-2023-vuhledar-vehicles]. Small grenade drops are also documented there [src:unherd-2023-brown-vuhledar][src:19fortyfive-2023-buckby-grenade-hatch].
- **FPVs were rare in February 2023 and becoming routine by late summer.** GeoConfirmed's count of documented Ukrainian FPV hits was about 10 in February 2023, about 50 in April, over 100 in July and over 250 in September [src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-fpv-small-drones]. A Washington Post team at Robotyne in September 2023 wrote that FPVs, "first used in substantial numbers earlier this year", were "now the predominant attack drone in Ukraine" [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].
- **In the June 2023 counteroffensive drones scouted by day and bombed by night.** RUSI describes daytime reconnaissance locating Russian positions that converted agricultural drones then attacked at night with RPG rounds [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Its example is five such drones that destroyed or badly damaged seven tanks and were all lost [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Russian EW decided whether the drones flew at all.** RUSI's spring-2023 estimate was about 10,000 Ukrainian UAVs lost a month [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Its June 2023 judgement was that Ukrainian bomber drones "could not get in" while Russian EW was on [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].

## 2. At Vuhledar, Jan–Feb 2023

**What flew.**
- **Mavic 3 and Matrice M30T.** On 17 February 2023 Militarnyi relayed Fedorov's account that "DJI Mavic 3 Fly More Combo and DJI Matrice M30T 'birds'" saw a Russian convoy in time and passed its coordinates to artillery [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t].
  - Fedorov credited the 21st Separate Battalion, the 55th Artillery Brigade and the 72nd Mechanised Brigade, with drones bought through UNITED24 [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t].
  - The claimed result was two BMP-3s, a BTR-82A and a T-80BV damaged and left behind [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t]. This is a Ukrainian official claim.
- **A Matrice adjusting fire.** On 28 January 2023 Serhiy Leshchenko said "the fire was adjusted by our Matrice drone", which had been handed to the 72nd Brigade two weeks earlier [src:militarnyi-2023-vuhledar-vehicles].
- **A Matrice 300 in the 72nd since 2022.** Fedorov handed the 72nd a DJI Matrice 300 with a thermal imager in August 2022, for watching positions and vehicle movement "day and night" [src:ukrinform-2022-72nd-matrice-300].
- **Other spotting and dropping drones.** The knowledge base already records DJI Matrice and similar quadcopters for spotting at Vuhledar, and small drones dropping charges [src:militarnyi-2023-vuhledar-vehicles][src:cnn-2023-lister-vuhledar-turkeys]. The 68th Jaeger Brigade near Vuhledar carried "handheld drones" alongside heavy machine guns and old anti-tank guns [src:unherd-2023-brown-vuhledar].
- **No FPV found.** No source found documents a Ukrainian FPV strike at Vuhledar in January–February 2023. Nationally GeoConfirmed counted only about 10 documented Ukrainian FPV hits in February 2023 [src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-fpv-small-drones], so FPVs at Vuhledar would at most have been a rarity (an inference).
- **No heavy bomber or fixed-wing found.** No source found places an R18, a Vampire, a converted agricultural drone, a Leleka or a Shark over Vuhledar in this window.

**Where they flew from.**
- UnHerd's reporter was taken to "a Ukrainian drone base a mile from the frontline" near Vuhledar in March 2023 [src:unherd-2023-brown-vuhledar].
- On the southern front in March 2023, a volunteer drone unit worked with Russian positions "only a few kilometers away" [src:kyivindependent-2023-query-war-from-above].
- The town's high-rises were also used as observation posts, so the drone was one of two eyes on the plain [src:kyivpost-2023-korshak-vuhledar-marines].

**What the drones saw and did.**
- Lt. Bayak of the 72nd Brigade described the kill zone as starting with a drone video feed spotting a column of about 15 tanks and APCs [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle].
- One Ukrainian T-64 crew fired from about three miles short of its ambush site at targets it could not see, on coordinates a drone pilot called over the radio [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle].
- "Day after day", Russian columns were "crushed by accurate shell strikes adjusted by" drones or by observers on the tallest buildings [src:kyivpost-2023-korshak-vuhledar-marines].
- Drone footage of columns hitting mines went viral, and the worst day was 6 February [src:forbes-2023-axe-vuhledar-halt].
- A drone dropped an RGD-5 grenade "right through the driver's hatch" of a Russian T-72B3 near Vuhledar, according to Ukraine Weapons Tracker in February 2023 [src:19fortyfive-2023-buckby-grenade-hatch]. The tank may already have been abandoned after an earlier hit [src:19fortyfive-2023-buckby-grenade-hatch].
- A 68th Brigade soldier said Russian marines did not run from a grenade-carrying drone: they waited, "step aside behind a tree", and kept fighting [src:unherd-2023-brown-vuhledar].

**Conditions.**
- On 6–10 February highs were −3.6 to +0.2 °C and lows −6.4 to −11.4 °C, with maximum winds of about 26 km/h on 7–8 February [src:open-meteo-historical-archive]. Sunrise was about 06:50 and sunset about 16:40 [src:open-meteo-historical-archive].
- DJI rates the Mavic 3 for −10 to 40 °C and 12 m/s of wind [src:dji-mavic3-specs]. So the coldest Vuhledar nights were at or below its rated minimum, while the wind was within limits (an inference from the two sources).
- Russian EW was already biting on the neighbouring Huliaipole sector. One unit that moved there in January 2023 lost or disabled three Mavics in two weeks, and a jammed Matrice crashed into a snowy field [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-jamming-fails].

## 3. In the June 2023 counteroffensive

**Quadcopters and the command post's picture.**
- RUSI found that only 3% of Ukrainian artillery fire missions were smoke, partly because smoke blinded "the view from UAVs which higher Ukrainian echelons and command posts use" to manage the fight [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Commanders "persistently prioritise maintaining their own understanding of the battlefield over laying down smoke" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- In a breach RUSI describes on the neighbouring Velyka Novosilka axis in June 2023, the command post watched two Russian tanks emerge "via UAV feeds" and called fires on them [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. No equivalent first-hand account was found for the 47th Brigade's 7–8 June breach.
- Ukrainian units kept using drones as spotters through the summer, but flew "only … when they found EW gaps" and changed radio frequencies often [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- RUSI and ISW both report Russian EW disrupting Ukrainian drones on the Zaporizhzhia front in June 2023 [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak][src:isw-2023-06-08-roca].
- The weather on 7–10 June was dry, with cloud rising from 69% to 100%, sunrise 04:40 and sunset 20:30 [src:open-meteo-historical-archive]. Night lasted only about eight hours [src:open-meteo-historical-archive].

**Night bombers.**
- Ukrainian units degraded Russian tactical reserves by day-time reconnaissance followed by night attacks with "converted agricultural UAVs dropping RPGs" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- In one incident five UAVs, each carrying four RPG rounds, struck a company of Russian tanks in a wood block. They destroyed or seriously damaged seven tanks, and all five drones were lost [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- The tactic was "fairly binary": with Russian EW active the drones "could not get in and usually were not committed" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- RUSI's photograph of such a converted agricultural drone is dated July 2023 [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].

**FPVs.**
- By September 2023 the 47th Brigade had a strike drone company flying FPVs from "a forward bunker position on the southern front line near Robotyne" [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].
  - It produced and used about 20 drones a day, and its pilot said "there are never enough drones" [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].
  - In one day the reporters watched, the team flew "three flights, three misses" [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].
- Whether the 47th flew FPVs in the June breach is not stated in any source found. In June 2023 GeoConfirmed's count of documented Ukrainian FPV hits nationally was still between about 50 (April) and 100 (July) a month [src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-fpv-small-drones].
- The Army of Drones fielded its first three strike drone companies in March 2023 and eight more in May 2023, the latter equipped with pickups and FPV drones [src:pravda-2023-shumilin-3-strike-companies][src:pravda-2023-balachuk-8-strike-companies].
- By September 2023 a 47th Brigade soldier said "Russia has the edge in FPV drones" [src:kyivindependent-2023-farrell-47th-surovikin-line].
- RUSI's later review says Ukrainian forces used "an expanding number" of FPVs "as they moved onto the defensive" in late 2023 and as artillery ammunition ran short [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Scale on the southern front.** Of 300 Mavic 3T thermal drones delivered by the Army of Drones in March 2023, 85 went to the Zaporizhzhia direction, 160 to the east and 55 to Kherson [src:ukrinform-2023-300-mavic-3t].

## 5. Drops and FPV strikes

**Drop munitions.**
- **VOG-17.** A modified anti-personnel VOG-17 grenade weighing 350 g is the commonest drone bomb [src:ctc-2025-hambling-moving-targets]. Its claimed six-metre radius "frequently fails to incapacitate the target and multiple drops are needed" [src:ctc-2025-hambling-moving-targets].
- **Carrying.** A Mavic typically carries two VOG-17-type munitions or one larger grenade, on a 3D-printed harness triggered by the drone's own light [src:ctc-2025-hambling-moving-targets]. In 2022 one serviceman showed a Mavic 3 dropping VOG-17s one at a time or together, with a stabilising tail, from about 60 m [src:gagadget-2022-panasovskiy-mavic3-vog].
- **Improvised bombs.** A volunteer unit on the southern front in March 2023 dropped handmade bombs with 3D-printed fins and "tiny metal balls" packed around the charge [src:kyivindependent-2023-query-war-from-above].
- **Hand grenades.** An RGD-5 was dropped through a T-72B3's driver's hatch near Vuhledar [src:19fortyfive-2023-buckby-grenade-hatch].
- **RKG-3 and RKG-1600.** These Soviet anti-tank grenades, given 3D-printed tail fins, fire a copper slug that can penetrate more than 20 cm of armour [src:rferl-2022-rkg3-grenades]. The RKG-1600 version weighs 1.6 kg, and R18 crews dropped it from 100–300 m [src:mezha-2022-danylov-r18].
- **RPG rounds.** Converted agricultural drones dropped four RPG rounds each in June–July 2023 [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **VOG-25 and F-1.** No source found documents them as drone drops at these two battles.

**Accuracy and wind.**
- A skilled operator "can drop grenades with great accuracy" from a hover described as "rock-steady" [src:ctc-2025-hambling-moving-targets].
- The only numeric accuracy figure is the RKG-1600's claimed 1 m from 300 m [src:mezha-2022-danylov-r18].
- No source found quantifies wind drift on drops. Wind is reported as a cause of FPV misses near Robotyne [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].

**Effects.**
- **On men.** Grenade-sized HE-FRAG payloads have "a lethal radius of several metres" [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike]. Russian marines at Vuhledar sheltered behind trees as the grenade fell [src:unherd-2023-brown-vuhledar].
- **On vehicles.** Small HE-FRAG drops damage vehicles "only if delivered accurately into hatches or on to fuel tanks or ammunition" [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike].
- **On engine decks.** An FPV pilot claimed in 2023 that a correct hit on a tank's rear engine compartment has "a 70% chance it will ignite", and that a track hit immobilises the tank for artillery to finish [src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table]. This is one pilot's estimate.
- **Armour in general.** FPVs have "an unreliable effect on armoured targets, requiring multiple hits to kill" [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike]. CNAS adds that at best they often disable large vehicles for artillery to finish [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].

**Documented hits on armour.**
- A T-72B3 near Vuhledar in February 2023, by a grenade through the hatch; it was possibly already abandoned [src:19fortyfive-2023-buckby-grenade-hatch].
- Seven Russian tanks destroyed or badly damaged by five bomber drones in one night strike in summer 2023, place not named [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- The 3rd Assault Brigade's drone chief told the Washington Post in October 2023 that his brigade's FPVs had destroyed four tanks in one day, and called FPVs "the main antitank weapon" [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate].
- Fedorov claimed Army of Drones units hit 428 pieces of Russian equipment in one week in October 2023, during the Russian offensive on Avdiivka [src:pravda-2023-romanenko-army-of-drones-428]. This is an official claim.

## 6. Cueing fires

**At Vuhledar.**
- **Who watched.** Fedorov credited drones working with the 21st Separate Battalion, the 55th Artillery Brigade and the 72nd Mechanised Brigade with seeing a convoy and passing its coordinates to the artillery [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t]. Observers on the high-rises did the same [src:kyivpost-2023-korshak-vuhledar-marines].
- **Who adjusted.** A Matrice adjusted fire while Javelin teams of the 72nd engaged the 155th Brigade [src:militarnyi-2023-vuhledar-vehicles]. A drone pilot called coordinates by radio to a T-64 firing indirectly from about three miles back [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle].
- **The sequence.** In Lt. Bayak's account, the drone feed spots the column, the ATGM teams in the tree lines open fire, drivers swerve onto the mined shoulders, and then artillery hits the stalled column and the crews climbing out [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle].
- **Smoke did not save the Russians.** On 6 February artillery fired into the head of a smoke screen behind which Russian vehicles had bunched up [src:forbes-2023-axe-vuhledar-halt].

**In the June 2023 offensive.**
- Command posts at brigade level and above used drone feeds to manage the fight, at the cost of rarely firing smoke [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- Fire control was centralised at higher echelons, which "limits mission command" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- The day-reconnaissance, night-bomber cycle was a drone-only kill chain against Russian reserves [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].

**How the picture reached commanders.**
- **Kropyva.** This Army SOS tablet app maps targets, calculates fire missions and "takes drone data to compute fire corrections" [src:forbes-2022-hambling-kropyva]. Linking Furia, Leleka and PD-1 to Kropyva had sped up Ukrainian artillery since before 2022 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Delta.** Delta was broadly operational from August 2022, and its full deployment was approved on 4 February 2023, two days before Black Monday at Vuhledar [src:wikipedia-delta]. It fuses satellite, sensor, drone and intercept data [src:united24-2025-daly-starlink-military].
- **Starlink.** An Aerorozvidka officer said a thermal drone at night "must connect through Starlink to the artillery guy" [src:united24-2025-daly-starlink-military]. The first Army of Drones strike companies were issued Starlink terminals and pickups in March 2023 [src:pravda-2023-shumilin-3-strike-companies]. Later in 2023 one unit flew from Starlink-equipped pickups and moved launch points to wherever the signal was best [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-jamming-fails].
- **Radio.** At Vuhledar coordinates also went by voice radio [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle]. Russian EW could intercept and decrypt Ukrainian Motorola traffic in near real time in 2023 [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Gap.** No source found says which of these links the 72nd or the 47th used on the two chapter dates.

**Why the curve was steep.**
- In mid-2023 a new drone control or navigation system was at peak effectiveness for about two weeks, degraded over four more, and was being jammed across the front within 6–12 weeks [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike].
- About 25–50 UAVs from both sides were typically over each 10 km of contested ground at any one time in spring 2023 [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- Russian EW was "weapons free", with at least one major EW system per 10 km of front [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Shipovnik-Aero can take control of drones and geolocate their operators [src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-economist-ew].
- Russian EW forced Ukrainian commercial drones to land and then turned them against their owners. DJI models can show the original take-off position, which exposes the pilots [src:kyivindependent-2023-query-war-from-above].
- Ukrainian commanders also limited their own jamming, to keep their drones and situational awareness working [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Aeroscope and operator detection.**
- **How it works.** AeroScope reads DJI drones' broadcast identity and locates both drone and pilot [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones]. CNAS says it tracked drones and operators "more than 20 miles away" and logged flight paths, altitudes, speeds and pilot locations [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- **Casualties.** An Aerorozvidka specialist confirmed in July 2022 that Ukrainians had been targeted and killed by Russian users of the system [src:rferl-2022-aeroscope-aerorozvidka]. An instructor said casualties were "initially high" because operators took too few precautions [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones].
- **Speed.** The Karlson unit's spokesman said Russia could find and target its operators in "as little as five or six minutes" [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones].
- **Counters.** From May 2022 open-source software let pilots switch off DJI Remote ID [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones]. Sources say DJI drones from about June 2022 had firmware that blocked this [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones]. Ukraine "eventually" had software to disable the tracking, and DJI stopped selling AeroScope [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- **Why operators stay exposed.** Short ranges keep quadcopter and FPV operators "relatively proximate to the drone", so finding them by radio and shelling them is a standing threat [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- **FPV pilots too.** A pilot in goggles cannot see his surroundings and "usually needs a partner to ensure their safety" [src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table]. One FPV pilot's ground station was destroyed by artillery in July 2023 [src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table].

**Team size and density.**
- Quadcopter crews usually had two or more operators: one flying and one spotting, matching the feed to a map [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution].
- The southern volunteer unit in March 2023 had 15 people [src:kyivindependent-2023-query-war-from-above].
- R18 crews were a pilot, a navigator and a sapper, trained in groups of three or four crews [src:euromaidan-2023-hrudka-r18].
- RUSI's later design rule is for each platoon to be able to generate two UAVs, so that launches can be staggered [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike]. This is a recommendation, not a 2023 establishment.
- No 2023 source found gives a brigade's or battalion's drone holdings for the 72nd or the 47th.

## 8. How it changed, 2024–2025

- **FPVs became mass munitions.** Ukraine had 3,000–5,000 FPVs in 2022 [src:justsecurity-2026-bidochko-drone-superpower]. In mid-2023 an aerial-reconnaissance centre head estimated Ukraine made fewer than 10,000 quality FPVs a month, against 45,000–50,000 for Russia [src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table]. Defence-ministry figures imply about 1.2m FPVs received in 2024 and 3m in 2025 [src:pravda-2025-hubina-3m-fpv].
- **Hit rates stayed low until autonomy.** RUSI found 60–80% of FPVs failing to reach their targets in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-reynolds-third-year]. CSIS reports 10–15% success under jamming, rising to a claimed 70–80% with terminal autonomy [src:csis-2025-bondar-ai-autonomy].
- **Fibre.** Russian fibre-optic FPVs arrived in August 2024. Ukraine had about 1 in 10 teams on fibre by May 2025, and its fibre reach grew to 40+ km by July 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-farrell-fiber-optic][src:ukrinform-2025-fedorov-fiber-optic-40km].
- **Heavy bombers.** The Vampire became a mass reusable bomber: about $8,500, about 100,000 a year and about a dozen sorties per airframe (2026 figures) [src:euromaidan-2026-axe-bomber-drones].
- **Organisation.** The Unmanned Systems Forces became a separate branch in June 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-usf-drone-line-group]. The Drone Line, announced in February 2025, aimed at a kill zone 10–15 km deep [src:kyivindependent-2025-goncharova-drone-line-launch].
- **The battlefield.** By 2024 RUSI found it dense with observation within 3 km of the line and taskable to 40 km [src:rusi-2025-watling-reynolds-third-year]. Russia's Rubikon centre made Ukrainian drone teams a priority target, with over 25% of its strikes on them by September 2025 [src:rferl-2025-rubicon].
- **Data links.** Delta's Mission Control was handling about 106,000 drone missions a month in 2024 [src:csis-2024-bondar-delta-cjadc2]. Under the e-points scheme units upload confirmed hits to Delta and spend the points on the Brave1 Market, and they had ordered over UAH 5bn this way since August 2025 [src:digitalstate-2025-brave1-epoints].

## 9. Game/sim relevance

- **Vuhledar loadout (6 Feb 2023).** Give the player a Mavic 3 (day) and a Matrice M30T (thermal, laser rangefinder, −20 °C rating), with Mavic speeds of 15 m/s normal and 21 m/s sport and about 40 minutes per battery [src:dji-mavic3-specs][src:dji-matrice30-specs][src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-vuhledar-mavic-m30t].
  - The main verb is "spot and adjust": coordinates go to artillery or to a T-64 firing indirectly [src:nyt-2023-kramer-epic-tank-battle].
  - Drops should be a minor option: two VOG-17s, with weak effect on men in cover and none on armour except through an open hatch [src:ctc-2025-hambling-moving-targets][src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike].
  - Leave FPVs out, or make them very rare [src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-fpv-small-drones].
- **Robotyne loadout (8 Jun 2023).** Give the player Mavic 3 / 3T recon, with feeds watched at the command post [src:ukrinform-2023-300-mavic-3t][src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
  - Night bombers (an agricultural conversion with four RPG rounds, or an R18 with 5 kg) can be committed only in an EW gap [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak][src:mezha-2022-danylov-r18].
  - FPVs should be scarce in June and become a stock item (about 20 a day per strike company) by September [src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate][src:euromaidan-2023-voichuk-fpv-small-drones].
- **Range is the EW budget, not the spec sheet.** The stock link is 8–15 km, but 1.5–3 km under strong interference and about 500 m under heavy jamming [src:dji-mavic3-specs][src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution]. Model link quality falling with distance and with each Russian EW bubble, and make bomber launches a binary go/no-go on EW state [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Attrition as a currency.** Use about three flights per quadcopter as the baseline life, with a skilled-pilot tail [src:cnas-2024-pettyjohn-evolution]. Five Mavics a week for a busy team is a sane resupply rate [src:kyivindependent-2023-query-war-from-above]. In June 2023, losing all five bombers in a successful strike is normal [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **The operator is a target.** Each flight from a fixed launch point should raise the chance that Russian fire finds the team within minutes [src:c4isrnet-2022-skove-cloak-drones]. Moving the launch point, as units did in 2023, should reset it [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-jamming-fails].
- **FPV is a skill shot with poor odds.** Use about one hit in five, an analog feed that breaks up under jamming, a few minutes of battery and wind as a miss factor [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike][src:wapo-2023-horton-korolchuk-fpv-intimate][src:pravda-2023-khalilov-fpv-negotiating-table]. A tank usually needs several hits or artillery to finish it [src:rusi-2024-bronk-watling-mass-precision-strike].
- **Weather and light matter.** Vuhledar nights ran below −10 °C, near or below the Mavic's rating, with about 10 hours of daylight; Robotyne had only about eight hours of darkness, so night-only bombers have a short window [src:open-meteo-historical-archive][src:dji-mavic3-specs].
- **Smoke versus seeing.** Laying smoke should blind the player's own drone picture, which is why Ukrainian brigades fired so little of it [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].

## 10. Open questions/gaps

- **Unit holdings.** No source found gives drone numbers for the 72nd Brigade in February 2023 or the 47th Brigade in June 2023 (per brigade, battalion or team).
- **The 47th on 7–8 June.** No first-hand account was found of the 47th's own drones during the 7–8 June breach, including whether it flew FPVs then.
- **Recon altitude and stand-off.** No 2023 source states the altitude or stand-off at which Mavic operators held over Vuhledar or Robotyne. Only drop altitudes (about 60 m for a Mavic, 100–300 m for the R18) were found.
- **FPV parameters.** No 2023 source found gives the typical frame size, the battery time in minutes, or video break-up as a function of distance and terrain.
- **Drop munitions.** VOG-25 and F-1 drone drops are not documented for these two battles. Wind drift on drops is not quantified anywhere found.
- **Fixed-wing over the chapter maps.** No source places Leleka, Furia or Shark over either chapter map on the chapter dates.
- **Data links.** Which link (Kropyva, Delta, Starlink, voice radio) carried drone data in the 72nd and the 47th on the chapter dates is not documented.
- **The Washington Post feature.** It was read through its Spokesman-Review republication because the original is paywalled.
- **Vampire timing.** The Vampire's first combat use is undated. dev.ua reports serial production in April 2023, but whether any flew at Robotyne in June 2023 is unknown.
- **The FPV hit series.** The monthly series comes from one OSINT analyst's count, published on a page labelled advertorial. It counts documented hits, not sorties.
