# The Kerch bridge (three acts)

8 Oct 2022 · 17 Jul 2023 · 3 Jun 2025, plus a 2026 coda. The Crimean (Kerch) Bridge across the Kerch Strait, between the Taman Peninsula and the Kerch Peninsula of occupied Crimea.

## 1. Summary and historical outcome

The Crimean Bridge is two parallel structures, a four-lane road bridge and a double-track rail bridge, about 19 km long. Russia built it after occupying Crimea in 2014, and it opened to cars in May 2018 and to trains in December 2019 [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge]. Ukraine hit it three times, each time by a different route: "The first time we approached it from land, the second time – from the water. In 2025, we approached it from underneath the water" [src:ssu-2025-maliuk-hallmark].

- **Act 1 (8 Oct 2022).** A truck carrying a concealed charge exploded at 06:07 on the Crimea-bound road lanes. It dropped road spans into the strait and set fire to fuel tank cars of a freight train on the rail bridge alongside [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion; isw-2022-10-08-roca]. The SBU was named by Ukrainian sources the same morning [src:pravda-2022-sbu-behind-bridge], and confirmed it in July and August 2023 [src:euromaidan-2023-shandra-sbu-confirms; militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details]. Two days later Putin called the 10 Oct missile and drone strike on Ukrainian energy and cities retaliation for the bridge [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca].
- **Act 2 (17 Jul 2023).** Two SBU "Sea Baby" surface drones, in a joint operation with the Navy, struck the bridge at night. One road span collapsed and an adjacent span was knocked loose [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby; isw-2023-07-17-roca].
- **Between Acts 2 and 3.** Russia surrounded the bridge with sunken ferries, barge lines, booms, nets, "hedgehogs", smoke and patrol craft. Storms broke the barrier more than once [src:twz-2023-altman-sinks-ships; navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses; espreso-2023-storm-barrier; unn-2025-bridge-protection-restored].
- **Act 3 (3 Jun 2025).** The SBU set off charges that its agents had placed at the underwater supports over several months. It went under the barrier rather than through it [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike].
- **Coda (2026).** The bridge still stands, but it carries no heavy trucks, and the Kerch Strait ferries that replaced it for dangerous cargo have been struck out of service [src:rusi-2026-ferris-logistics-targeting; kyivindependent-2026-pakhnyuk-ferry-75].

## 3. The diorama: geography, terrain, weather

**The structure.**
- A pair of separate, parallel bridges: a four-lane road (two two-lane carriageways) and a double-track, unelectrified railway worked by diesel locomotives [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge; wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion]. Total length is about 19 km [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge].
- Summed OSM bridge ways give about 16.8 km of road per carriageway and about 18.1 km of rail (computed 2026-10-01) [src:osm-overpass-2026-kerch-bridge].
- **Three sea sections:** Taman to Tuzla Spit, 7 km; across Tuzla Island, 6.5 km; Tuzla Island to Crimea, 5.5 km [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge].
- **The arch.** Steel arches over the Kerch–Yenikale shipping canal span 227 m, with 35 m clearance [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge]. OSM tags the navigation span as a truss of length 227 with a 185 m clearance width. Its centre is about **45.3086 N, 36.5060 E**, roughly 2.3 km from the Crimean landfall (computed) [src:osm-overpass-2026-kerch-bridge].
- **Foundations.** More than 7,000 piles, driven up to 90 m below the water, some raked for earthquakes, through about 60 m of silt over a faulted bedrock [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge].
- **Piers and spans.** Concrete piers carry the roadway and railway [src:reuters-2025-polityuk-underwater]. Spans are short, a few tens of metres: the 2022 replacement road span was 64 m [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion]. Piers are numbered, as in "support 145" in 2023 and "supports 235 and 236" in 2022 [src:twz-2023-altman-kerch-closed; pravda-2024-kommersant-rocket-fuel]. Cylindrical concrete fenders were reportedly built in front of the piers against ramming [src:ukranews-2022-how-bridge-guarded]. No source here gives the total pier count or a numbering origin.

**Water and approaches.**
- The strait is the only passage between the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. The arch's height limit forced some ships to cut their masts [src:wikipedia-crimean-bridge].
- Sea drones reach the bridge from the Black Sea side. The 2023 block-ship line was "about 15 miles north of where the mouth of the Kerch Strait meets the Black Sea, which is the route Ukrainian USVs have almost certainly taken" [src:twz-2023-altman-sinks-ships].
- The ferry route runs north of the bridge [src:maritimeexecutive-2026-rail-ferries].
- On the northern (Sea of Azov) side, a parallel structure with air-defence platforms was under construction in late 2024 [src:usm-2024-bridge-security].

**The land alternative.**
- The bridge is one of two southern ground lines of communication. The other runs through occupied Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca].
- After Act 1, columns moved Mariupol–Berdiansk–Melitopol [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca]. After Act 2, tourist traffic was rerouted through Dzhankoi and Armiansk toward Melitopol [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca].
- Russia built a rail line through the occupied coast [src:euromaidan-2024-rail-uk-mod; molfar-2024-bridge-satellite]. By 2026 that corridor was itself under drone attack: M-14 freight was down about 71%, and the Chonhar and North Crimean Canal crossings were cut [src:euromaidanpress-2026-crimea-island; kyivindependent-2026-moroziuk-crimea-rail-bridge; maritimeexecutive-2026-rail-ferries].

## 5. Systems and tactics in use

**The truck bomb (Act 1).**
- *Ukrainian account.* RDX in metal cylinders, 21 t TNT equivalent, wrapped in packing film. "The Security Service calculated the thickness of the film layer needed to hide the explosives from custom scanners." The cargo was declared as film rolls. The SBU bypassed the bridge's GPS jammers with "a technically sophisticated system" and set off the charge mid-bridge [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details].
- *Russian case file.* About 10 t TNT equivalent of solid rocket fuel with a hexogen-type initiator, fired by a GPS navigator at a set point (km 156) [src:pravda-2024-kommersant-rocket-fuel].
- *Counters.* An X-ray scanner existed at the entrance, but the truck was not scanned [src:meduza-2022-what-we-know-bridge]. Afterwards came heavier checks, FSB control, and bans on fuel and ammunition on the bridge [src:isw-2022-10-08-roca; militarnyi-2024-kushnikov-strengthening].

**Sea Baby (Act 2).**
- An SBU surface drone carrying up to 850 kg, with electro-optical and infrared cameras and a SATCOM dome [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby; navalnews-2023-ozberk-sea-baby-video]. The SBU also credited it with hitting the *Olenegorsky Gornyak* and the tanker *SIG* in Aug 2023 [src:ukrinform-2023-new-footage].
- Guided by an operator from a thermal view and driven at a pier at speed in darkness [src:navalnews-2023-ozberk-sea-baby-video].
- Two boats struck from opposite directions about five minutes apart [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby]. The blast "lifted and then dropped a section of the road bridge" [src:navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses].
- No floating or sunken barrier is reported before Aug 2023; only the claimed pier fenders, boats and divers of 2022 [src:ukranews-2022-how-bridge-guarded]. After the strike, milbloggers, Girkin among them, attacked the authorities for guarding the road checkpoint rather than the sea [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca].

**Underwater charges (Act 3).**
- Charges were placed at the supports over several months and fired starting at 04:44 [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike].
- The method is undisclosed. Russian milbloggers guessed a sea drone [src:reuters-2025-polityuk-underwater], and Euromaidan inferred underwater drones [src:euromaidan-2025-third-strike].
- The barrier system above was built against surface and underwater *drones*. It did not stop charges laid by agents.

**Cargo and traffic management (Russian counter-logistics).**
- After 2022, fuel and ammunition were barred from the bridge and moved by ferry and landing ship [src:militarnyi-2024-kushnikov-strengthening; navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses].
- Road weight limits were 3.5 t after the 2023 repair [src:molfar-2024-bridge-satellite] and 5 t in 2025 [src:ssu-2025-maliuk-hallmark]. The SBU in Nov 2023 also described reverse-flow road traffic and wagon limits [src:ssu-2023-bridge-doomed].
- Every closure brings manual checks, queues of thousands and a 200-litre liquids rule [src:pravda-2026-kerch-2500-vehicles].
- When the ferries were destroyed in 2026, dangerous rail cargo may have had to return to the bridge [src:maritimeexecutive-2026-rail-ferries].

## 6. Set pieces

1. **The Taman checkpoint (Act 1, ~06:00).** A truck with a load of film rolls bound for Simferopol pulls into inspection. A guard has the bay opened, glances in, and waves it on; the X-ray portal stands unused. Surveillance video later showed the driver "calm" [src:meduza-2022-what-we-know-bridge]. The player has set this up for months: route, paperwork, film thickness against the scanners [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details; pravda-2024-kommersant-rocket-fuel].
2. **06:07, the white flash.** A car passes the truck as the road climbs toward the arch. A blinding flash, all four lanes in flames, two spans dropping into the strait [src:meduza-2022-what-we-know-bridge; wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion]. On the rail bridge alongside, a fuel train burns, with the smoke driven seaward by a northerly to northeasterly wind in pre-dawn twilight [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion; openmeteo-era5-kerch-attack-days].
3. **The ferry queue.** By afternoon, light traffic crawls over the surviving carriageway while trucks wait days for a 16-truck ferry [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion; isw-2022-10-08-roca].
4. **10 October.** The cost: 84 cruise missiles and 24 drones across Ukraine, 19 dead [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca; ukrinform-2022-19-killed]. This plays as a scripted consequence, not a player choice.
5. **The thermal run (Act 2, 03:04).** The operator's screen shows a grey-white pier growing in a FLIR view. The first Sea Baby hits the road section; about five minutes later a second comes in from the other side toward the rail section [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby; navalnews-2023-ozberk-sea-baby-video]. A span lifts and drops [src:navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses].
6. **Smoke over the strait (12 Aug 2023).** Explosions in the afternoon, Russian claims of downed S-200s, and white smoke-generator plumes mixed with black smoke over the bridge [src:euromaidan-2023-shandra-bridge-smoke].
7. **The block-ship line.** Seven half-sunk ferries in a dotted line south of the bridge, with booms strung between them [src:twz-2023-altman-sinks-ships]. In late November a storm tears most of it apart [src:espreso-2023-storm-barrier].
8. **Running the barricades (2024).** An outer chain of barges, then booms and nets, sunken hedgehogs, a towable gate at the arch, a Pantsir on the deck and patrol boats [src:navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses; kyivpost-2024-brown-pantsir; ukrinform-2025-hedgehog-barriers]. This is the defence the player's drones have to beat or get around.
9. **04:44, beneath the waterline (Act 3).** A column of water erupts beside a pier in the SBU's video [src:reuters-2025-polityuk-underwater], two days after Spiderweb [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike].
10. **The emptied strait (2026 coda).** The last rail ferry burns at sea. Ferries are towed as barges. Ten-kilometre queues form at the approaches, and smoke vehicles stand on the deck [src:maritimeexecutive-2026-rail-ferries; kyivindependent-2026-pakhnyuk-ferry-75; pravda-2026-kerch-2500-vehicles].

## 7. Decision points

- **Rail or road (SBU, spring–summer 2022).** The SBU first considered freight cars on the railway. Russia then restricted non-military cargo on the rail bridge, and the SBU switched to a truck [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details].
- **Concealment and routing (SBU).** The SBU chose film-roll cover sized against scanners, and a long indirect route. Per Russian investigators the route ran Odesa → Ruse → Poti → Yerevan → Verkhny Lars → Armavir, using unwitting carriers. Malyuk said: "we used so many people without their knowledge" [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details; pravda-2024-kommersant-rocket-fuel; kyivpost-2025-life-sentences].
- **Russian checkpoint choice.** The truck was inspected by hand but not scanned on the X-ray at the entrance [src:meduza-2022-what-we-know-bridge].
- **Russian retaliation (Putin, 8–10 Oct 2022).** Milbloggers demanded retaliation [src:isw-2022-10-09-roca]. Putin presented the 10 Oct infrastructure strikes as retaliation. GUR said they had been planned earlier, and ISW argued the use of scarce precision weapons might cost options elsewhere [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca]. The same pattern followed Act 2, with the Odesa port strikes of 18 Jul 2023 [src:pravda-2023-peskov-odesa-revenge].
- **Silence vs acknowledgement (Ukraine).** Ukraine was coy in 2022 [src:ssu-2022-maliuk-far-from-reality], then claimed both attacks in summer 2023. CNN describes Malyuk's direct claim as "an unusually direct bid to alert Moscow" to the drone threat [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby; euromaidan-2023-shandra-sbu-confirms]. Neizhpapa stressed "information silence" before Act 3 [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike].
- **Timing Act 2 in the counteroffensive.** ISW judged the strike would hit southern logistics "in the midst of the ongoing Ukrainian counteroffensive" [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca].
- **Russian defence posture.** After Act 2, Russia chose physical barriers: sunken ferries, then barges, booms, nets and hedgehogs. It put up with storm losses and rebuilt repeatedly [src:kyivindependent-2023-court-hur-ferries; navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses; unn-2025-bridge-protection-restored].
- **Russian cargo routing.** Russia moved dangerous cargo off the bridge onto ferries and ships [src:navalnews-2024-sutton-kerch-defenses], and built the land railway as "redundancy" [src:euromaidan-2024-rail-uk-mod]. Once the ferries were destroyed, the bridge again became "the critical remaining link" [src:maritimeexecutive-2026-rail-ferries].
- **Is another strike worth it? (debated, 2024).** Molfar argued in May 2024 that attacking the bridge was no longer needed, since military rail traffic had stopped [src:molfar-2024-bridge-satellite]. The SBU said "the bridge is doomed" [src:ssu-2023-bridge-doomed], and the Navy said it remained a target [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike]. Ukraine struck again in 2025.
- **Under, not through (SBU, 2025).** Ukraine went under the surface barrier with charges placed over months [src:pravda-2025-prots-third-strike; liga-2025-malyuk-two-tons]. The sources do not explain the choice.

## 9. Russia's side as reported

- **Attribution and framing.** Russia called each attack terrorism against civilian infrastructure. Putin said Ukrainian special services "devised, carried out and ordered" Act 1 [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion]. Russia blamed HUR and Budanov, and in Apr 2023 a Moscow court issued an arrest warrant for Budanov [src:pravda-2022-fsb-detains-eight; wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion].
- **Investigation (Act 1).** In the Russian version the charge was about 10 t TNT equivalent of solid rocket fuel in 22.7 t of film reels, triggered by GPS at km 156 [src:pravda-2024-kommersant-rocket-fuel]. Twelve people were identified and eight detained in Oct 2022 [src:pravda-2022-fsb-detains-eight]. Eight were jailed for life in Nov 2025 after a closed trial; all denied knowing what the cargo was [src:kyivpost-2025-life-sentences]. Malyuk called the 22 people Russia jailed "ordinary Russian smugglers" [src:militarnyi-2023-kushnikov-malyuk-details].
- **Damage minimised.** Meduza reported a Kremlin guide to the media on playing down the 2022 damage [src:isw-2022-10-08-roca]. The Ministry of Transport claimed the 2023 strike left the rail bridge and road supports intact [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca]. After Act 3, Peskov said "no damage" [src:kyivindependent-2025-fornusek-kremlin-no-damage].
- **Interception claims.** Russia claimed:
  - S-200s downed on 12 Aug 2023, with the smoke presented as a deliberate screen [src:euromaidan-2023-shandra-bridge-smoke; pravda-2023-bridge-smoke-air-defence];
  - three USVs destroyed on 1–2 Sep 2023 [src:aljazeera-2023-sea-drones-bridge];
  - 12 ATACMS and five USVs (by helicopter) on 15 Aug 2024 [src:rferl-2024-kerch-atacms-claim];
  - a drone shot down over the bridge on 3 Jun 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-fornusek-underwater].

  None was independently verified.
- **Internal criticism.** In 2022 nationalist milbloggers criticised Putin and Medvedev's silence after Medvedev's "red line" threats [src:isw-2022-10-09-roca]. In 2023 they faulted the neglect of maritime security in favour of checking cars [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca].
- **Retaliation framing.** Russia presented the 10 Oct 2022 missile strike and the 18 Jul 2023 Odesa strike as retaliation [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca; pravda-2023-peskov-odesa-revenge].

## 10. Game/sim relevance

- **Three acts, three verbs.** Act 1 is a *logistics-and-deception board*, like Spiderweb's long game: route, paperwork, scanner-defeating packaging and an unwitting driver. It ends in one timing decision on the bridge. Act 2 is a *night USV run*: thermal view, two boats, two directions. Act 3 is *covert placement over months*, then a single command.
- **"Running the barricades" must track the real build-up.** No floating or sunken barrier is reported before Aug 2023, and Russian critics blamed neglected maritime security for Act 2 [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca; ukranews-2022-how-bridge-guarded]. The full layered system (§5 table) only existed from 2024. Act 2 should not face 2024 defences. The barrier layer is best played as the *what-if pressure between acts*: the Aug–Sep 2023 attempts and the Aug 2024 claims. In Act 3 the barrier sits on the surface and the charges go beneath it.
- **Storms are an ally.** Barrier integrity degrades with weather. Real storms flooded, scattered or set adrift all seven boom barges in Nov 2023 and most of 36 in late 2024 [src:espreso-2023-storm-barrier; unn-2025-bridge-protection-restored]. Russia rebuilds over weeks.
- **Win bands:**
  - Act 1: 2–3 road spans down plus a fuel-train fire, and the bridge partly open the same day [src:wikipedia-2022-crimean-bridge-explosion; isw-2022-10-08-roca].
  - Act 2: 1 span down and 1 displaced, rail open within hours, full repair in about three months [src:isw-2023-07-17-roca; meduza-2023-bridge-repaired].
  - Act 3: an explosion at the supports and a closure of hours. The damage band runs from "no damage" (Kremlin) to "emergency condition" with a ≤5 t limit afterwards (SBU) [src:kyivindependent-2025-fornusek-kremlin-no-damage; pravda-2025-prots-third-strike; ssu-2025-maliuk-hallmark].
  - Over-performing, by destroying the bridge, should not count as the historical win.
- **Consequence scripting.** After Act 1, a scripted Russian missile wave hits Ukrainian cities [src:isw-2022-10-10-roca]. After Act 2, the Odesa strikes and the end of the grain deal [src:pravda-2023-peskov-odesa-revenge; isw-2023-07-17-roca]. After every act, Russian logistics shift: ferry queues, the land corridor, weight limits.
- **The real payoff is logistics, not rubble.** Show the diversion: trucks to ferries (2022), military rail falling to 4–5 trains a day (2024), then ferries destroyed and a ≤5 t bridge (2025–26) [src:molfar-2024-bridge-satellite; ssu-2025-maliuk-hallmark; rusi-2026-ferris-logistics-targeting]. The coda can be a logistics overlay that ties into the 2026 "island Crimea" campaign [src:euromaidanpress-2026-crimea-island].
- **Pacing.** Act 1's prep runs for months, compressed, and its strike lasts seconds. Act 2 is two impacts minutes apart: about 5 minutes on the SBU's CCTV, 16 minutes between the explosions reported by Russian channels (03:04 and 03:20) [src:cnn-2023-walsh-sea-baby; twz-2023-altman-kerch-closed]. Act 3's placement is undisclosed: the dossier has no detail, so do not invent a mini-game the sources cannot support.

## 11. Open questions/gaps

- **Pier geometry.** The total number of piers, the pier numbering origin, typical span lengths per section (only the 64 m replacement span is sourced) and pier coordinates for supports 145 and 235–236 are not in any source used. A figure of 595 supports appeared only in search snippets and could not be tied to an acceptable source, so it was not used.
- **Barrier dimensions.** Boom heights, net mesh, mine positions and the exact standoff distance from the bridge are unpublished. The spacing of the 2023 block ships is given as 160 m (UK DI), just over 0.1 mi (TWZ) or about 200 m (Militarnyi). Their direction is given as "south" (TWZ, UK DI) or "western approaches" (Sutton's post).
- **Barge counts** swing with storms and reporting: 7 sections (May 2024), 21 in a second line (Jun), 30+ (Jul), 36 (Oct), 17 (Jan 2025). No clean time series.
- **Act 1 charge.** The SBU says 21 t TNT equivalent of RDX; the Russian case file says about 10 t TNT equivalent of rocket fuel in 22.7 t of cargo. Spans down: 2 (case file) or 3 damaged with 2 down (Wikipedia). The explosion site is described three ways (§3).
- **Act 2 launch point, route, range and endurance** of the Sea Babies are not disclosed. Neither is whether the CNN "road" and "rail" impacts struck the same pier group.
- **Act 3 damage** is not independently verified. Reuters confirmed only the location [src:reuters-2025-polityuk-underwater]. The delivery method (divers, UUV, agents) is unknown, and Malyuk's "two × 1,100 kg" adds to the initial "1,100 kg".
- **Unverified Russian claims:** whether the Aug–Sep 2023 and Aug 2024 attacks on the bridge happened as Russia described them.
- **Not researched (search budget exhausted):**
  - the 2024 German Taurus debate over the bridge;
  - Russian rail-bridge load limits after 2022;
  - the Ukrainian Navy's role in planning Act 3;
  - the UK MoD's original wording on repair estimates (cited here via Wikipedia).
- **Weather** comes from ERA5 reanalysis, not station observations, and gives no sea state or visibility.
