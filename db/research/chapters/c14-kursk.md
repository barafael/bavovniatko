# The Kursk incursion and the North Korean defence
6 Aug 2024 – late Apr 2025, with a coda to Jun 2025. Kursk Oblast (Russia): Sudzha, Korenevo and Glushkovo raions, plus the Sumy Oblast border strip (Yunakivka–Basivka–Novenke) the salient was supplied through.

## 1. Summary and historical outcome

Before dawn on 6 August 2024 Ukrainian regular brigades crossed the border into Kursk Oblast. Preparation had been long: special forces had worked in the Russian rear since June, and a company of the 80th Air Assault Brigade had hidden in border forests since 4 August. Russian communications and surveillance were suppressed beforehand, and the build-up in Sumy Oblast passed as a defensive deployment [src:isw-2025-02-06-roca; src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-80th-crossed-early; src:bbc-2024-waterhouse-entered-easily; src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].

The 80th and 82nd Air Assault and 22nd Mechanised brigades broke a thin line of conscripts, FSB border guards and Akhmat units on two axes, from the Sudzha checkpoint and from Novenke [src:osw-2024-wilk-kursk-action; src:isw-2024-08-06-roca; src:isw-2024-08-09-roca]. Within a day they held the Sudzha checkpoint and, by Russian accounts, the Sudzha gas distribution (metering) station [src:isw-2024-08-07-roca]. By 14–15 August they held the town of Sudzha [src:kyivindependent-2024-fornusek-military-administration; src:kyivindependent-2024-hodunova-sudzha-captured].

From 16 to 19 August air strikes and HIMARS dropped the three Seim road bridges in Glushkovo Raion. That isolated the Russian troops between the river and the border [src:isw-2024-08-16-roca; src:isw-2024-08-19-roca; src:militarnyi-2024-kursk-crossings-osa]. The advance peaked in late August at a claimed 1,250–1,300 km², then turned into a positional defence [src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-92-settlements; src:isw-2024-08-28-roca].

Russia counterattacked from 10 September. From November about 11,000–12,000 North Korean troops joined it, attacking on foot in large groups across open, snowy fields [src:isw-2024-09-11-roca; src:isw-2024-11-13-roca; src:isw-2025-01-11-roca]. The salient shrank to about 600 km² in November and about 400 km² in late February [src:pravda-2024-kyrylenko-horets-95th; src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-more-nk-deployed]. Its single supply road from Sumy to Sudzha came under Russian fibre-optic FPV fire [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit; src:rferl-2025-rubicon; src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk].

On 5–6 March 2025, as the US paused intelligence sharing (5 March), Russian forces broke through south of Sudzha. On 8 March about a hundred of them came out of a disused gas pipeline inside the town [src:pravda-2025-kyrylenko-breach-south-sudzha; src:isw-2025-03-09-roca; src:twz-2025-newdick-kursk-pipeline]. Ukraine withdrew from Sudzha on 10–12 March, and Russian troops raised flags in the town on 12 March. On 16 March the Ukrainian General Staff's maps confirmed the loss [src:isw-2025-03-12-roca; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive; src:kyivindependent-2025-sudzha-withdrawal-confirmed].

Ukraine kept slivers of the border: about 30 km² on 21 April and about 90 km² by Syrskyi's count on 22 June 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-zadorozhnyy-syrskyi-april; src:bbc-2025-mackintosh-90-sq-km]. Russia declared Kursk Oblast fully retaken on 26 April and thanked North Korea for the first time [src:isw-2025-04-26-roca; src:bbc-2025-somerville-russia-claims-kursk].

## 3. The diorama: geography, terrain, weather

**The box.** The map runs from about 51.0 to 51.5 N and 34.3 to 35.6 E, roughly 90 by 55 km [src:osm-overpass-2026-c14-places].
- **West:** the Seim at Glushkovo and Tyotkino.
- **East:** the Psel valley east of Sudzha and Plekhovo.
- **North:** Korenevo and the Malaya Loknya–Pogrebki line.
- **South:** the Sumy Oblast border villages, with Sumy city (about 45 km from Sudzha) as the rear base.

**The map's logic.**
- **One road in.** The salient hangs on one road: the H-07 from Sumy through Yunakivka to the Sudzha border crossing, which becomes the 38K-004 / R-200 in Russia [src:isw-2024-08-06-roca; src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit; src:osm-overpass-2026-c14-places]. Everyone knew Russia would try to cut it, and from January 2025 it did, with drones and then by ground [src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk; src:pravda-2025-kyrylenko-breach-south-sudzha].
- **The Seim as a moat.** The Seim forms the western boundary. The three road bridges in Glushkovo Raion are its only crossings [src:militarnyi-2024-kursk-crossings-osa]. North of the river the ground rises, which Euromaidan's analysts judged too hard to take [src:euromaidan-2024-seym-bridges-cauldron].
- **The Psel and the Sudzha river.** The Psel runs through the east of the salient; Russia's March push took "the entire eastern bank of the Psyol" [src:euromaidan-2025-martyniuk-218-days]. Inside Sudzha, Russian troops first held the east bank of the Sudzha river [src:isw-2025-03-09-roca].
- **The ground.** Villages, steppe fields and woodland belts, much like the Donbas according to a 95th Brigade officer [src:pravda-2024-kyrylenko-horets-95th]. Kofman: "the terrain was challenging … the salient by its nature meant the geometry of the fight was disadvantageous" [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].

## 4. Forces

**Ukraine (the player).**
- **Command.**
  - Commander-in-Chief Col. Gen. Oleksandr Syrskyi planned the operation in a tight circle [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
  - Commanders included Maj. Gen. Eduard Moskaliov (military commandant of the held area), Pavlo Rozlach (80th Air Assault) and Dmytro Voloshyn (82nd Air Assault) [src:isw-2024-08-15-roca; src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-80th-crossed-early; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- **The breakthrough echelon (6 Aug).**
  - The 80th and 82nd Air Assault brigades led the break-in [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
  - OSW identified the 22nd Mechanised and 82nd Air Assault (on Strykers) and put the whole grouping at about 2,000, including artillery and air defence elements [src:osw-2024-wilk-kursk-action].
  - Mick Ryan noted experienced formations, highly mobile and well covered by air defence [src:kyivpost-2024-ryan-battle-of-kursk].

- **The follow-on echelon.**

  - **Sep–Oct:** the 116th Mechanised (Novy Put) and the 47th Mechanised, with its 33rd Assault and 130th battalions, on Abrams, Bradleys and BMP-2s, supported by 21st Mechanised artillery (late October) [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive; src:militarnyi-2024-47th-brigade-kursk].

It is a checklist, not individually verified [src:wikipedia-kursk-campaign-order-of-battle].

## 5. Systems and tactics in use

- **Deception and the quiet build-up.**
  - Ukraine disguised its massing as defence against an expected Russian attack on Sumy and kept planning secret [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
  - Russian units had in fact been warned since February, but the warnings went unheeded [src:guardian-2024-walker-seized-papers; src:isw-2024-08-07-roca].
- **Electronic warfare first, then armour.**
  - Drone units spent two days blinding Russian surveillance and communications [src:bbc-2024-waterhouse-entered-easily]. Disrupted Russian comms disoriented the defenders [src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-80th-crossed-early].
  - Ukraine captured a Volnorez jammer with its documents in August [src:militarnyi-2024-kushnikov-volnorez].
  - Starlink did not work inside Russia at first, so radio and satellite modems filled the gap [src:pravda-2024-kyrylenko-horets-95th; src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
- **Mobile columns that bypass.** Spaced armoured convoys were ordered to keep moving and not be delayed by resistance. FPVs broke up counterattacks as they formed [src:politico-2024-dettmer-urged-not-to-invade].
- **The anti-air umbrella.** An SBU FPV hit a Mi-28, a Ka-52 was downed, and analysts noted few glide bombs in the first days [src:kyivindependent-2024-fornusek-mi28-fpv; src:isw-2024-08-07-roca; src:kyivpost-2024-ryan-battle-of-kursk].
- **Deep fires against movement.** The HIMARS strike on the Oktyabrskoye convoy hit Russians moving in columns 30 km from the border [src:kyivpost-2024-oktyabrskoye-himars].
- **Bridge interdiction.**
  - Bridges were dropped one at a time over four days: HIMARS (Glushkovo), then MiG-29s with JDAM-ER and SDB (Zvannoye and the pontoons) [src:isw-2024-08-18-roca; src:euromaidan-2024-seym-bridges-cauldron; src:militarnyi-2024-kursk-crossings-osa].
  - FIRMS thermal data and satellite imagery confirmed the pontoon kills [src:euromaidan-2024-seym-bridges-cauldron].
- **North Korean massed infantry.**
  - Company- and battalion-sized foot assaults with Russian fire and EW support. They ignored losses and drones and did not evacuate the wounded [src:cnn-2024-mezzofiore-nk-heavy-casualties; src:isw-2025-01-11-roca; src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
  - Kirby: "massed dismounted assaults" without armour [src:isw-2024-12-27-roca].
  - They were very vulnerable to FPVs at first [src:bbc-2025-gardner-1000-nk-killed].
- **North Korean adaptation.**
  - After losing about a quarter of their strength they shifted to Russian-style small groups of 5–8 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year; src:kyivpost-2025-nk-tactics-kursk].
  - A drone-baiting drill used one soldier as bait about 7 m out and shooters at 10–12 m [src:kyivpost-2025-nk-tactics-kursk].
  - They were good at shooting drones down at about 100 m [src:cnn-2025-nk-pulled-back], and later fielded FPV teams and mobile ATGM crews [src:frontelligence-2025-nk-lessons].
  - The NIS judged their combat capability "significantly improved" with drones by April [src:euromaidan-2025-tril-nis-4700].
  - Coordination with the Russians was poor: they would take a position and leave before Russian infantry consolidated it [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
- **Ukrainian counters to the waves.** Drones and massed fires [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. A feigned retreat by the 225th lured attackers into a HIMARS cluster strike near Sverdlikovo, per Forbes via Wikipedia [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- **The fibre-optic interdiction of the road.**
  - Russia's KVN fibre FPV appeared first in Kursk [src:kyivindependent-2025-farrell-fiber-optic].
  - Rubikon "systematically cut" the Sudzha supply lines [src:rferl-2025-rubicon].
  - By March, two or three drones a minute were over the Sudzha–Sumy highway, movement by day was impossible, and wrecks blocked the road [src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk].
  - In e7, visibility was full to 3 km and faded to 15 km, with tasking out to 40 km [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Pipeline infiltration.** About 15 km through the idle Sudzha pipe. Infiltrators wore blue tape to pass as Ukrainians (Alaudinov), which ISW said may be perfidy. Some suffered lung damage [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive; src:kyivindependent-2025-zadorozhnyy-pipeline-tunnels].
- **Intelligence dependence.** During the US pause (5–11 Mar) ISW saw no HIMARS or ATACMS support in Kursk. Ukrainian sources said they lost early warning of aircraft [src:isw-2025-03-08-roca].

## 6. Set pieces

1. **Dawn at the Sudzha checkpoint (6 Aug).**
   - A company hidden in the woods since 4 Aug takes the border stronghold.
   - The armoured columns, marked with white triangles, roll past the wrecked customs post towards Sudzha and Oleshnya. A drone unit has blinded the Russian sensors [src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-80th-crossed-early; src:bbc-2024-waterhouse-entered-easily; src:isw-2024-08-06-roca].
   - Overhead, an SBU FPV clips a Mi-28's tail rotor [src:kyivindependent-2024-fornusek-mi28-fpv].
2. **The gas station.** The Sudzha gas distribution station, 500 m inside Russia, changes hands on day 2. Gas keeps flowing to Europe at about 42 million m³ a day [src:isw-2024-08-07-roca; src:aljazeera-2024-duggal-mapping-kursk].
3. **The Oktyabrskoye column (night 8–9 Aug).** Fourteen covered Ural and KAMAZ trucks carrying reserves burn on the 38K-017 east of Rylsk [src:isw-2024-08-09-roca; src:kyivpost-2024-oktyabrskoye-himars].
4. **Flags and prisoners (12–14 Aug).** The 225th pulls the flag in Darino, and Ukrainian state TV shows the flag coming down in Sudzha. More than 100 Russians, many of them conscripts, surrender in a day [src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-225th-darino; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive; src:kyivindependent-2024-100-pows-syrskyi].
5. **Breaking the Seim (16–20 Aug).** The Glushkovo span collapses, the Zvannoye deck is holed, and Karyzh goes last. Drones then hunt the pontoon trucks 3 km north of Glushkovo [src:isw-2024-08-16-roca; src:isw-2024-08-18-roca; src:isw-2024-08-19-roca; src:isw-2024-08-20-roca].
6. **The siege of Malaya Loknya (Aug).** The 95th encircles the village and clears it house by house over about two weeks. More than 100 Russians hold basements and a women's prison turned fortress [src:pravda-2024-kyrylenko-horets-95th; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
7. **North Koreans in the snow (Dec 2024 – Jan 2025).**
   - Lines of infantry cross icy fields near Plekhovo under Russian fire support, and bodies are left where they fall [src:cnn-2024-mezzofiore-nk-heavy-casualties].
   - An SSO detachment holds for eight hours, then pulls out in two Humvees with a third of its ammunition [src:kyivindependent-2025-gyori-sso-21-nk].
8. **The prisoners (9 Jan 2025).** Two wounded North Koreans are taken alive, one carrying a Tuvan registration card. They tell interrogators they thought they were going to training [src:isw-2025-01-11-roca; src:euromaidan-2025-kravchuk-nk-pow-ferry]. By February one asks to go to South Korea, which says it will take them. Kyiv weighs a transfer against a swap [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-nk-pow-asylum].
9. **The road of death (Feb – Mar 2025).** One highway, drones "around the clock", and burnt vehicles choking the lanes. Men walk out 15–20 km at night [src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk].
10. **Out of the pipe (8 Mar 2025).** About a hundred men crawl 15 km through the dead gas main and surface in Sudzha's industrial zone. Air assault troops film the exit and call in artillery and HIMARS [src:twz-2025-newdick-kursk-pipeline; src:militarnyi-2025-kushnikov-sudzha-pipeline; src:kyivindependent-2025-denisova-pipeline-video].
11. **Putin at the command post (12 Mar).** Putin, in uniform, hears Gerasimov the day Sudzha falls [src:isw-2025-03-12-roca].

## 7. Decision points

- **Whether to invade at all (Jun – Jul 2024).**
  - Syrskyi says intelligence in May showed a Russian plan to strike Sumy, so Kyiv chose a preventive offensive [src:ukrinform-2024-syrskyi-20000-losses].
  - Zaluzhnyi reportedly objected that there was no clear second step ("once you have the bridgehead, what then?"). The 80th's then commander, Emil Ishkulov, reportedly feared exposure and casualties [src:politico-2024-dettmer-urged-not-to-invade].
  - Allies were not told in advance [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- **Russia's first response (8–15 Aug).**
  - ISW set out four Russian options, from using only border forces to pulling operational reserves [src:isw-2024-08-08-roca].
  - Moscow chose conscripts, border units and lateral moves while keeping the Donetsk offensive going. It chose a "counterterrorism operation" over martial law [src:isw-2024-08-09-roca; src:isw-2024-08-10-roca; src:isw-2024-08-15-roca].
- **How to cut the Seim (16–19 Aug).**
  - The bridges were dropped one by one, not all at once. Euromaidan's analysts read this as giving Russian troops a window to leave the pocket and so keep Ukrainian losses down. The alternative was a full encirclement [src:euromaidan-2024-seym-bridges-cauldron].
  - The 700 km² Glushkovo pocket was never cleared [src:isw-2024-10-23-roca].
- **Raid or hold (late Aug).**
  - By 28 Aug Ukrainian units were digging in [src:isw-2024-08-28-roca]. The leadership halted to resupply and hold the bridgehead [src:politico-2024-dettmer-urged-not-to-invade].
  - Analysts later argued that a weeks-long raid would have been better than a grinding defence. Cooper called holding without supply "absolutely nonsensical" [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
- **Russia commits North Koreans (Oct – Nov).** US officials assessed that the idea came from Pyongyang [src:isw-2024-12-23-roca]. The decision let Russia avoid stripping Donetsk [src:isw-2025-02-06-roca].
- **Counterattack or withdraw (Jan – Feb 2025).**
  - Kyiv launched spoiling attacks on 5 Jan and 6 Feb [src:isw-2025-01-05-roca; src:isw-2025-02-06-roca].
  - Janovsky (Oryx): "around early February, it stopped being viable for Ukraine to stay". Kofman: the pocket was steadily compressed until fibre-optic drones could interdict the supply routes [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
  - A withdrawal a month earlier would have saved scarce US-made equipment [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
- **The US intelligence pause (5–11 Mar).** ISW called the timing "noteworthy" but the link "unclear" [src:isw-2025-03-09-roca]. A Ukrainian government source said the pause hit Kursk hardest [src:isw-2025-03-08-roca; src:aljazeera-2025-psaropoulos-kursk-us-cutoff].
- **The withdrawal (10–12 Mar).**
  - Syrskyi ordered units to "more favourable" lines, with saving soldiers' lives as his stated priority [src:isw-2025-03-13-roca; src:kyivindependent-2025-sudzha-withdrawal-confirmed].
  - Soldiers say the cut of the road "again came as a surprise to our command" [src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk].
  - Matveev notes Russia followed the retreat rather than trying to encircle [src:euromaidan-2025-martyniuk-218-days].
  - Ukraine then opened a diversion into Belgorod Oblast (18 Mar) [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- **The political purpose and its cost.**
  - **Stated goals:**
    - prevent a Sumy offensive and create a buffer zone [src:isw-2025-02-06-roca; src:bbc-2025-somerville-russia-claims-kursk];
    - draw Russian forces from the Donbas [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit];
    - fill the POW "exchange fund" [src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-92-settlements];
    - force Russia into "fair" talks [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
  - **The bargaining chip:** later, Kursk as leverage. Zelensky said: "We will exchange one territory for another" [src:euromaidan-2025-kirichenko-troops-split-kursk; src:kyivindependent-2024-york-atacms-kursk].
  - **Critics:**
    - Rob Lee and Kofman argued it drained the reserves defending Pokrovsk [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
    - Russia's troops came mostly from reserves and quieter fronts, not Donetsk [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit; src:isw-2024-08-15-roca].
    - The Atlantic Council found Ukraine lost 25% more vehicles than Russia [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-sutea-fiber-optic].
  - **Defenders:**
    - Reynolds (RUSI) said the pocket was held "for a remarkable amount of time" and had "a shaping effect on Russian thinking" [src:guardian-2025-sabbagh-border-sumy].
    - ISW said it pinned VDV and naval infantry for months [src:isw-2025-04-26-roca].
    - Kofman called it tactically successful, but its purpose at the operational and strategic level was more doubtful [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].

## 9. Russia's side as reported

- **Denial, then "halted".**
  - On 6 Aug the MoD and FSB claimed that raids by about a battalion of armour had been repelled. Milbloggers mocked it as a "media stunt" [src:isw-2024-08-06-roca; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
  - On 7 Aug Gerasimov told Putin about 1,000 attackers had been stopped. Putin called it a "large-scale provocation" [src:isw-2024-08-07-roca].
  - Milbloggers raged that the build-up had been seen for months [src:isw-2024-08-07-roca]. They called the column move to Oktyabrskoye "criminal incompetence" [src:kyivpost-2024-oktyabrskoye-himars].
- **Framing.**
  - A "counterterrorism operation", not war, to avoid panic [src:isw-2024-08-10-roca].
  - Putin publicly chastised officials and shifted the blame [src:isw-2024-08-12-roca].
  - Zakharova blamed US HIMARS for the Glushkovo bridge and for killing evacuation volunteers [src:aljazeera-2024-seym-bridge-western-weapons].
- **Silence about North Koreans.** Russian officials did not acknowledge them until Gerasimov thanked them on 26 Apr 2025 [src:isw-2024-12-17-roca; src:isw-2025-04-26-roca]. Pyongyang confirmed the next day [src:euromaidan-2025-tril-nk-acknowledges].
- **Encirclement claims.**
  - Putin claimed 2,000 Ukrainians were "encircled" in October; ISW never saw evidence [src:isw-2025-03-14-roca; src:kyivindependent-2024-denisova-17800-casualties].
  - In March 2025 he claimed Ukrainians were "blocked" and offered surrender terms in reply to Trump. Milbloggers' own maps showed exits into Sumy Oblast [src:isw-2025-03-14-roca].
  - Medvedev: "The lid of the smoking cauldron is practically closed" [src:cnn-2025-edwards-russia-advancing-kursk].
- **Victory narrative.**
  - Gerasimov: Gornal "liberated" and 76,000 Ukrainian casualties. Putin: "The Kyiv regime's adventure has completely failed" [src:bbc-2025-somerville-russia-claims-kursk].
  - Russia accused Ukraine of shelling the Sudzha gas station on 21 Mar [src:euromaidan-2025-tril-sudzha-gas-station].
  - A section of the pipe went on show in a 2025 exhibition celebrating the operation [src:ukrpravda-2026-kyrylenko-kupiansk-is-yours].
- **Internal strain.**
  - Conscripts were captured in large numbers [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
  - Captured papers show suicides and morale orders in the 488th Regiment [src:guardian-2024-walker-seized-papers].
  - Alaudinov called for conscripts to fight and dismissed their families' concerns [src:isw-2024-08-19-roca].

## 10. Game/sim relevance

- **Two-phase chapter.**
  - **Phase 1 is a manoeuvre sandbox** (6–28 Aug): speed, bypass, prisoners and bridge interdiction. Its scripted cap is the historical 1,000–1,300 km² claimed, with 800 km² as the confirmed floor [src:isw-2024-08-12-roca; src:isw-2024-08-20-roca].
  - **Phase 2 is a shrinking-perimeter defence** (Sep – Mar). The win condition is to hold Sudzha until about 10–12 Mar 2025, then withdraw without a large encirclement [src:isw-2025-03-14-roca].
- **The supply road is the clock.**
  - Model the H-07 as a single logistics edge with a rising interdiction meter. Drivers are Russian drone density, fibre-optic FPVs immune to EW, and ground approaches (Novenke from the north in late February, Kurilovka from the south on 5–6 March) [src:pravda-2025-kyrylenko-breach-south-sudzha; src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk].
  - When the meter peaks, by-day movement stops and the player must move on foot at night.
- **Bridge staging.** Let the player choose between sequential and simultaneous Seim strikes. The historical choice gave Russians a window to leave [src:euromaidan-2024-seym-bridges-cauldron].
- **The North Korean AI behaviour switch.** Phase A (Dec – Jan) is massed waves, high casualties and poor coordination with Russian follow-on forces. Then a pause and retraining. Phase B (Feb – Mar) is small groups, drone-shooting and coherence [src:cnn-2025-nk-pulled-back; src:euromaidan-2025-tril-nis-4700; src:isw-2025-03-09-roca].
- **Prisoner economy.** The POW count (594 → 983) feeds an exchange meter [src:pravda-2025-tyshchenko-67000-losses]. Two North Korean prisoners are a scripted bonus event (9 Jan).
- **Scripted global events.**
  - The US intelligence pause (5–11 Mar) removes HIMARS targeting and air-raid warning [src:isw-2025-03-08-roca].
  - ATACMS permission (19 Nov) and Storm Shadow strikes add deep-strike options [src:wikipedia-atacms; src:wikipedia-storm-shadow].
- **Debrief bands.**
  - Peak area of 800–1,300 km².
  - North Korean casualties of 2,304 killed to 7,000+ total.
  - Russian losses of 62,400 (Ukrainian claim).
  - Equipment of 790 against 740.
  - All of these are shown with claimants [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit; src:pravda-2025-tyshchenko-67000-losses].
- **Weather pacing.** Summer dry ground for the dash; a November mud dip; snow in Dec – Feb, which exposes infantry to drones; overcast days that ground drones (6 Feb); the March thaw [src:openmeteo-2026-c14-sudzha-weather; src:isw-2025-02-06-roca].

## 11. Open questions/gaps

- **Peak area actually controlled.** No independent final figure. ISW's 800 km² is *claimed* extent as of 12 Aug. Ukrainian numbers (1,000–1,300 km²) are unverified. DeepState's history API excludes Russian territory [src:isw-2024-08-12-roca; src:deepstate-api-history].
- **Ukrainian strength and losses.** The force estimates range from 2,000 at the start (OSW) to 12,000 (Western) to "at most 30,000" (ISW). No official casualty figures exist; only UALosses via Wikipedia [src:osw-2024-wilk-kursk-action; src:bbc-2025-beale-retreat-from-kursk; src:cnn-2025-edwards-russia-advancing-kursk; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- **The full Ukrainian order of battle.** Zelensky's 6 Feb 2025 award decree reportedly listed the units [src:isw-2025-02-06-roca], but the decree was not retrieved. The Wikipedia order of battle is unverified unit by unit.
- **Gas station timeline.** The 7 Aug seizure rests on Russian sources via ISW. The date and terms of Ukraine's control are unclear, as is any damage before the 21 Mar 2025 fire. No OSM coordinate was found.
- **The US intelligence pause.** Its causal weight is disputed: ISW calls it a correlation; some Ukrainian sources call it key [src:isw-2025-03-09-roca; src:aljazeera-2025-psaropoulos-kursk-us-cutoff].
- **The pipeline force.** About 100 (Ukrainska Pravda) against "over 600" (Gerasimov). One unverified claim says 80% were killed at the exit [src:twz-2025-newdick-kursk-pipeline; src:nv-2025-romanenko-pipeline-ambush].
- **North Korean totals.** Casualty figures vary threefold. The total deployment to Kursk (12k + 1–3k) is not reconciled with the 16–22k whole-war figure.
- **Paywalled or blocked sources not used directly:** Forbes (Axe), Reuters, the Telegraph, WSJ and the FT. Their claims appear only via Wikipedia, where noted.
- **The Oktyabrskoye toll.** Estimates of up to 200–500 casualties are analysts' estimates, unverified [src:kyivpost-2024-oktyabrskoye-himars; src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
