# Next: the winter, and the same field in summer

*A plan, written 2026-10-11, after the first diorama.*

> The road is left clear. The fields beside it are mined. Tomorrow they come down it again.

---

## Where we stand

One day exists: Vuhledar, 6 February 2023.
- The day runs by itself. Four columns come down the same two roads, then men on foot, then dusk.
- The field remembers: every crater, track, wreck and body stays.
- There are three ways of seeing it:
  - the drone, which flies like an aircraft in a gusting wind;
  - the observer on a nine-storey roof;
  - the gunner in a tree line, holding a Stugna's crosshair on a moving tank for twelve seconds, or waiting for a Javelin to lock.
- The player calls 155 mm, RAAM and HIMARS, and decides when the ATGM teams open fire.
- The debrief compares the day with the record.

Two things are still missing. **One day is not a winter**, and **a spectator with a fire button is not a player.** This plan is about both, and then about a second place.

## What stays true

These are the principles in `design/chapters.md`, sharpened by what the first diorama taught us.

1. **The world runs by itself.** If the player does nothing, the winter happens roughly as it did.
2. **History is the frame, not the scoreboard.** The record shapes the script, the forces and the outcome bands. **It never appears in the interface.** There are no citations and no "the record says". The debrief speaks only in the day's own terms. The record lives in the explorer, for anyone who wants it.
3. **The outcome is fixed; the cost is yours.** Vuhledar holds; that is not in question. What the player owns is the price: the men, the positions, the shells, the drones, and whether the line can still be held tomorrow.
4. **Seeing is the scarcest thing.** The ATGM teams, the brigade guns and the T-64 act on what someone has seen. A drone that is down or jammed, or an observation post that is destroyed, means a blind sector.
5. **Nothing is cleared.** Wrecks, craters and bodies carry over from day to day. The field is the campaign's memory.
6. **Every view is a person.** The drone operator, the observer, the gunner and the tank crew. No god's-eye camera, ever.

## Part I: the Vuhledar winter

### The shape: night, day, ledger

Each campaign day has three phases:

```
   NIGHT                    DAY                         LEDGER
   the map table      →     the day runs by itself  →   what it cost
   (decide)                 (see, call, endure)         (carry it forward)
        ↑                                                     │
        └──────────────── the next night ─────────────────────┘
```

### The days

Nine days from the dossier's timeline (§2). Difficulty rises the way it rose in history: Russia adapts, and Ukraine runs short. Enemy statistics never inflate. What changes is what comes down the road, what the brigade has left, and the ground under it all.

| # | date | what comes | what is new for the player | ground and weather |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 24 Jan | Probes, 10–15 men to a group | The drone, spotting. One road. | Frost, clear |
| 2 | 26 Jan | Marines toward the T-0509; the first TOS-1A on the blocks | The OP; the brigade guns | Frost, 0% cloud |
| 3 | 29 Jan | 36th Motor Rifle tanks; Ka-52s at a distance | ATGM hold or fire; the first night at the map table | Light snow |
| 4 | 3 Feb | *Vignette: the SOF ambush* | The trench view | Snow cover |
| 5 | 6 Feb | **Black Monday**: two battalions, smoke and the plow | Everything at once (today's diorama) | Hard frost |
| 6 | 9 Feb | The column that loses 13 tanks and 12 BMPs; *vignette: the Wanderer* | The T-64 gunner | Hard frost, low cloud |
| 7 | 14 Feb | The pushback south of the town | Counterattack orders | Snow, 13 cm over the week |
| 8 | 26 Feb | No armour left: infantry waves into the thaw | Mortars; evacuation runs | Thaw, mud |
| 9 | 5 Mar | "At any cost": the 136th, the last push; *vignette: the notebook* | Nothing new. Only less. | Thaw, rain |

The arc is the pointlessness, played. Days 1–5 teach the kill zone. On days 6–9 the player is good at it, and it doesn't matter: they keep coming, the shells run lower, and the field fills up.

### The night before

The game's one moment of authorship. A paper map under a headlamp, with marker strokes.

- **Mines.** A night's worth of TM-62s. Where the belts go and which lanes stay open: Bayak's design, done by the player. Mines laid one night stay in the ground (and in the minefield state) for the rest of the winter.
- **ATGM teams.** Drag a team to a tree line, and the map shows its sight cone and what it actually sees. The line-of-sight model already exists (we found it when the Stugna at x = 560 was looking into a belt).
- **Registered points.** Three or four. The brigade guns fire on these without being asked.
- **The OP.** Which roof. The highest sees furthest, and is where the TOS-1A aims.
- **The road.** Resupply comes up the T-0509 at night: shells, missiles, drone batteries, replacements. Each night shows how much came, and it is never what was asked for.

### The day: tasks over the radio

The radio already carries the brigade's voice; now it also gives orders. Every task is a real practice of that front:

- **Spot.** "Bird, find the column." While a column is in the drone's frame, the ATGM teams and the guns see it too. When it leaves, they lose it.
- **Adjust.** "Splash. Adjust." Calls on the same target get tighter, as they already do; now the player is asked to.
- **Confirm.** "Confirm the kill at point two." Damage assessment from the drone: a vehicle no one confirmed is still a vehicle on tomorrow's map.
- **Evacuate.** "Three hundred at the tree line." Escort the evacuation run with the drone, or don't. Wounded who are not brought out become dead in the ledger.
- **Endure.** On the roof during the TOS, there is nothing to do but stay.

### Rationing

Scarcity is the one difficulty slider the history gives us.

- **Shells:** 96 rounds for today is already in the game. The rule changes: what is spent today is missing on day 8. Zaluzhnyi named this as the binding constraint (dossier §4).
- **Missiles:** each team's load, resupplied at night if the road was open.
- **Drones:** three airframes and their batteries. EW takes some; the gusts take some.
- **Men:** positions lose strength, and replacements arrive late and green.

### The ledger

The debrief, rebuilt without the record. It is the day's own account, in four parts:

1. **The field at dusk.** One slow drone pass over today's wrecks, smoke still rising. No text for a few seconds.
2. **What it cost.** The men of the 72nd: killed, wounded, brought out. The positions lost and kept. Shells, missiles and drones spent.
3. **What came.** The Russian tally in the form the war itself kept it: *sent so many, came back so many.* From day 9 this column is headed with a photograph of a notebook page.
4. **What is left.** Tomorrow's stock, tomorrow's line, tomorrow's weather.

**Winning** stays the historical outcome, kept invisible. The game knows the bands: no Russian foothold in the first belt, no position inside the town, armour losses in the plausible range. Breaking them ends the campaign early, with a quiet card: *the line moved.* Holding them is not celebrated. The next night simply comes.

The campaign ends on 31 March with the field. How many wrecks, counted by the drone. Then one line about October 2024, if we decide we want it (see the questions below).

### The embodied vignettes

These are short, first-person, scripted, and placed in the campaign; each is two to four minutes long. Each one costs little, because the systems already exist.

| vignette | day | where you are | what you do | built on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The gunner** | any | an ATGM team's trench parapet | Hold a Stugna's crosshair for twelve seconds while shells walk toward you | Exists (the ground view) |
| **The ambush** | 4 (3 Feb) | Night, an SOF team in a tree line, thermal sight | A T-80 hits a mine; take the two crew alive (the dossier's set piece 2). Capture, not kill | Ground view, plus night and thermal post-process |
| **The Wanderer** | 6 (9 Feb) | Inside a T-64 in a hide at grey pre-dawn | The commander reads grid numbers from a drone pilot over the radio; you lay the gun on a target you cannot see, fire, and wait for "splash" | New: a turret interior, indirect lay |
| **The roof** | 2 or 5 | The OP during a TOS-1A salvo | Nothing. Stay. The building shakes; dust falls; then you report what you saw | The OP view, with shake, sound and dust |
| **The notebook** | 9 (5 Mar) | A drone hovering low over a dead Russian officer in the snow | Read the four days of tallies on the open page: *100, 116, 103, 115. 16, 23, 15, 3.* | One still scene, the notebook page as a texture |

The notebook is the campaign's last image before the ledger. Nothing in it is explained.

### What has to be built for Part I

- **Diorama as data.** The layout, the day scripts, the force lists and the weather move out of code into one RON file per diorama, and one per campaign day. That's the refactor that makes Part II cheap.
- **Persistence.** At the end of the day, save the ground textures (252 PNGs), the wrecks, bodies and minefields, the positions and the stock. Load them at night.
- **The picture.** The model of who sees what: drone footprint, OP sight lines, the ground teams' line of sight. Fire decisions come from it.
- **The map table.** A 2D top-down interface over the same layout, for placement and for the night's resupply.
- **Night and thermal.** A night lighting preset, and a white-hot thermal post-process for the ambush and the drone at dusk.
- **A regression day.** Run day 5 headless and assert the tally stays in its band. The day is deterministic, so this test is cheap.

## Part II: Robotyne, the same field in summer

### Why this one

At Vuhledar the player watched Russian columns die in a minefield. At Mala Tokmachka on 8 June 2023 a Ukrainian column of Leopards and Bradleys drove into one. **Robotyne is Vuhledar from the other side.** The player, still always Ukraine, now commands the column. The game asks for the same thing as before, the historical outcome: the breach fails, the armour is stopped, and the summer is spent going forward on foot, a tree line at a time, until Robotyne is taken in a day in August, and the line stops short of Tokmak.

It is also the cheapest new place: the same shelterbelt grid (0.5–0.6 km by 1.0–1.2 km, dossier §3), the same procedural kit, the same systems. What changes is the season, the hardware and the direction.

### The scene

- **Summer.** Wheat and sunflower fields instead of snow; green, dusty belts; heat shimmer at noon; 04:40 sunrise, 20:30 sunset.
- **Dust.** It replaces the snow spray. Every vehicle trails it, every impact throws it, and drones see it from kilometres away.
- **The Surovikin line.** Trenches cut into the terrain, anti-tank ditches and rows of dragon's teeth.
- **The sky.** Ka-52s orbit at the edge of view and lob rockets from 8 km; Lancets hunt the guns.
- **The emblem.** The drone image of a Leopard 2A6 and eight Bradleys in a row is an end state the player will recognise. No one tells them; they have seen it before.

### The days

| # | date | the player | the outcome to reproduce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 Jun | The breach south of Mala Tokmachka: Leopard 2R and BMR-2 in front, twenty vehicles in two groups | The column is stopped; most crews get out through open hatches |
| 2 | 11 Jun | **The decision Zaluzhnyi made:** stop the armour, or send it again | The armour stops (the game does not forbid sending it; the field will) |
| 3 | 8 Jul | Dismounted platoons through the tree lines under drones | Hundreds of metres a day |
| 4 | 26 Jul | The second push east of Robotyne | A break-in that does not hold its furthest gains |
| 5 | 22–23 Aug | "Robotyne in a day": the order changes mid-move, three times | The flag on the 23rd |
| 6 | 20–23 Sep | Through the dragon's teeth at Verbove | A breakthrough announced; no breakthrough made |

**The vignettes:**
- **The Bradley crew** popping smoke to take back the dismounts, from inside the hull.
- **The MANPADS gunner** who downs the brigade's eighth Ka-52 with a single shot (17 Aug).

### What has to be built for Part II

- **Season and palette.** A summer ground (crops, stubble, dust), green belt crowns, and heat haze in the feed shader.
- **Hardware.** Leopard 2A6 and 2R, Bradley M2A2 ODS, BMR-2, Wisent and MaxxPro for Ukraine; T-72B3, BMP-2 and the Ka-52 (the first flying model, with rotor and lob-fire behaviour) for Russia.
- **Terrain cuts.** Trenches and an anti-tank ditch carved into the height function, and dragon's-teeth instancing.
- **The attacker's AI.** Breaching drills: the plow lane, marking, the following vehicles keeping to the lane, and dismounting in contact. The defender's AI: ATGM positions, mines re-laid at night, Ka-52 orbits.
- **The inverted ledger.** Our crews brought out, vehicles recovered, metres gained. The Russian side is reckoned only as what stood in the way.

## The order of work

Each milestone ends with a clip in `devlog/videos/`, as the history requires.

1. **Diorama as data and persistence.** Day 5 loads from RON, saves at dusk, and reloads with its wrecks. *The clip: the same field, the next morning.*
2. **The night before and the ledger.** Minefield placement, the team cones, the road, and the debrief without the record. *The clip: the map table at night, then the field at dusk.*
3. **The picture and the tasks.** Who sees what; spot, adjust, confirm and evacuate. *The clip: a column lost and found again by the drone.*
4. **Three days of campaign.** Days 1, 3 and 5, with rationing carried across them. *The clip: three dawns over the same road.*
5. **The vignettes.** The ambush, the Wanderer, the roof, the notebook. *The clip: the notebook.*
6. **The full winter.** All nine days, and the end of March.
7. **Robotyne.** First the breach of 8 June alone, as a single diorama, then the summer.

## Questions for you

- **The end card.** Should the campaign's last line say what happened in October 2024, that the town fell to its flanks? That is narration, not a comparison, but it is the record speaking in the interface.
- **Names.** The 72nd's soldiers in the ledger: call signs (fictional), or no names at all, only numbers? Real names are out.
- **The ambush's prisoners.** The source video contains footage of prisoners. The vignette can stop at "taken alive", or show them being walked back. I would stop.
- **Robotyne's weight.** The breach of 8 June killed few men and destroyed much Western armour. Playing your own side's column into a minefield is the hardest thing this game will ask. Should it be the second diorama, or wait until the winter has taught the player what a minefield is?
