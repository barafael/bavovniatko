# 04: Ground tactics, fortifications and manpower

Research base for *bavovniatko*. Accessed 2026-09-28. English-language sources only, with Ukrainian outlets preferred and paired with Western analysis. Russian tactics are described as Ukrainian or Western observers report them. Casualty and recruitment figures are **claims**, and the claimant is always named. Citations use the form `[src:<id>]` and resolve in `sources/04-ground-tactics-manpower.yaml`.

## 1. Overview

Ground combat went through three broad phases:

1. **Manoeuvre and firepower (2022).** Russia invaded with under-strength battalion tactical groups (BTGs). Its units moved in administrative road columns and were ordered to bypass resistance. Many soldiers "arrived in towns without their weapons loaded" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Once that failed, Russia fell back on artillery saturation in Donbas, with a 12:1 artillery advantage in May–June 2022. Ukraine then used a thinly held Russian line to break through at Kharkiv (75 km in four days) [src:euromaidan-2022-shandra-kharkiv]. At Kherson it advanced slowly and at high cost [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
2. **Attrition by expendable infantry against prepared defences (late 2022 to 2024).** Wagner and convict assault groups fought at Bakhmut, and mobilized and Storm-Z/Storm-V troops were layered by function into "disposable", line, assault and specialised infantry [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Russian engineers built belts of minefields and trenches up to 30 km deep, which broke Ukraine's mechanized 2023 counteroffensive [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Russia then tried battalion-size mechanized waves at Vuhledar and Avdiivka, and later around Kurakhove, and lost armour at scale [src:frontelligence-2023-avdiivka-losses].
3. **The drone kill zone (mid-2024 to now).** Drones cause 60–70% of damaged or destroyed Russian systems [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. Vehicles cannot survive near the line, so Russia shifted to **small-group infiltration**: 2–4 soldiers, often on motorcycles or ATVs, move through the gaps between sparse Ukrainian positions and gather in the rear [src:euromaidan-2024-bbc-small-groups] [src:aljazeera-2025-psaropoulos-pokrovsk]. Ukraine defends with a "drone-first" posture. Fire teams of two or three hold positions about 50 m apart, sometimes for months [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments] [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-502-days]. Evacuation of the wounded now takes hours to weeks [src:euromaidan-2026-kossov-evacuation]. The kill zone is now 15–30 km deep, and Ukraine is netting roads against drones [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-anti-drone-nets].

**2026 turn (e9).** In February 2026 Russia lost Starlink, which "blinded headquarters" [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-air-assault-south]. Ukraine began local counteroffensives: the Air Assault Forces in eastern Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia, and the 3rd Army Corps' **Operation Vivaldi** near Lyman. Vivaldi's key idea was to kill the *reinforcement and supply* of Russian infiltration groups rather than chase every infiltrator [src:mwi-2026-rose-vivaldi] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment] [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi].

**Manpower is the binding constraint for both sides.**
- **Russia** recruited more than 400,000 contract soldiers in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments] and, by HUR's count, 403,000 in 2025 [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k]. Recruitment fell to about 800 a day in Q1 2026 despite record bonuses [src:kluge-2026-recruitment] [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-bonuses]. Ukrainian intelligence says Russia recruited about 232,000 from January to July 2026 while losing about 240,000 [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-mobilization-600k].
- **Ukraine** lowered its mobilization age to 25 in 2024 [src:rferl-2024-mobilization-law] and mobilizes about 30,000 a month, roughly half of what the army says it needs [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-draft-crisis]. Voluntary schemes have drawn few recruits: about 500 on the 1M UAH "18–24" contracts by December 2025 [src:euromaidan-2026-katola-18-24], and about 2,000 on the June 2026 fixed-term contracts in their first month [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-syrskyi-silence]. It still faces an AWOL crisis: a record 21,602 cases in October 2025, and about 200,000 people AWOL by January 2026 [src:kyivindependent-2026-terajima-awol].

## 2. Per-era notes

### e1-invasion (Feb–Apr 2022)
- **Russian assault style.** BTGs were assembled from whole brigades and had not trained together. Orders "descended into orders to reach specific locations within a specified timeframe". Units moved in administrative column and were told to bypass Ukrainian units [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. The air assault on Hostomel came in two waves of 10 helicopters each.
- **Ukrainian defence.** RUSI credits massed fires from two artillery brigades, more than ATGMs, with stopping the thrust on Kyiv. The artillery ratio at the start was just over 2:1 in Russia's favour: 2,433 tube guns against 1,176 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Before the war Ukraine had received about 150 Javelin launchers with 1,000–1,200 missiles and 2,000 NLAW. NLAW went to ground-holding units and Javelin to specialist troops.
- **Force density.** The Russian invasion force was too small for the breadth of the front. This became decisive later [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e2-donbas-artillery (May–Aug 2022)
- **Russian assault style.** Russian guns fired about 20,000 rounds a day, peaking above 32,000, against at most 6,000 Ukrainian rounds; the ratio was about 12:1 in May–June. Each attack followed a cycle: LNR/DNR conscripts skirmished forward to make Ukrainian positions reveal themselves, reconnaissance fixed the positions, artillery pounded them, and then VDV, spetsnaz or Wagner companies assaulted. Conscripts then occupied the ground and the capable troops withdrew. Each cycle took 1–2 days, and advances were "a couple of kilometres per day, and often less" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Ukrainian defence.** Ukraine held positions under saturation fire until they were no longer viable, then withdrew from Sievierodonetsk in mid-June. Precision long-range fires (HIMARS) then hit Russian logistics, which depended on railways [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

### e3-counteroffensives-22 (Sep–Nov 2022)
- **Ukrainian offence.** Kherson began on 29 August 2022 and drew in Russian reserves. Kharkiv followed on a thinly held front: Ukraine did not surprise Russia about its intentions, but did surprise it on timing [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Ukraine advanced 75 km in four days (Balakliia, Izium, Kupiansk) against a Russian defence of "one tier" [src:euromaidan-2022-shandra-kharkiv]. At Kherson, Russia withdrew in good order across the Dnipro in October–November [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Russian response.** Russia declared mobilization, took the Dnipro as a barrier, and began building deliberate defence lines (the "Surovikin line") in Zaporizhzhia. By May 2023 its group of forces was about 420,000 strong [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e4-bakhmut (Dec 2022–May 2023)
- **Russian assault style.** Meatgrinder describes a four-tier infantry system:
  - Disposable infantry came from DNR/LNR conscripts, Wagner convicts and under-trained mobilized men. They were first organised in companies of about 60, later in platoons of about 15, and attacked in fire teams of 2–5, with up to 5 teams on one axis.
  - The teams skirmished "often until killed". Some were shot by their own side when retreating, and Ukrainian troops report many appeared to be on amphetamines.
  - Specialised and assault troops were then sent against the Ukrainian positions this exposed [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
  - At Artemivske in December 2022, four UAVs flew over each assault group, covering reconnaissance, reserve routes, ambush detection and the commander's view.
- **Losses.** 19,547 Wagner fighters were killed at Bakhmut and a similar number seriously wounded. 88% of them were convicts; trained Wagner deaths averaged 40–60 a week. About 10,000 Ukrainians were killed or severely wounded in and around the city, and Russia held a 5:1 artillery advantage [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Russian penal units.** Storm-Z convict companies in regular units offered a pardon after six months and ended in August 2023. From late 2023 they were replaced by Storm-V, with no pardon and contracts lasting until the end of the war [src:ukrpravda-2024-isw-storm-v].
- **Vuhledar.** The ill-prepared January–February 2023 offensive "beginning in Vuhledar" was extremely costly. The Russian command appears to have judged it could "trade lives for time" [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Ukrainian defence.** Ukraine lost about 10,000 UAVs a month to Russian EW [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Ukrainian losses turned unfavourable once Russian artillery controlled the roads into Bakhmut [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e5-counteroffensive-23 (Jun–Nov 2023)
- **Russian defence.** Two to three lines. AT ditches 4 m deep and 6 m wide, dragon's teeth and wire; each obstacle belt 700 m to 1 km wide, with the second line about 5 km back and total depth above 30 km on some axes [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. On the Orikhiv–Tokmak axis each 10 km of front was held by one motor-rifle regiment plus one airborne regiment, with about 105,000 troops available [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Minefields were deepened from 120 m to up to 500 m, and AT mines were stacked two high so that a single strike immobilises a vehicle even with a dozer blade [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Ukrainian offence.** The 9th Corps opened with mechanized company breaches using MICLIC and UR-77. The cleared lanes were too shallow, crews strayed off the path, and the corps had only 10 demining vehicles, which overheated [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Deliberate dismounted assaults then worked but were slow: 700–1,200 m every five days [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Each battalion produced only about two platoons of assault-capable troops [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Russian remote mining.** The ISDM Zemledeliye rocket minelayer scatters mines 5–15 km away and maps the fields automatically. It was used heavily against the 2023 counteroffensive [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-zemledeliye], alongside artillery-delivered scatterable mines that made night resupply through the breach lanes deadly [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Krynky (Oct 2023–mid-2024).** Marines held a lodgement on the east bank of the Dnipro, but it was never exploited because a larger force could not be sustained across the river [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. National Police data obtained by Slidstvo.Info list 788 marines missing and 262 dead recovered from October 2023 to June 2024. Ukraine pulled out in about June–July 2024 after the village was destroyed [src:euromaidan-2024-martyniuk-krynky-missing].

### e6-avdiivka-attrition (Oct 2023–Jul 2024)
- **Russian assault style.** Battalion-level mechanized waves. Satellite imagery shows 109 vehicles lost from 10 to 20 October 2023, about 200 according to ground sources. Once the armour failed, the infantry went in on foot [src:frontelligence-2023-avdiivka-losses]. Storm-V units fought at Avdiivka [src:ukrpravda-2024-isw-storm-v]. "Turtle" (shed) tanks appeared in April 2024 at Krasnohorivka, with mine rollers and EW. They absorb FPVs but are nearly blind, and their turrets cannot traverse [src:wikipedia-turtle-tank].
- **Ukrainian defence.** Ukraine defended with mines, artillery (short of shells) and FPVs. The April 2024 mobilization law lowered the call-up age from 27 to 25 and dropped demobilization after 36 months [src:rferl-2024-mobilization-law].
- **Vovchansk (from May 2024).** Russia's cross-border push into northern Kharkiv Oblast turned into an urban fight for Vovchansk. By July over 200 Russians had been besieged in the reinforced aggregate plant for more than a month and were kept alive by drone resupply. Ukraine chose bombardment over assault, and its drones destroyed dozens of excavators digging Russian trenches [src:euromaidan-2024-rfu-vovchansk-aggregate].

### e7-kursk-pokrovsk (Aug 2024–Mar 2025)
- **Ukrainian offence (Kursk).** The 6 August 2024 break-in was led by the 80th and 82nd Air Assault brigades, with the deployment disguised as defensive. About 100 settlements and roughly 600 prisoners were taken [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit]. Preparatory collection and shaping took up to a month, even though the initial breach was small, at two points [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. The salient depended on one road, which Russian fiber-optic FPVs cut. Ukraine withdrew in February–March 2025, with equipment losses of 790 Ukrainian against 740 Russian [src:kyivindependent-2025-terajima-kursk-gambit].
- **North Korean troops.** More than 11,000 combat troops were sent from October 2024 [src:koreatimes-2026-yonhap-nk-kursk].
  - They first attacked in company or battalion strength, took mass casualties, and sometimes overran sparse Ukrainian positions. After losing about a quarter of the force they adopted Russian small-group tactics [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Ukrainian commanders describe teams of 5–8 and a 50-man group advancing 8 km on foot through mined ground. A captured diary gives the spacing for baiting drones: 7 m for the bait, 10–12 m for the shooters [src:kyivpost-2025-nk-tactics-kursk].
  - Later they fielded FPV teams, mobile ATGM crews and anti-drone drills [src:frontelligence-2025-nk-lessons]. Their later roles are in e8 and e9.
  - Casualty claims by claimant are in §4.
- **Russian assault style.**
  - The Russian group of forces numbered about 580,000 [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Russia first probes by section: groups of about 8, or up to 30 with 1–2 IFVs, conduct reconnaissance by drawing fire and look for the boundaries between Ukrainian units. One Ukrainian brigade counted an average of 27 section attacks a day. Russian units rotate out after about 30% losses [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Troops threatened with being "zeroed" (executed) for retreating tend to go to ground rather than fall back [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Autumn 2024 brought battalion-sized mechanized pushes before the mud season (Kurakhove, Pokrovsk) [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. In early November Kurakhove was the busiest axis, with 690 clashes in two weeks (435 at Pokrovsk). ISW recorded two mechanized assaults into and south of the town on 13 November, after Russia struck the Kurakhove reservoir dam, possibly to flood the flanks [src:euromaidan-2024-tril-kurakhove]. Then came the switch to small groups on motorcycles, civilian cars and scooters. The General Staff claims over 45,000 Russian casualties in November 2024 [src:euromaidan-2024-bbc-small-groups]. Motorcycle and ATV groups of "a dozen to a hundred riders" had been formally adopted by mid-2024 [src:rusi-2025-mcgovern-motorcycles].
- **Ukrainian defence and density.**
  - An infantry section covers 70–200 m of front, in fire teams of 2–3 in positions about 50 m apart. A company is spread over about 3 km of depth and a battalion over about 7 km. One brigade held 27 km with four battalions [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Local force ratios run 1:2 to 1:6 against Ukraine. Less than 25% of the roughly 800,000-strong Defence Forces is available as combat power [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - Armour hides and sallies out to fire directly, then returns to cover. About 50% of Ukrainian casualties are taken in the rear. Rotations are minimised, and troops stay on position for more than a month [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].

### e8-drone-kill-zone (Mar 2025–Jan 2026)
- **Russian assault style: infiltration.** At Pokrovsk, up to 300 soldiers a day were sent in "groups of three people with the expectation that two will be destroyed". Some infiltrators wore civilian clothes, and they exploited wet weather [src:aljazeera-2025-psaropoulos-pokrovsk]. Motorcycles and ATVs were used to cross open fields and bypass engineering barriers [src:rusi-2025-mcgovern-motorcycles]. In shelterbelt country both sides mostly attack *along* a treeline, not across it. A Russian assault group strings out 300–500 m deep: scouts 7–9 m apart, the main body 30 m behind, a consolidation group at 100–150 m and a reserve at up to 450 m. Approaches are timed to rain, fog, darkness or pre-dawn [src:saratoga-2026-parfonov-treelines]. Advance rates varied widely, from about 70 m a day at Pokrovsk to about 297 m a day at Huliaipole [src:csis-2026-jones-grinding-war].
- **Huliaipole (Nov–Dec 2025).** Russia massed up to nine brigades and regiments, one of them a tank brigade, on a 41 km front. Months of air interdiction of roads and rail, plus rain and fog that blinded Ukrainian drones, preceded the infiltration [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-huliaipole-nine-brigades]. The 102nd and 106th Territorial Defence brigades had taken heavy losses without rotation. When a 57th Motor Rifle Brigade assault group infiltrated the centre on 17 December, they broke, and the assault units sent in from November could not stabilise the town [src:euromaidan-2025-axe-huliaipole-fell]. No fetched source ties Rubikon to Huliaipole specifically. Elsewhere Rubikon, a drone centre of about 5,000, cut the Izium–Sloviansk road; its job is to interdict logistics and hunt drone crews, with no duty to support Russian infantry [src:euromaidan-2025-mukhina-rubikon-izium].
- **Ukrainian defence.**
  - The brigade-to-corps reform was announced in early 2025 and the 3rd Army Corps formed in March 2025. By early 2026 there were 18 corps [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-corps]. Part of the aim was to fix rotation problems, since rotations let Russians slip into positions [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
  - A robot held a position for 45 days in the 3rd Assault Brigade's sector. UGVs evacuate wounded 1–3 km [src:euromaidan-2025-kirichenko-golden-hour].
  - The "golden hour" is gone: the Medical Forces' chief surgeon says armored casevac is "practically impossible", and tourniquets left on during delayed evacuation cause amputations [src:euromaidan-2025-kirichenko-golden-hour]. Kostiantyn Humeniuk, the Armed Forces' chief surgeon, says bleeding is the main cause of death: 81.3% of wounds are from fragments and 18.7% from bullets, and 70% of vascular injuries are to the legs [src:euromaidan-2025-mukhina-trauma]. RUSI found early-2025 UGV casevac too unreliable to trust. Medics talk casualties through self-aid by radio and log tourniquet times centrally to cut amputations [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **North Korean later roles.** On 17 June 2025 Shoigu announced that Kim Jong Un would send 1,000 sappers to demine Kursk Oblast and 5,000 military builders [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-nk-restoration].
- **Manpower.**
  - Ukraine: "18–24" contracts opened on 11 February 2025, and a two-year drone-operator track was added in July. Uptake was slow, at about 500 by December 2025 [src:euromaidan-2026-katola-18-24] [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-contracts]. There is still no demobilization law, and service is tied to martial law [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-draft-crisis]. A record 21,602 AWOL and desertion cases were logged in October 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2026-terajima-awol].
  - Russia: Q1 2025 recruitment of 89,601, about 975 a day [src:kluge-2026-recruitment]. HUR says the 2025 plan of 403,000 was met by early December, and the 2026 plan is 409,000 [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k]. Russian casualties in 2025 were about 415,000, or about 35,000 a month [src:csis-2026-jones-grinding-war]. Syrskyi put them at 416,550, of whom about 110,000 died [src:euromaidan-2026-midttun-losses-recruits].
- **Empty ground.** By 2024, 7% of Ukraine's cropland (about 2.2 Mha) had been abandoned, mostly along the front. This is the untended field and grey-zone terrain that infiltrators cross [src:wagner-2025-cropland-abandonment].
- **Fortress belt.** Kostiantynivka is the last link of the Sloviansk–Kramatorsk–Druzhkivka–Kostiantynivka chain and was under infiltration by late 2025 [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-fortress-belt].

### e9-counteroffensive-26 (Feb 2026–present)
- **Starlink cutoff.** From 4 February 2026 Starlink whitelisted terminals, so Russia's smuggled units went dark. This "grounded many Russian drones and blinded headquarters" [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-air-assault-south]. Russians tried to re-register terminals through Ukrainian proxies [src:osw-2026-wilk-zaporizhzhia].
- **Southern counteroffensive.** Led by the Air Assault Forces, with the 425th Assault Regiment (Abrams) and the 37th Marines. By 22 February it had retaken 300+ km² and 8 settlements, amid up to 50 engagements a day [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-air-assault-south]. Possibly two brigades crossed the Vovcha [src:osw-2026-wilk-zaporizhzhia].
- **Operation Vivaldi (Lyman, from about late May or early June 2026).** Run by the 3rd Army Corps under "maximum information silence":
  - Assault teams and drones conducted "strike-search" actions against infiltration groups. The corps reported 75 km² cleared and 10 km² liberated, including Serednie; OSINT estimates exceed 200 km² [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi].
  - The deeper logic was to cut off infiltrators rather than hunt them. A "middle-strike" campaign at 30–200 km plus local drone dominance hit Russian logistics, depots and launch sites [src:mwi-2026-rose-vivaldi]. ISW says the first phase defeated infiltration groups by destroying the ATV and motorcycle assaults sent to reinforce them. Strikes on supply routes left the stranded groups demoralized and short of supplies, and only then were they cleared. By late September Ukraine was applying the same "target the reinforcement" pattern in other sectors [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
  - Assaults go in behind robots. 3rd Corps UGVs cleared 30 km of routes in two nights on the Shandryholove axis and now lift about 60% of the anti-personnel mines in its zone. One crew can also remotely mine up to 200 m of Russian routes a day. The corps claims 125 km² retaken over two phases [src:euromaidan-2026-vivdych-robot-demining]. Outside mappers put it higher, at 200–240 km²; that range is an estimate by mappers, not ISW's [src:kyivindependent-2026-york-vivaldi-gains].
  - The 60th Mechanized Brigade struck Russian drone launch sites at set times before infantry advanced. Russia responded by shifting south to infiltrate Mykolaivka toward Sloviansk [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-vivaldi-shift].
- **Fortress belt.** Kostiantynivka's population is down from 67,000 to about 2,500. Russian small groups infiltrate its streets, and ground robots haul most supplies [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-fortress-belt].
- **Russian infiltration in 2026.** Russia has made near-zero net gains since March 2026 [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment]. Radio Svoboda, citing DeepState, says Russian gains fell from 450–550 km² a month at the 2025 peak to 160 km² in April 2026 [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km]. Its figure of 27 km² for July is too low: DeepState itself reported 36 km² [src:kyivpost-2026-zavadska-deepstate-july], and the full monthly series is in file 07 §2.7. Infiltration groups of two to six walk 15–20 km to reach positions [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km]. At Kupiansk, Russian troops walked about 10 km through a disused gas pipeline to enter the city [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-kupiansk-pipeline]. In northern Kharkiv Oblast they bypass Ukrainian positions in a "chessboard" pattern. Glide bombs turn near-rear settlements into ruins that later infiltrators use for cover, and in the south the dried Kakhovka reservoir bed is an infiltration route [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **Protected armour.** A Russian T-72B3A fitted with an FPV-adapted Arena-M active protection system (July 2026) took seven drones to exhaust its countermeasures and 15–20 FPVs in all to kill [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-aps-80].
- **Anti-drone works.** 1,066 km of roads netted in 2026, with 4,000 km planned by year's end. The kill zone is 15–30 km deep. Ground robots flew 66,000 logistics and evacuation missions in the first half of 2026 [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-anti-drone-nets].
- **Remote mining.** Drones lay mines, and Ukraine claims 678 Russians killed by engineering obstacles in February 2026 [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-drone-mines]. Russian remote mining and drones have made supply routes near Novopavlivka too dangerous to drive, and a Zemledeliye launcher was destroyed by FPVs on the Zaporizhzhia front in July [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-zemledeliye]. Drone-dropped anti-tank mines also hit evacuation vehicles [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-chornovol-evacuation].
- **Mined land.** The war has affected about 133,300 km², including 57,900 km² of farmland, according to Deputy Minister Ihor Bezkaravainyi. He says the 20 km zone along the front "is not described in any international standard" [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-bezkaravainyi-demining]. A far smaller area, about 460,000 ha (4,600 km²), has been identified as requiring clearance [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-460k-hectares].
- **Rotation and evacuation.** After cases of soldiers held on position for 502 days, Syrskyi capped deployments at two months [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-502-days]. His July review restated it as rotation at least every 60 days "whenever operationally possible". No fetched source measures compliance [src:euromaidan-2026-murdoch-syrskyi-h1]. Wounded can still wait "days, weeks, and sometimes months" [src:euromaidan-2026-kossov-evacuation].
  - Robotic casevac is expensive. One early operation used about four UGVs to bring out a single wounded soldier, and one robot drew about 15 FPVs plus artillery. UGVs cannot cross rivers and get stuck in mud and glide-bomb craters [src:euromaidan-2026-murdoch-robotic-medevac].
  - Most units still lack dedicated evacuation groups or light armoured evacuation vehicles. In one 2025 case a single evacuation cost seven casualties after two vehicles hit drone-dropped AT mines [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-chornovol-evacuation].
  - More than 44,000 people have received prosthetics under the state programme since 2022, military and civilian combined [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-prosthetics].
- **Ukrainian manpower reform.** Fixed-term contracts opened on 15 June 2026. They run 10–14 months in infantry and assault roles and 24 months elsewhere, followed by a guaranteed deferral from mobilization. About 2,000 soldiers signed in the first month [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-syrskyi-silence]. Soldiers read the scheme as restarting the clock rather than offering a way home [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-draft-crisis].
  - Defence Minister Fedorov was dismissed in July amid street protests, and Mykhailo Drapatyi replaced Syrskyi as Commander-in-Chief [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-syrskyi-silence] [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-awol-return].
  - Drapatyi ordered a force-wide manning audit. An AWOL return scheme let those absent by 12 June report back by 20 September and choose among more than 70 units [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-awol-return].
  - Syrskyi's own H1 2026 review said Russia still held almost a 2:1 edge in personnel and equipment, while Ukraine carried out 40% of all assault actions [src:euromaidan-2026-murdoch-syrskyi-h1].
- **Casualty claims (September 2026).**
  - *Russian dead, counted by name.* Mediazona/BBC confirmed 248,844 to 10 September [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-mediazona] and about 259,000 to 21 September [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count]. Their estimated true toll is about 438–535k killed [src:wikipedia-casualties].
  - *Russian dead, official estimates.* The UK intelligence chief said in May 2026 that 500,000 Russians had been killed [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
  - *Russian killed and wounded.* Ukraine's General Staff claims 1,531,060 by 28 September [src:ukrpravda-2026-genstaff-losses]. Syrskyi puts the H1 2026 rate at about 32,000 a month [src:euromaidan-2026-murdoch-syrskyi-h1].
  - *Russian cost per km².* Radio Svoboda, from General Staff and DeepState data, puts Russian losses per km² gained at 85 in 2025 and 290 in 2026, against ISW's 53 for autumn 2024 [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
  - *Ukrainian dead.* Zelensky said in February 2026 that 55,000 had been killed, up from 31,000 (Feb 2024) and 46,000 (Feb 2025), with a "large number" missing [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-zelensky-55k]. UALosses has named 96,821 Ukrainian dead and 97,938 missing to 21 June 2026 [src:ualosses-2026-soldiers].
  - *North Koreans.* HUR claims over 7,000 casualties, against about 6,000 per NIS and British estimates [src:kyivindependent-2026-terajima-nk-hur].
- **North Koreans in 2026.** In January the troops in Kursk were firing tube artillery and MLRS at Ukrainian border areas and running reconnaissance, rotating under a bilateral agreement; about 3,000 had gone home (HUR via Yonhap) [src:koreatimes-2026-yonhap-nk-kursk]. On 28 September Zelensky said more than 8,000 were in Russia and another 10,000 were being prepared [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-nk-mobilization].
- **Russian manpower.** Q1 2026 recruitment was 71,216 (about 800 a day), a three-year low; it recovered to about 1,000 a day in April [src:kluge-2026-recruitment]. Regional sign-up bonuses reached a mean of 1.47M RUB [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-bonuses]. Russia missed its 30,000-a-month target in 2026 [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-mediazona].
  - Syrskyi says Russian losses exceeded recruitment for the first time in H1 2026 [src:euromaidan-2026-midttun-losses-recruits]. Ukrainian intelligence gives about 232,000 recruited against about 240,000 lost from January to July. It also describes a Russian General Staff contingency to mobilize 300,000 a year in 2026–27, which Putin publicly denied [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-mobilization-600k].
  - After the September Duma elections Zelensky said "additional mobilization" had begun. A 28 September decree raised active strength to 1,550,500 [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-nk-mobilization].

## 5. Russia's side as reported

(Reported by Ukrainian and Western sources, not Russian state media.)

- **Force generation.** Russia shifted from BTGs to divisions and regiments, fixing armies and divisions to axes while rotating battalions. Commanders stay on familiar ground; Ukraine struggled to match this until the corps reform [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Recruitment is money-driven.** Prisoners of war describe their motives as mainly financial. Death payments are large enough to change a family's standard of living, while payments for wounds are being cut [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. Russia contracted more than 400,000 in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. HUR counts 403,000 in 2025 against a 2026 plan of 409,000 [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k]. Budget-derived counts show Q1 2026 at a three-year low of 71,216 [src:kluge-2026-recruitment], even as regional bonuses hit record averages of about 1.5M RUB [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-bonuses]. By Ukrainian estimates, 2026 is the first year in which losses outrun recruitment, so a mobilization contingency of 300,000 a year is being prepared [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-mobilization-600k] [src:euromaidan-2026-midttun-losses-recruits]. A senior Ukrainian official estimates that Russia needs about 400,000 recruits a year just to stand still [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-nk-mobilization].
- **Who dies.** Among the Russian dead confirmed by name to September 2026, the largest groups are contract soldiers (88,600) and volunteers (80,679). Prisoners (26,979) and mobilized men (20,350) follow [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-mediazona]. Volunteers are usually over 30 and mobilized men over 25, while early-war dead were 21–23-year-old regulars [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
- **Penal and expendable troops.** Wagner convicts (2022–23), then Storm-Z (until August 2023), then Storm-V (no pardon, contract until the end of the war) [src:ukrpravda-2024-isw-storm-v]. Ukrainian troops report drugs, retreating soldiers being shot, and threats of being "zeroed" [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder] [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **Engineering is a Russian strength.** Two engineer companies per brigade, doctrinal belts of defences, heavy mining, and concrete command posts [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Russian formations seed mixed AP and AT minefields systematically, while Ukraine, short of mines, seeds only the axes where it expects attacks [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]. Rocket (Zemledeliye) and artillery remote mining close routes deep behind the line [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-zemledeliye]. Glide bombs (over 70,000 UMPK kits ordered for 2025) demolish Ukrainian positions, and "no fortification offers adequate protection" [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **Adaptation is reactive.** Russia gets better at the problems in front of it but struggles to anticipate new ones [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Its answer to the kill zone was cheaper, faster and smaller units: motorcycles, then infiltration. Its dependence on Starlink became a single point of failure in February 2026 [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-air-assault-south].
- **Drone interdiction as an arm of its own.** Rubikon is a well-funded drone centre of about 5,000. It interdicts roads such as Izium–Sloviansk and hunts Ukrainian drone crews [src:euromaidan-2025-mukhina-rubikon-izium].
- **Allies.** North Korean infantry (2024–25) learned fast but at heavy cost [src:frontelligence-2025-nk-lessons]. Later deployments shifted to demining and construction (1,000 sappers and 5,000 builders announced in June 2025) [src:euromaidan-2025-zoria-nk-restoration]. By 2026 the troops were artillery and reconnaissance units on regular rotation [src:koreatimes-2026-yonhap-nk-kursk]. A further 10,000 are reportedly being prepared [src:kyivindependent-2026-hyatt-nk-mobilization].

## 6. Game/sim relevance

- **Model the kill zone as a depth band with per-hex detection and strike probability.** Transparency is near total within about 3 km and falls off to about 15 km (2025) [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments]; by 2026 it extends to 15–30 km [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-anti-drone-nets]. Infantry, vehicles, rotations and casevac all pay a "movement in the open" tax there. Nets, fog, rain and night should lower it, and **Russian assaults also favour bad weather** [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **Defend with 2–3 soldier positions, not lines.** Each position gets a supply state (drone drops, UGV runs), an exhaustion clock (rotation every month or more; at most 2 months after April 2026) and a morale and AWOL risk [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-502-days] [src:kyivindependent-2026-terajima-awol]. Rotation should be a risky, deliberate operation that the enemy can exploit.
- **Give Russian AI assault behaviour by era.** BTG columns (e1); artillery plus conscript cycle (e2); disposable teams of 2–5 (e4); mechanized battalion waves (e6/e7); motorcycle rushes; then 2–4-man infiltration that accumulates behind the line (e8/e9). Russian units should rotate out at about 30% losses [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **Wounded as a resource-time problem.** A wounded soldier on a position stays there for hours to weeks. Tourniquet timing drives amputation (a "limb loss" state). Robotic casevac succeeds or fails on terrain and FPV density [src:euromaidan-2025-kirichenko-golden-hour] [src:euromaidan-2026-kossov-evacuation]. Unrecovered dead should hurt morale and compensation [src:rusi-2025-watling-tactical-developments].
- **2026 player verbs: "interdict the reinforcement" and "exploit a C2 blackout".** The Vivaldi pattern (strike launch sites, depots and ATV or motorcycle reinforcement; isolate infiltrators; then clear them) should beat whack-a-mole hunting [src:mwi-2026-rose-vivaldi] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment]. A Starlink-style event could degrade Russian drone and C2 effectiveness for a window [src:euromaidan-2026-zoria-air-assault-south].
- **Mines as a persistent, remotely placed layer.** Both sides lay mines at range: Russian rocket fields 5–15 km out [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-zemledeliye], and Ukrainian UGV crews up to 200 m of route a day [src:euromaidan-2026-vivdych-robot-demining]. Mines should block routes, raise evacuation losses (drone-dropped AT mines on evac vehicles) [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-chornovol-evacuation], and leave a post-battle contamination footprint.
- **Casevac as a costed mission.** A robotic evacuation may need several UGVs and a four-role team, and it draws FPVs [src:euromaidan-2026-murdoch-robotic-medevac]. Most wounds are fragment wounds and the main killer is bleeding [src:euromaidan-2025-mukhina-trauma], so tourniquet time is the key state variable.
- **Manpower flows as a strategic layer.** Ukraine takes in about 30,000 mobilized a month [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-draft-crisis], and voluntary contract uptake is small [src:euromaidan-2026-katola-18-24] [src:euromaidan-2026-helme-syrskyi-silence]. Unrotated units break, as the territorial brigades did at Huliaipole [src:euromaidan-2025-axe-huliaipole-fell]. On the Russian side, recruitment of about 33,000 a month (January–July 2026) barely covers losses [src:euromaidan-2026-thomas-mobilization-600k], while the losses per km² gained rose from 53 to 290 [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- **Offensives need breaching capacity and assault-qualified platoons.** Only about 2 per battalion [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Mine depth (120 m versus 500 m) and demining vehicle count should gate breakthroughs, and breaches should exploit poorly unless fires dominate [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

## 7. Terms

- *pikhota* (піхота) — infantry; used by troops for the scarce "trench infantry" on which everything depends.
- *shturmovyk* / *shturmovyky* (штурмовик) — assault soldier(s); also the new assault regiments and the Assault Forces (Штурмові війська).
- *nul* / *nulovka* / *nulova liniia* (нуль, нульова лінія) — the "zero line", the forward-most occupied positions.
- *sira zona* (сіра зона) — grey zone; contested ground between the lines.
- *kilzona* (кілзона) — kill zone; the drone-dominated belt, 15–30 km deep by 2026.
- *lisosmuha* (лісосмуга) — treeline or shelterbelt; the lines of trees between fields that shape the southern and eastern steppe fight.
- *blindazh* (бліндаж) — dugout or bunker with overhead cover.
- *pozytsiia* (позиція) — position; the unit of Ukrainian defence (2–3 soldiers).
- *rotatsiia* (ротація) — rotation of troops on and off positions.
- *evak* / *evakuatsiia* (евак) — evacuation, usually casevac.
- *NRK* (НРК, наземний роботизований комплекс) — ground robotic system, i.e. a UGV.
- *skyd* (скид) — drop; a munition (or supply bundle) dropped from a drone.
- *SZCh* (СЗЧ, самовільне залишення частини) — unauthorized leaving of a unit, i.e. AWOL (Criminal Code Art. 407; desertion is Art. 408).
- *TTsK* (ТЦК) — territorial recruitment centre (the draft office); *busyfikatsiia* is slang for forcible street mobilization into vans.
- *"200" / "300"* — Soviet-derived codes still in use: 200 = killed, 300 = wounded.
- *cherepakha* (черепаха) — "turtle"; a Russian shed tank.
- *mangal* — "barbecue grill"; improvised cage armour against drones.
- *poias fortets* (пояс фортець) — fortress belt: Sloviansk–Kramatorsk–Druzhkivka–Kostiantynivka.
- *zeky* (Russian зэки) — convicts; Wagner and Storm prisoner recruits.
- *myasnye shturmy* (Russian мясные штурмы) — "meat assaults"; repeated expendable-infantry attacks.
- *obnulit* (Russian обнулить) — to "zero" someone, i.e. execute them; the threat used against Russians who retreat.
- *Storm-Z / Storm-V* — Russian MoD penal assault units of convicts: Z had pardons (2023), V has none.
- *BTG* — battalion tactical group, the 2022 Russian unit of action.
- *MICLIC / UR-77* — explosive line-charge minefield-breaching systems.
- *UMPK / KAB* — Russian glide-bomb kits and guided aerial bombs.
- *Surovikin line* — the Russian 2022–23 fortified belt in Zaporizhzhia.
- *Zemledeliye* (Russian Земледелие, "agriculture") — ISDM truck-mounted rocket minelayer that scatters mines 5–15 km away.
- *Rubikon* (Russian Рубикон) — Russia's elite drone centre (about 5,000 personnel), used for deep drone interdiction and drone-crew hunting.
- *Kontrakt 18–24* (контракт 18–24) — Ukraine's one-year voluntary contract for men below mobilization age.

## 8. Open questions and gaps

- **Ukrainian casualties remain opaque.** The range runs from Zelensky's 55,000 killed (Feb 2026) through UALosses' named count (about 97,000 dead and 98,000 missing, June 2026) to CSIS's 100–140k. Kyiv publishes no wounded figure after Zelensky's February 2025 statement of 380,000. The missing blur the split between killed and wounded.
- **Russian totals still diverge.** They range from about 259,000 named dead (Mediazona/BBC), through 500,000 killed (UK intelligence), to about 1.53M killed and wounded (Ukrainian General Staff). The only KIA:WIA trend found is a low-reliability columnist's derivation from General Staff data.
- **Ukrainian manpower reform is in flux.** Whether the June 2026 contract "experiment" becomes law is unknown: Syrskyi questioned its legal basis, and the defence minister who launched it has been dismissed. No figures were found for the AWOL return scheme's uptake or for compliance with the 60-day rotation target. AWOL totals conflict: Euromaidan says "more than 100,000" cumulative, while Fedorov said about 200,000 in January 2026.
- **Minefield density per km** for 2023 versus 2024–26 was not found. The RUSI reports, Euromaidan and ISW give only qualitative descriptions (systematic Russian seeding, remote mining) and unit claims such as 200 m a day. No UN or HALO figure for mined area was fetched; the two official Ukrainian figures (133,300 km² affected, 4,600 km² requiring clearance) measure different things.
- **UGV casevac success rate.** No success or failure percentage was found, only anecdotes (four UGVs for one casualty) and RUSI's early-2025 verdict that the systems were unreliable.
- **Battles still thin.** Euromaidan reports on the later Vovchansk fighting (2025–26) and on the September 2024 recapture of the aggregate plant were found but not added. Kurakhove rests on one DeepState/ISW summary. Rubikon's role at Huliaipole is unconfirmed.
- **North Korean primary sources.** South Korean NIS statements were reached only through Yonhap and OSW secondary reports; 38 North, NK News (paywalled) and France24 (403) could not be read.
- **Search limits.** The web-search budget was exhausted before this pass, so sources came from Euromaidan's WordPress search API, Kyiv Independent tag pages, and links inside fetched pages. The ISW page was read through a Wayback Machine snapshot.
