# Kherson and the Antonivskyi bridge: the HIMARS campaign against the Dnipro crossings
9 Jul – 14 Nov 2022 · right-bank Kherson Oblast and the edge of Mykolaiv Oblast, Ukraine: the Dnipro from the Kakhovka dam to Kherson city, the Inhulets river, the Antonivskyi road and rail bridges

Accessed 2026-10-01. New sources are in `db/staging/sources/c04-kherson-antonivskyi.yaml`; sources already in the knowledge base are cited by their existing ids. The player is Ukraine. Russian claims are given as Ukrainian and Western sources report them. Place names use Ukrainian spellings.

## 1. Summary and historical outcome

After seizing Kherson in the first days of the invasion, Russia held a bridgehead on the right (west) bank of the Dnipro. It was supplied over three fixed crossings: the 1,366 m Antonivskyi road bridge, the Antonivskyi rail bridge 6 km upstream, and the road and rail crossing on the Kakhovka dam at Nova Kakhovka. A single bridge at Darivka linked the two halves of the bridgehead across the Inhulets [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:wikipedia-antonivka-road-bridge]. From 19 July 2022 Ukraine struck these crossings with US-supplied HIMARS. The Antonivskyi road bridge closed to freight on 20 July and to all traffic after the night of 26–27 July. The dam crossing was declared unfit on 10 August. By mid-August the UK assessed that Russia had no bridge left that could carry heavy equipment across the Dnipro [src:isw-2022-07-20-roca] [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca] [src:isw-2022-08-13-roca]. Russia adapted with pontoon ferries, barges, a barge bridge and civilian river craft, and it reinforced the bridgehead with much of its airborne force [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca]. Ukraine's ground offensive opened on 29 August from its Inhulets bridgehead and along the Mykolaiv line. It was heavily signalled, ran into prepared defences and was slow and costly [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll]. It broke the northern sector along the Dnipro between 1 and 4 October (Zolota Balka, Dudchany, Davydiv Brid) [src:isw-2022-10-03-roca] [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca]. The new theatre commander, Army General Sergei Surovikin, spoke of "difficult decisions" on 18 October. The Russian withdrawal across the river appears to have begun on about 21–22 October [src:isw-2022-10-19-roca] [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. On 9 November Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu and Surovikin announced the withdrawal on television [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca]. Russia claimed it had completed the withdrawal at 05:00 on 11 November, after blowing up the Antonivskyi road and rail bridges, the road section on the Kakhovka dam and the Darivka bridge [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca]. Ukrainian troops entered Kherson city the same day to cheering crowds on Freedom Square [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].

**The outcome the player must reproduce**
- **Russia withdraws across the Dnipro on 9–11 November 2022**, and Ukraine holds all of right-bank Kherson Oblast and the occupied part of Mykolaiv Oblast, with Kherson city entered on **11 November** [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. Ukraine does **not** cross the Dnipro; the offensive culminates at the river [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
- **Most of the Russian force escapes.** This is the contested part:
  - The Russian MoD claimed 30,000 personnel and 5,000 pieces of equipment were withdrawn and that nothing was left behind [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson] [src:isw-2022-11-12-roca]. Other Russian sources said 20,000 men and 3,500 pieces of equipment over three days [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca].
  - Russian withdrawal schedules published by Ukrainian journalist Andriy Tsaplienko list 42,370 troops, 253 tanks, almost 400 APCs and several thousand wheeled vehicles [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. They also show more than 6,000 pieces of equipment ferried across between 21 October and 7 November [src:unian-2022-urbanska-kherson-escape].
  - Ukrainian estimates of the force were 40,000 (Budanov in late October; Reznikov on 11 November) [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive] [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge].
  - Losses in the crossing itself were small and are not counted. There were abandoned helicopters and armoured vehicles, prisoners, and soldiers in civilian clothes [src:isw-2022-11-12-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].
- **Bridges down:** the central spans of the Antonivskyi road bridge, the Antonivskyi rail bridge, the Kakhovka dam road and the Darivka bridge were destroyed on 11 November. Satellite imagery showed at least seven bridges destroyed in 24 hours, four of them across the Dnipro [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].

**Key points and distances.**
- Kherson city is at 46.6401, 32.6144, about 8.7 km from the road bridge, 58 km from Nova Kakhovka (46.7583, 33.3567) and 18 km from Darivka.
- The two Antonivskyi bridges are 5.9 km apart, and the road bridge is 51 km from the dam.
- Davydiv Brid (47.2409, 33.1935) is on the Inhulets, 73 km from the road bridge.
- Dudchany (47.1914, 33.7803) is on the reservoir shore, 48 km north of Beryslav, and Arkhanhelske (47.4318, 33.4054) is 66 km from Beryslav.
- Snihurivka (47.0707, 32.7925) is 50 km from Kherson.
- The Ukrainian line at Posad-Pokrovske (46.8021, 32.2681) is 37.5 km from the road bridge and 84 km from the dam, against a GMLRS range of about 80 km. The dam was therefore at the edge of reach from the Mykolaiv side and closer from the north [src:osm-nominatim-2026-kherson-places] [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- In the Russian rear, Oleshky (46.6261, 32.7220) lies just across the road bridge, Hola Prystan is at 46.5320, 32.5345, Radensk (a depot) at 46.5511, 32.9256, the Brylivka railhead at 46.4202, 33.1411 and the Kalanchak repair yards at 46.2572, 33.2844 [src:osm-nominatim-2026-kherson-places] [src:isw-2022-10-13-roca].

**Terrain.**
- The ground is flat steppe farmland cut by shelterbelts. Kherson Oblast has about 30,000 km of windbreaks, and in Oleshky district the belts are 350–780 m apart [src:sudnik-2006-windbreaks-southern-ukraine] [src:ffm-oleshky-shelterbelts].
- The Inhulets runs north–south and was the de facto front from March. Ukraine's foothold on its east bank (Lozove, Andriivka, Bilohirka, Sukhyi Stavok) was the springboard of 29 August [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:isw-2022-08-13-roca].
- The northern sector runs along the Kakhovka reservoir on the T0403 road. One Russian milblogger complained that it was held at one man per 60 m of front, with some villages defended by 15 men [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca].
- Russia built three defence lines around Kherson city [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Wounded Ukrainians described tanks firing from concrete shelters [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll].
- **Water as a weapon.** Russian missiles on the Karachunivske dam raised the Inhulets by 1–2 m on 14–15 September [src:pravda-2022-kryvyi-rih-dam]. Russia lowered the Kakhovka reservoir from late October [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca].

## 4. Forces

**Russia: the right-bank grouping**
- **Core.** The 49th Combined Arms Army (Southern MD) was the main force: two motor-rifle brigades, the 205th and 34th, plus a tank battalion, about 10,000 men as of July [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:wikipedia-49th-combined-arms-army]. The 22nd Army Corps (Crimea) held the southern half [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca].
- **Summer reinforcement (VDV and others).**
  - From the 7th Guards Air Assault Division: the 247th Regiment at Kherson airport, the 108th at Blahodatne and the 56th.
  - The 11th and 31st Air Assault Brigades at Novovoskresenske and the 83rd at Dudchany.
  - The 331st Regiment of the 98th Division.
  - Elements of the 76th Division (still in the line in November) and of the 106th Division.
  - Eastern MD units of the 35th CAA (with its 165th Artillery Brigade) and the 5th and 36th CAA.
  - The 20th Guards MRD (8th CAA), the 126th Coastal Defence Brigade and the 810th Naval Infantry Brigade of the Black Sea Fleet.
  - Chechens, National Guard, BARS reservists, Wagner, and the DNR 109th, 113th, 125th and 127th Regiments.

  - From May, Russia moved about a dozen battalions from Donbas to Kherson [src:forbes-2022-axe-attacking-everywhere].
- **Command.** Grouping commanders are not named in the sources gathered. Army General Surovikin was theatre commander from October [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca].
- **Attrition, as claimed by Ukraine.**
  - The 247th Regiment (about 1,500) "no longer existed" by 9 September.
  - The 810th Brigade (about 2,500) lost 85%+ by 12 September.
  - The 126th Brigade (about 2,000) was "degraded, if not destroyed" by 4 October; its men complained of one BTR per 80 soldiers.
  - Ukraine claimed 2,500 Russians killed and 5,000 wounded from 29 August to 29 September.
  - The Russian MoD admitted 1,200–1,360 killed and wounded.

  Sources: [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive] [src:wikipedia-810th-naval-infantry-brigade]
- **Change over the window.** Mobilised men reinforced the 205th Brigade and 810th Brigade from October [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca]. Officers were moved across first, leaving newly mobilised men as the rearguard [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca].

**Ukraine: Operational Command South**
- **Command.** Maj. Gen. Andrii Kovalchuk commanded at first; Brig. Gen. Oleksandr Tarnavskyi took over in October. The "Kakhovka" operational group fought in the northern sector [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
- **Strength.** About three dozen army, marine and territorial battalions, perhaps 20,000 combat troops [src:forbes-2022-axe-attacking-everywhere].
- **Brigades named in sources.**
  - 28th Mechanized: Oleksandrivka and Posad-Pokrovske sector, then Kyselivka on 10 Nov and Kherson on 11–12 Nov [src:wikipedia-28th-mechanized-brigade] [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
  - 59th Motorized and 63rd Mechanized: named by Zelensky with the 28th as liberators of Kherson [src:kyivpost-2022-60-settlements] [src:wikipedia-63rd-mechanized-brigade].
  - 57th Motorized: Velyke Artakove, Bila Krynytsia, Andriivka and Sukhyi Stavok [src:wikipedia-57th-motorized-brigade].
  - 60th Mechanized: Ivanivka in July, then one battalion with one of the 128th's in the October drive on Zolota Balka, Dudchany and Mylove. It had had no break since February [src:wikipedia-60th-mechanized-brigade].
  - 128th Mountain Assault: heavy losses in the first days, then Myroliubivka on 3 Oct [src:wikipedia-128th-mountain-assault-brigade].
  - 35th and 36th Marine: the push south from the Inhulets, Bruskynske, and Davydiv Brid on 4 Oct [src:forbes-2022-axe-attacking-everywhere] [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive] [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca].
  - 46th Airmobile: reported by Russian sources near Ishchenka [src:isw-2022-10-13-roca].
  - 131st Reconnaissance Battalion: Snihurivka [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
  - Special Operations Forces, the GUR, National Guard and partisans [src:kyivpost-2022-60-settlements] [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge].
- **Long-range fires.** Ukraine had 4 HIMARS by 24 June and 8 by mid-July [src:kyivindependent-2022-himars-arrive]. Four more arrived on 1 August together with German MARS II [src:pravda-2022-reznikov-four-himars]. By 8 September the US had sent 16 launchers and "thousands" of GMLRS rockets [src:defenseone-2022-tucker-milley-himars], and British M270s were in service from July [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine]. No source splits the launchers between the Kherson and Kharkiv fronts.
- **Losses.** Ukraine published no figures.
  - Wounded soldiers interviewed by the Washington Post spoke of units with almost all 120 men wounded, 7 killed and 20 wounded out of 100, and 16 of 32 wounded. One said "We lost five people for every one they did" [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll].
  - RUSI counts the Kherson push, with the Donbas defence, among the two main sources of the roughly 30,000 Ukrainian killed and missing in 2022 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
  - Russia claimed 9,500 Ukrainian killed and wounded [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].

## 5. Systems and tactics in use

- **HIMARS/GMLRS against bridges.** GMLRS reaches about 80 km [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons], with a CEP "less than a meter" [src:militarnyi-2023-kozatskyi-himars-year]. Ukraine fired small salvoes at the same aim point: 8 rockets on 20 Jul, 7 at Darivka and the dam about 22 Jul, and 8 + 2 on 26–27 Jul. This cratered the deck rather than dropping spans, and the strikes were repeated whenever repairs began [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca] [src:isw-2022-08-25-roca]. TWZ reports that Ukraine deliberately hit only one small segment of the road bridge so that it could be repaired after liberation [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge]. Russian claims of interceptions (10 on 27 Jul, 44 on 30 Jul) are not borne out by footage [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca] [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca].
- **HIMARS against depots, HQs and trains.** Strikes hit the Kherson base on 10 Jul, the Nova Kakhovka depots, Radensk, the Brylivka train on 30 Jul, Chornobaivka, VDV regimental command posts on 1 Sep, and repair bases [src:isw-2022-07-12-roca] [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive] [src:isw-2022-07-31-roca] [src:isw-2022-09-01-roca] [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca]. 8–12 launchers wrecked most Russian depots in the south and Donbas within weeks, and depots moved beyond about 85 km [src:kyivindependent-2022-ponomarenko-himars]. The US counted 400+ HIMARS targets by 8 Sep [src:defenseone-2022-tucker-milley-himars]. After the strikes Russia could no longer stockpile ammunition beside its guns [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Supporting precision.** Ukraine reportedly also used Excalibur and possibly Vulcano 155 mm rounds against crossings and depots [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal], and HARM against air defence (Russia claimed HARMs shot down over the bridge on 1 Sep) [src:forbes-2022-axe-attacking-everywhere] [src:isw-2022-09-01-roca].
- **Russian counters to interdiction.**
  - Radar reflectors on the bridges [src:isw-2022-07-31-roca].
  - Rapid deck repair, which was struck again each time [src:isw-2022-08-25-roca].
  - Pontoon ferries manoeuvred by river boats at up to six sites [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far].
  - Repaired barges, then a barge bridge, then civilian ferries and barges commandeered from Kherson port, which were hard to target [src:isw-2022-08-21-roca] [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca] [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal].
  - A fuel pipeline along the rail bridge [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal].
  - Moving logistics deeper, to repair yards at Kalanchak and a new base about 70 km southeast of Kherson [src:isw-2022-10-13-roca] [src:isw-2022-11-12-roca].

  Ferries were inefficient: about 100 vehicles were seen moving north by 25 Aug [src:isw-2022-08-29-roca]. Hitting a ferry has a shorter-lived effect than closing a bridge, so ISW dates the systematic ferry strikes to the opening of the ground offensive on 29 August [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca]. Bridging columns had been hit as early as 29 July [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far].
- **Russian defence of the bridgehead.** Russia relied on Orlan-10 UAVs spotting from more than 1 km up, counter-battery radar, tanks in concrete shelters, mortars that answered three Ukrainian rounds with 20 in one soldier's account, EW that hijacked Ukrainian drones, and direction-finding of phones [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll]. ATGM ambushes and artillery killing areas inside three prepared lines completed the defence [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Water.** Russia struck the Karachunivske dam to flood the Inhulets [src:pravda-2022-kryvyi-rih-dam] and drew down the Kakhovka reservoir while warning of a Ukrainian "false flag" on the dam [src:isw-2022-10-19-roca] [src:isw-2022-10-22-roca]. Ukraine considered using HIMARS on the dam to raise the river as a "last resort" and did not do so [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
- **Demolitions in retreat.** Russia blew bridges near Snihurivka on 9 Nov, and the four main bridges, the TV tower, the heat and power plant and other utilities on 11 Nov. Mines and booby traps were left in the city [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca] [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].

## 6. Set pieces

**The night of 26–27 July: "cheese".** At about 22:55, eight rockets hit the Antonivskyi road bridge and two hit the rail bridge 6 km upstream [src:wikipedia-antonivka-road-bridge] [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca]. Russian air defence lights up only after the impacts. The next morning the deck is holed across its full width, and the occupation administration closes the bridge to all traffic [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca]. A Ukrainian newspaper calls it "cheese" [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far]. The weather is clear and the night warm [src:openmeteo-era5-kherson-2022]. A diorama can show the queue of trucks at the Oleshky end.

**The ferry war at Lvove and Kozatske.** Pontoon ferries shuttle under the Kakhovka dam and at Lvove, 16 km downstream. Each is a moving target that has to be found and timed. Ukraine strikes them from 29 August, and Russia rebuilds them [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far] [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca] [src:isw-2022-09-02-roca]. In October a Luhansk soldier films himself on a loaded ferry heading for the Nova Kakhovka canal mouth, and RFE/RL geolocates it [src:rferl-2022-krutov-kherson-ferries]. An intercepted Russian officer complains of "12 cars… transported by pontoon" lost to the river bottom [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far].

**29 August: out of the Inhulets bridgehead.** After weeks of strikes Ukraine attacks on several axes. The forcibly mobilised DNR 109th Regiment flees Arkhanhelske, and Ukrainian units push about 6 km from the bridgehead toward Sukhyi Stavok [src:isw-2022-08-29-roca] [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca]. The cost is heavy. Within days Zakarpattia mourns the 128th Brigade's dead, and hospitals in the south fill with men wounded by cluster munitions and mortars [src:wikipedia-128th-mountain-assault-brigade] [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll].

**1–4 October: the Dudchany collapse.** A battalion each of the 128th and 60th Brigades breaks through at Zolota Balka and rolls south along the reservoir road [src:wikipedia-60th-mechanized-brigade] [src:isw-2022-10-03-roca]. The 35th Marines cross the Inhulets and take Davydiv Brid. About 20 villages are abandoned in a day [src:wikipedia-35th-marine-brigade] [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca].

**9 November: the televised retreat.** In a staged meeting Surovikin recommends defending "along the barrier line of the Dnipro River", and Shoigu agrees [src:euromaidanpress-2022-petryk-withdrawal-announced] [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca]. The same day the occupation deputy head Stremousov dies in a car crash [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca].

**The last crossing, 10–11 November.** Russian infantry walk over the pontoon bridge beside the Antonivskyi bridge in the dark [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. Then the bridge's central spans go into the river, along with the rail bridge, the dam road and Darivka [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge]. Some stragglers are told to "change into civilian clothing" and get out any way they can, and some drown trying to swim [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].

**11 November: Freedom Square.** Ukrainian troops drive in along the M14 and T1501. Crowds chant "Glory to the ZSU" in Freedom Square, and a female soldier is lifted onto residents' shoulders [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. At night residents dance around a bonfire singing "Chervona Kalyna" [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].

## 7. Decision points

1. **Choosing the axis (summer 2022, Ukrainian General Staff with US and UK planners).** The most attractive option was a thrust from Zaporizhzhia to Melitopol that would also cut the Kherson grouping's supply. It was judged too risky against the main Russian concentration, and allied wargames reportedly showed Ukraine lacked the means. Ukraine chose Kherson, where the river would protect its gains [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
2. **Signalling the offensive (Kyiv, June–August).** Ukraine announced the Kherson push for months because it needed a visible "win" for its partners. RUSI judges that this cost Ukraine surprise [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Commentators such as Phillips O'Brien and ISW argue that it drew Russian reserves south, onto the far side of bridges under fire, and opened Kharkiv [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
3. **Russia reinforces the bridgehead (summer, Russian command).** Russia sent its VDV "fire brigade" and about a dozen battalions from Donbas across a river it could not supply reliably [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:forbes-2022-axe-attacking-everywhere]. This hollowed out the Kharkiv front, where the Russian line collapsed [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
4. **Timing: Kherson first, on 29 August (Zelensky).** The plan was reportedly Kharkiv on about 4 September and Kherson on about 18 September, after two weeks of shelling. Zelensky moved Kherson forward for political effect before the UN General Assembly, and a US general warned that this put the operation at risk [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
5. **The dam option (Kovalchuk).** Kovalchuk's plan was to cut the bridgehead in two and trap about 25,000 Russians. He considered striking the Kakhovka dam to raise the river, kept it as a "last resort" and did not use it [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
6. **No retreat (Putin, late September).** Russian commanders reportedly asked to fall back to more defensible positions, and Putin forbade giving up Kherson city [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
7. **Change of Ukrainian commander (October).** Kyiv replaced Kovalchuk with Tarnavskyi without announcing it, judging the advance too slow. US generals had pressed for a faster tempo. A report that UK Defence Secretary Ben Wallace ordered the dismissal is denied by Wallace [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
8. **Withdraw, and when (Surovikin, October–November).** From 18 October Surovikin prepared the public for "difficult decisions" [src:isw-2022-10-19-roca]. The captured schedule shows a phased pull-out from 21 October, under cover of the civilian "evacuation" [src:unian-2022-urbanska-kherson-escape] [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. US intelligence reported that Russian officials discussed holding the announcement until after the 8 November US midterms [src:babel-2022-yarmolenko-midterms-kherson]. ISW judged that the withdrawal was not a trap [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca].
9. **How hard to pursue (Ukrainian command, 9–11 November).** Ukraine advanced cautiously, wary of a trap and of mines [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. Miller argues that the Russian army "got away" unharassed, and that this cost Ukraine a bigger defeat for Russia [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. ISW instead saw Ukraine making the expected gains against a withdrawal in good order [src:isw-2022-11-10-roca].
10. **Stopping at the river (November).** Ukraine did not try to cross the Dnipro, reportedly because it was short of ammunition [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive]. RUSI later notes that a force large enough to matter on the left bank could not be supported across the river (see Krynky) [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

## 9. Russia's side as reported

- **The rationale.** Surovikin said Kherson and nearby settlements could not be supplied and that civilians were in danger from Ukrainian fire. He called defending "along the barrier line of the Dnipro" a hard decision that would "preserve the lives of our soldiers", and said half the withdrawn troops would be redeployed elsewhere [src:euromaidanpress-2022-petryk-withdrawal-announced] [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. Russian sources themselves called the withdrawal the result of systematic Ukrainian strikes on supply arteries [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca].
- **Claims of success.** The MoD claimed the withdrawal was complete by 05:00 on 11 November with no losses and "not a single piece" of equipment left behind (30,000 men and 5,000 pieces), which milbloggers openly disputed [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson]. It claimed mass interceptions of HIMARS and HARM [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca] [src:isw-2022-09-01-roca]. On 29–30 August it claimed it had defeated the counteroffensive with 1,200 Ukrainians killed [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca], and it later claimed 9,500 Ukrainian casualties [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
- **Information preparation.** From 18–19 October Surovikin and Saldo warned of a Ukrainian strike on the Kakhovka dam. ISW read this as cover for a possible Russian demolition and for the "evacuation", in fact the forced removal, of civilians [src:isw-2022-10-19-roca]. Occupation officials put the civilian movement at 50,000–60,000 people, and later reports say at least 70,000 were moved [src:rferl-2022-krutov-kherson-ferries] [src:wikipedia-liberation-of-kherson].
- **Reaction.** Most milbloggers, Prigozhin and Kadyrov backed Surovikin at first. Within days nationalists such as Dugin attacked Putin directly, and Wagner-linked channels spoke of a "sell-out" [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca] [src:isw-2022-11-12-roca]. One soldier's post: the last order in his unit was to "change into civilian clothing" and leave [src:twz-2022-newdick-antonivskyi-bridge]. The prominent milblogger Rybar said Russian forces destroyed the Antonivskyi bridge [src:wikipedia-antonivka-road-bridge].
- **Admitted losses.** Kadyrov acknowledged 23 killed and 58 wounded [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive]. Men of the 126th Brigade said they had been "crushed" [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].

## 10. Game/sim relevance

- **Interdiction as the main lever.** Give each crossing a capacity state (open, light vehicles only, closed, destroyed) and a repair rate. HIMARS salvoes of about 8 rockets degrade a bridge for days or weeks, and repairs invite re-strikes [src:isw-2022-07-20-roca] [src:isw-2022-07-27-roca] [src:isw-2022-08-25-roca]. Model ferries and barges as low-capacity, mobile, regenerating crossings that must be re-found each time. They are worth striking mainly when the ground offensive is active [src:isw-2022-08-30-roca].
- **Scarce launchers.** About 16 US launchers plus M270/MARS were shared across the whole front [src:defenseone-2022-tucker-milley-himars] [src:pravda-2022-reznikov-four-himars]. Every salvo sent to Kherson is one not sent to Kharkiv. No source quantifies the GMLRS stock, so treat it as a tunable parameter.
- **The Russian supply meter.** Throughput should fall but never reach zero. Kherson "did not starve", and Russia fought until withdrawal [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. The historical script: bridges closed by mid-August, the right bank untenable by late October.
- **Ground pacing.** Expect a slow and costly August–September (about 1,440 km² of net gain by 7 November in our window), a sudden northern collapse on 1–4 October, and a 3-day Russian collapse to the river on 9–11 November (about 4,570 km²) [src:deepstate-api-history] [src:isw-2022-10-04-roca]. Losses should be steep against prepared lines, with drones and counter-battery working against the attacker [src:stripes-2022-hudson-kherson-toll].
- **The pinning effect.** Pressure at Kherson drew Russian reserves south and so opened Kharkiv [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The debrief can link to chapter 6.
- **Win band.** Ukraine reaches the Dnipro along the whole right bank by about 11–12 November, Kherson city is entered on 11 November, and Russia withdraws 20,000–42,000 men and most of its heavy equipment. Bonus scoring for Russian equipment caught in the crossing should stay modest, because the historical haul was small [src:isw-2022-11-11-roca] [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal]. The bridges always end up dropped by Russia.
- **Water as a hazard.** Inhulets flooding (14 Sep) and the drawdown of the Kakhovka reservoir are scripted events, and striking the dam should exist as a forbidden "last resort" option [src:pravda-2022-kryvyi-rih-dam] [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].

## 11. Open questions/gaps

- **Ammunition and launchers in the south.** No source gives the number of GMLRS rockets fired at the Kherson crossings or how many launchers were allotted to OC South.
- **Date of the first bridge strike.** ISW reports 19 July [src:isw-2022-07-19-roca], while Miller gives 20 July in one article and implies 19 July in the other [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:wavellroom-2022-miller-river-too-far].
- **Was the road bridge ever reopened?** Wikipedia says repairs were completed by 23 August. Satellite imagery two days later showed 16 holes and ferries in use [src:wikipedia-antonivka-road-bridge] [src:isw-2022-08-29-roca].
- **VDV composition.** Miller says the whole VDV except the 106th Division was on the right bank, while ISW names the 106th among the withdrawing units [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:isw-2022-11-09-roca]. The ORBAT figures come from open sources and Ukrainian claims, and are partly unverified.
- **Size of the Russian force and of the withdrawal.** Estimates range from 20,000 to 42,370 (see section 1).
- **Equipment losses.** There is no Kherson-specific Oryx-type count of equipment destroyed or abandoned in July–November, or of the trophies taken in November.
- **Ukrainian losses.** Ukraine has never published figures. Only unit-level anecdotes and Russian claims exist.
- **Date of the strike on the 247th Regiment and Chornobaivka.** The sources give 22 or 23 August [src:wikipedia-2022-kherson-counteroffensive].
- **Did weather force the withdrawal?** Miller argues that winter ice would have stopped ferries, but ERA5 shows no daily minimum below 0 °C through 15 November [src:wavellroom-2023-miller-kherson-withdrawal] [src:openmeteo-era5-kherson-2022]. Treat the freeze as a looming threat, not a fact of the window.
- **Paywalled sources not checked first-hand.** The Washington Post (29 Dec 2022) and NYT (Entous, 29 Mar 2025) accounts of the planning, Kovalchuk's dam test and his dismissal are cited only through Wikipedia's summary of them.
- **Coordinates.** The Darivka bridge itself was not geolocated; only the village centre is given. An Overpass query failed.
