# Azovstal: the defence of Mariupol and the steel plant
24 Feb – 20 May 2022 (epilogue to 2025): Mariupol, Donetsk Oblast, on the Sea of Azov; the Illich and Azovstal steel works, the port, and the Mi-8 air corridor from Dnipro.

Accessed 2026-10-01. New sources are in `db/staging/sources/c03-azovstal.yaml`, cited inline by source id in square brackets. The player side is Ukraine. Russian claims are given as Ukrainian and Western outlets report them.

## 1. Summary and historical outcome

Russian and DNR forces attacked Mariupol from the first days of the invasion. Russia claimed to have encircled the city by land and sea on 1 March, and ISW assessed it fully encircled as of 2 March [src:isw-2022-03-01-roca] [src:isw-2022-03-02-roca]. A garrison of about 3,500 (a Ukrainian estimate quoted by The Economist; Russia claimed more than 8,000) faced about 14,000 attackers [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. The garrison included the Azov Regiment, the 36th Marine Brigade, National Guard brigades, border guards and police [src:voa-2022-ap-mariupol-last-defenders]. A deliberate bombardment of civilian infrastructure followed: the maternity hospital (9 Mar), the drama theatre (16 Mar) and at least 10,284 people buried in mass graves [src:isw-2022-03-02-roca] [src:hrw-2024-beneath-the-rubble]. Russian forces cut the city in two from the centre to the coast on 10 April [src:isw-2022-04-10-roca]. The 36th Marine Brigade's pocket at the Illich steel works fell on 13 April, when part of the brigade broke through to Azovstal and its commander was captured [src:isw-2022-04-13-roca] [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi]. The port fell about 16 April [src:isw-2022-04-17-roca]. On 21 April Putin declared victory and ordered the plant blockaded rather than stormed [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca].

Inside, the defenders used the plant's Soviet-era tunnels and shelters: about 11 km² of plant above 24 km of tunnels [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal]. Seven Ukrainian Mi-8 missions (16 helicopters) flew in ammunition, Stingers, NLAWs, Starlink terminals and 72 Azov volunteers, and brought out wounded. Three helicopters were lost [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions] [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:militarnyi-2022-sapwood-helicopters-seven-times]. UN/ICRC convoys took civilians out between 30 April and 7 May [src:un-ukraine-2022-05-03-lubrani-azovstal] [src:isw-2022-05-07-roca]. Russian troops broke into the plant on 4 May [src:isw-2022-05-04-roca]. On 16 May the General Staff declared that the garrison had "fulfilled its combat mission" and ordered the commanders to save their men's lives [src:defenceua-2022-mariupol-garrison-mission]. By 20 May Russia claimed 2,439 had left the plant and surrendered [src:pravda-2022-mazurenko-2439-shoigu]. At least 50 prisoners, many of them from Azovstal, were killed in the Olenivka barracks explosion of 28–29 July 2022 [src:un-news-2023-olenivka-justice].

**The outcome the player must reproduce:**
- **The plant holds until the ordered "evacuation" of 16–20 May 2022.** Sources count the defence as 82 days [src:wikipedia-azovstal-iron-steel-works] or 83 days [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. There is no relief and no breakout. Ukraine's own position was that a military unblocking was impossible [src:militarnyi-2022-kushnikov-unblocking-mariupol].
- **Russian forces stay tied down.** The General Staff claimed "up to 17 BTGs (about 20,000 personnel)" were pinned, which thwarted a quick drive on Zaporizhzhia [src:defenceua-2022-mariupol-garrison-mission]. US officials said a dozen BTGs were still at Mariupol on 21 April and two on 9 May [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-09-roca]. Win band: hold the plant until mid-May while keeping 2 to about 12 BTGs there after the city falls.
- **The city falls in stages.** The Illich works fall on 13 Apr and the port about 16 Apr; Russia announces victory on 21 Apr [src:isw-2022-04-13-roca] [src:isw-2022-04-17-roca] [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca].
- **Civilians.** About 101 civilians leave Azovstal in the first UN/ICRC operation (29 Apr – 3 May) and 173 in the second (arriving 8 May). The plant is clear of civilians on 7 May [src:un-ukraine-2022-05-03-lubrani-azovstal] [src:pravda-2022-karlovskyi-173-civilians] [src:isw-2022-05-07-roca].
- **The air bridge.** Seven missions, 16 Mi-8s, 72 troops in, 64 wounded out, about 30 t delivered, and 3 helicopters lost [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions].
- **Prisoners: contested.** Russia claims 2,439 [src:pravda-2022-mazurenko-2439-shoigu]. Ukrainian officials later used "about 2,500" [src:kyivpost-2025-pehlman-azovstal-half-home]. The ICRC had registered only "hundreds" by 20 May [src:isw-2022-05-20-roca].

**The plant.** Azovstal was founded in 1930. It is a full-cycle works with coke ovens, a sinter plant, six blast furnaces and a steelmaking complex [src:wikipedia-azovstal-iron-steel-works]. Reported size: about 11 km² [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal] or 4 sq mi (about 10.4 km²) [src:npr-2022-sullivan-kakissis-azovstal]. The OSM outline measures about 10.3 km², spanning about 6.1 km N–S by 4.8 km E–W [src:osm-azovstal-works-way]. It lies on the coast in the Left-Bank district, across the river from downtown [src:npr-2022-sullivan-kakissis-azovstal] [src:osm-azovstal-works-way]. The M14 highway runs past the western entrance [src:isw-2022-05-13-roca]. Before the war it employed more than 10,000 people [src:npr-2022-sullivan-kakissis-azovstal].

**Underground.** The plant has about 24 km of tunnels, built after WWII, with maps posted in each workshop. Metinvest said the shelters could hold 4,000 people and had food stocks, ventilation, lighting, water and heat-resistant doors; the shelters were stocked for about three weeks [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal]. Evacuees called the tunnels a dark maze "like a big city" [src:npr-2022-sullivan-kakissis-azovstal]. Defenders moved, shot and communicated through them, and GAZ-66 trucks brought the wounded up from the tunnels to the helicopters [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue].

**Surface obstacles** (useful for the helicopter act): tall stacks that poked through the fog, power lines 30–40 m high on the sea approach, and vertical obstacles throughout the compound [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. A giant ore pile and rail tracks lay inside the compound [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters].

**Distance to friendly lines.** Hromov put Ukrainian units 150–200 km away on 11 May [src:militarnyi-2022-kushnikov-unblocking-mariupol]. The helicopters flew about 60 mi (about 97 km) over Russian-held territory after refuelling "right up on the line" [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue]. Biletskyi said "more than 100 kilometers" [src:rbc-2026-honcharova-serbov-interview]. In March, ISW reported up to 17 BTGs assembled as far as Rozivka, about 47 km NW of the city [src:isw-2022-03-09-roca].

The five "unit leaders of the defence of Azovstal" freed on 21 Sep 2022 were Prokopenko, Volynskyi, Palamar, Denys Shleha and Oleh Khomenko [src:pravda-2022-swap-215-medvedchuk].

**Strength over time.**
- **March.** About 3,500 (Ukrainian estimate quoted by The Economist, 21 Mar). Wikipedia extrapolates up to 4,400; Russia claimed 8,000 or more [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].
- **8 April.** Pro-Russian sources claimed 3,000 were in the plant [src:isw-2022-04-08-roca].
- **18 April.** A Russian estimate gave 2,500 plus 400 foreigners at Azovstal; an outside expert said 500–800 holdouts city-wide [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].
- **23 April.** About 2,000 fighters and up to 1,000 civilians were in the plant [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal].
- **Additions.** About 500 marines from Illich, about 500 border guards and police from the port (claimed), and 72 Azov volunteers by air [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi] [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol] [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions].
- **Wounded.** More than 600 on 27 Apr [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi], and "about 600" with almost no medicine, water or food before the walk-out [src:pravda-2022-ru-mod-959-left-azovstal].
- **End.** About 2,500 captured [src:rferl-2025-peretiatko-azovstal-captivity].

**Equipment.** Small arms; NLAWs delivered by air and learned from the internet; Stingers; Starlink [src:pravda-2025-palamar-mariupol-interview] [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue]. Wikipedia also lists Javelins on the first flight [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. Ammunition ran short: a British volunteer gave lack of ammunition and supplies as the reason the marines surrendered at Illich on 12 Apr [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].

### Russia and the DNR
- **8th Combined Arms Army** (Lt Gen Andrei Mordvichev), incl. the 150th MRD (68th Tank Regiment, 102nd MR Regiment) [src:hrw-2024-mariupol-whos-responsible].
- **Black Sea Fleet**: 810th Naval Infantry Brigade and 382nd Marine Battalion. The 810th was still blockading the plant on 30 Apr [src:hrw-2024-mariupol-whos-responsible] [src:isw-2022-04-30-roca].
- **Spetsnaz**: 22nd and 346th brigades [src:hrw-2024-mariupol-whos-responsible].
- **Chechen forces**: the 141st Special Motorized Regiment and a Rosgvardia special-purpose police regiment [src:hrw-2024-mariupol-whos-responsible].
- **DNR 1st Army Corps**: Vostok Battalion, "Somalia" tank battalion, 9th Marine Regiment, Slovyansk Brigade, 107th Regiment, Kaskad [src:hrw-2024-mariupol-whos-responsible].
- **Overall siege commander** per Wikipedia: Mikhail Mizintsev [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].
- **Strength over time.** About 14,000 in March [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. The Ukrainian General Staff claimed up to 17 BTGs (about 20,000) were pinned [src:defenceua-2022-mariupol-garrison-mission]. US officials: a dozen BTGs on 21 Apr, two on 9 May [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-09-roca].
- **Air**: Tu-22M3 strikes from about 16 Apr [src:isw-2022-04-16-roca] [src:isw-2022-04-17-roca].
- **Losses**: Ukraine claims 6,500 killed [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].

## 5. Systems and tactics in use

- **Siege by infrastructure destruction.** Strikes on water, power and heat were meant to force capitulation without assault [src:isw-2022-03-02-roca]. HRW surveyed 4,884 damaged or destroyed buildings over 14 km², including 93% of high-rises [src:hrw-2024-beneath-the-rubble].
- **Heavy air bombardment of the plant.** Tu-22M3s [src:isw-2022-04-17-roca], multi-ton "bunker-busters" on the field hospital [src:isw-2022-04-28-roca], 38 strikes and 4 bomber flights in a single day [src:isw-2022-05-11-roca] and unguided "dumb bombs" [src:isw-2022-05-10-roca]. **FAB-3000:** there were Telegram photos of a FAB-3000 at a Tu-22M (6 Apr) and a Ukrainian warning of its use (16 Apr) [src:aerotime-2022-petrauskaite-fab-3000] [src:infobae-2022-fab-3000-mariupol]. Use is reported, not confirmed in the sources fetched here.
- **Other fires.** Thermobaric weapons [src:isw-2022-03-30-roca], naval gunfire [src:isw-2022-05-02-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-15-roca], thermite incendiaries [src:isw-2022-05-15-roca] and ATGMs against vehicles and people [src:ukrinform-2022-azov-atgm-evacuation-vehicle] [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters].
- **Ukrainian defence in depth underground.** Shelters and tunnels let the garrison survive bombardment and shift between positions [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue]. Russia answered by blockading the tunnel exits rather than clearing them [src:isw-2022-05-12-roca], and by assaults from the north and later along the M14 at the western entrance [src:isw-2022-04-19-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-13-roca].
- **The Mi-8 air bridge.** The profile was:
  - nap-of-the-earth flight at 10–15 m under night-vision goggles, 3 m in places [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:abc-2022-ap-mariupol-rescue-missions];
  - about 150 mph (220 km/h) [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue] [src:abc-2022-ap-mariupol-rescue-missions];
  - about 80 min each way, 42 of them over enemy ground [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters];
  - a refuelling point at the line on the way back, and fuel carried only to that point, to save weight [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters];
  - artillery fired a distraction at the planned front-line crossing [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters];
  - 10 minutes on the ground with engines running (12 and 15–20 minutes in two accounts) [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue];
  - an intelligence officer aboard each helicopter, and crews in civilian clothes to escape on foot if shot down [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue];
  - routes alternated between land and sea, once passing between two Russian ships [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue].
  Threats were Pantsir-S1 (briefed, no Buk), ship fire, a missile that failed to explode and MANPADS [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:abc-2022-ap-mariupol-rescue-missions] [src:rbc-2026-honcharova-serbov-interview]. Ukrainian counters were the Mi-24 decoy (which failed on mission 6), surprise, and never sending the same pilot twice [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. Russia's counter was to thicken air defences once the flights became public, which ended the missions [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions].
- **Information.** With no internet, the defenders printed A4 leaflets with front news to keep up morale [src:pravda-2025-palamar-mariupol-interview]. Outbound media (GoPro clips, Kozatskyi's photos) mattered politically [src:rbc-2026-honcharova-serbov-interview] [src:kyivindependent-2024-shulzhenko-kozatskyi].

## 6. Set pieces

1. **The drama theatre (16 Mar).** A theatre sheltering hundreds, with "children" painted on the square, is hit by two 500 kg bombs shortly after 10:00. The death toll is contested: at least a dozen (Amnesty), at least 15 (HRW), about 300 (Ukrainian officials) and about 600 (an AP investigation) [src:amnesty-2022-mariupol-theatre] [src:hrw-2024-beneath-the-rubble] [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. As a set piece it is a civilian-shelter event the player cannot prevent; it is part of the prelude.
2. **The first night run (21 Mar).** The first mission had been scrubbed on 19 Mar and men had phoned home to say it was off. Two Mi-8s lift off from Dnipro at 03:30 and refuel at the line. They cross 60 mi of occupied ground at treetop height, and one clips a tree. They land beside a tunnel mouth while Azov fires to cover them, take on about 16 wounded and are back by 07:30 [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue] [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. One pilot volunteered because his wife was a combat medic in the plant [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue].
3. **Fog over the sea (28 Mar).** On mission 3 the guns are stripped for 200 kg more medicine, and the crew is told the flight is "two missions with one helicopter each". Dawn breaks over the sea. The pilot finds the plant by its stacks through the fog, pops up over the 30–40 m power lines and dives in, then spends 12 minutes on the ground [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters].
4. **The Illich breakout (night 11–12 Apr).** The marines' pocket in the northern works is out of ammunition. Two groups break out: one north (Baraniuk, captured) and one to Azovstal (Volynskyi, about 500 men). Russia parades its claimed "1,026 prisoners" [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol] [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi] [src:isw-2022-04-13-roca].
5. **Blockade, not storm (21 Apr).** In the staged Putin–Shoigu meeting, the storm of the plant is called off in favour of starvation [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca]. This is a pacing beat: the map goes quiet and the bombing grows heavier.
6. **The field hospital (28 Apr).** Heavy bombs hit the plant's field hospital [src:isw-2022-04-28-roca]. Inside, amputations are done without painkillers and infections turn deadly [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:kyivindependent-2024-shulzhenko-kozatskyi].
7. **The convoy (29 Apr – 3 May).** A UN/ICRC convoy drives 230 km and civilians climb out of the bunkers. Firing resumes the moment the pause ends [src:un-ukraine-2022-05-01-ocha-azovstal] [src:un-ukraine-2022-05-03-lubrani-azovstal] [src:isw-2022-05-03-roca].
8. **"Orest's" light (10 and 20 May).** Kozatskyi's photos of bunkers and the wounded, including his own portrait in a shaft of light, go out from inside the plant. His last post before captivity reads: "I am thankful to Azovstal for shelter – the place of my death and my life" [src:kyivindependent-2024-shulzhenko-kozatskyi] [src:euronews-2022-kozatskyi-photos].
9. **The walk-out (16–20 May).** Before leaving, the defenders destroy phones, weapons and documents; then the wounded go to Novoazovsk and the rest to Olenivka [src:pravda-2025-palamar-mariupol-interview] [src:pravda-2022-tyshchenko-general-staff-garrison-mission].
10. **Epilogue: Olenivka (28–29 Jul).** The barracks explosion kills at least 50 prisoners. The UN later rules out a HIMARS strike [src:un-news-2023-olenivka-justice] [src:kyivpost-2023-afp-olenivka-not-himars]. This belongs in the debrief, not in play.

## 7. Decision points

1. **Launching the air bridge (Mar).** Budanov (GUR) devised a plan that a US aviation veteran called work for highly proficient crews only. Several pilots opted out once read in [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue]. Each flight needed the ground commander's final "all clear" (Prokopenko) [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. After the seventh mission GUR judged further flights too risky [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]. Hromov said deliveries stopped once news of them spread and Russia strengthened its air defences [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions].
2. **Flight-level choices** (sources describe these as real dilemmas):
   - payload against defensive guns (the guns were removed);
   - re-routing around updated air-defence positions, while keeping the supporting artillery in sync;
   - extra minutes on the ground to load every wounded man, at a cost in fuel;
   - the "single-ship mission" rule, under which no one turns back to save a downed partner.
   [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters]
3. **The breakout from Illich (11–12 Apr).** The 36th Marine Brigade split: one group went north to break the ring, the other went to link up with Azov. Prokopenko later called Baraniuk's breakout "uncoordinated" [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. ISW and Ukrainian officials confirmed that the link-up succeeded in part [src:isw-2022-04-13-roca] [src:isw-2022-04-14-roca].
4. **Russia: storm or starve (21 Apr – 4 May).** Putin halted the assaults to limit casualties [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca]. Ground attacks resumed on 25 Apr and became a full storm on 3–4 May, which ISW linked to the 9 May Victory Day deadline [src:isw-2022-04-25-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-04-roca].
5. **Civilian evacuation deals (late Apr – 7 May).** The UN/ICRC safe passage was agreed after Guterres visited Moscow and Kyiv [src:un-ukraine-2022-05-03-lubrani-azovstal]. Russia had first refused any pause until the defenders surrendered [src:isw-2022-04-22-roca].
6. **Relief, breakout or "extraction" (Apr – May).**
   - **Relief.** Defenders asked for an offensive towards them [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions]. Hromov and Maliar ruled out unblocking: 150–200 km of fortified ground and heavy losses [src:militarnyi-2022-kushnikov-unblocking-mariupol] [src:defenceua-2022-mariupol-garrison-mission].
   - **Extraction.** Volynskyi appealed to world leaders for a "Dunkirk" [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi]. Turkey offered to carry the defenders by ship from Berdiansk and intern them until the war's end. Russia would release only civilians [src:lb-2022-erdogan-azovstal-offer]. Ukraine separately proposed swapping the seriously wounded for Russian prisoners [src:lb-2022-erdogan-azovstal-offer].
   - **Breakout.** No source examined describes a planned breakout from Azovstal itself. Russian claims of captured "escapees" (6 May) are unconfirmed [src:isw-2022-05-06-roca].
7. **The order of 16 May.** The Supreme Military Command ordered the unit commanders to save their men. Ukraine framed it as an "evacuation" with later exchange; Russia called it a surrender [src:defenceua-2022-mariupol-garrison-mission] [src:pravda-2022-ru-mod-959-left-azovstal]. ISW judged that the Kremlin might have agreed to a conditional surrender to speed up its claim of control, and warned that it might refuse exchanges [src:isw-2022-05-17-roca].
8. **Exchanges (Sep 2022 onward).** The five commanders were freed on condition they stay in Turkey under Erdoğan's guarantees [src:pravda-2022-swap-215-medvedchuk]. They returned with Zelensky on 8 Jul 2023 [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi]. The slow return of Azov fighters remained contested in 2025: Prokopenko called a swap without Azov fighters "a disgrace" [src:kyivpost-2025-pehlman-azovstal-half-home] [src:rferl-2025-peretiatko-azovstal-captivity].

## 9. Russia's side as reported

- **Victory before the end.** Putin and Shoigu declared victory on 21 Apr while the plant still held. Putin framed the halt to assaults as a way to limit Russian casualties [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca]. ISW read the Kremlin as setting up a claim of control by 9 May, with propagandists brought into the city [src:isw-2022-05-04-roca].
- **Inflated numbers.** Shoigu claimed more than 8,000 Ukrainian troops at the time of encirclement [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol], 1,026 marines surrendered at Illich [src:isw-2022-04-13-roca], and 2,439 surrendered at Azovstal [src:pravda-2022-mazurenko-2439-shoigu]. ISW suggested Russia might overstate the totals, either to maximise exchange leverage or to avoid admitting a months-long siege against only "hundreds" [src:isw-2022-05-20-roca].
- **Framing.** Russia presented evacuated civilians as "hostages" freed [src:isw-2022-05-02-roca]. It said a downed helicopter had been sent to evacuate Azov commanders [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol]. Volodin said the defenders must be tried for war crimes and not exchanged [src:isw-2022-05-17-roca].
- **Domestic reaction.** Pro-Russian channels were angry about the negotiated deal [src:isw-2022-05-17-roca] and complained that Russian wounded were moved out of DNR hospitals for Ukrainian ones [src:isw-2022-05-19-roca].
- **Aftermath.** Pushilin announced that Azovstal would be demolished and Mariupol made a "resort city" [src:isw-2022-05-18-roca]. Russia blamed Olenivka on HIMARS, which the UN rejected [src:kyivpost-2023-afp-olenivka-not-himars]. Russian courts sentenced 12 Azov members to 13–23 years in March 2025 [src:rferl-2025-peretiatko-azovstal-captivity].
- **Russian losses.** Paly, deputy commander of the Black Sea Fleet, was confirmed killed [src:isw-2022-03-20-roca]. A Ukrainian claim names the 150th MRD commander, Maj Gen Mityaev, as killed [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].

## 10. Game/sim relevance

- **Win band.** The plant holds to the scripted 16 May order, which the player cannot bring forward. The score rises with each day Russian BTGs stay committed: about 12 on 21 Apr, falling to 2 by 9 May [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-09-roca]. A loss is an early collapse, or a premature attempt at relief or breakout. The sources treat both relief and breakout as impossible [src:militarnyi-2022-kushnikov-unblocking-mariupol].
- **Three-phase pacing.**
  - City prelude (compressed, 1 Mar – 16 Apr): the pockets shrink, Illich falls and the port falls.
  - Siege (17 Apr – 15 May): bombardment, rationing, the evacuations.
  - Walk-out (16–20 May): a short procedural coda.
- **Resource model.** Ammunition, medicine, food, water and the wounded count (about 600) are the main meters [src:isw-2022-04-27-roca] [src:wikipedia-serhii-volynskyi]. The shelters started with about three weeks of stocks [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal]. Each helicopter sortie adds supplies (more than 30 t over 16 sorties) and removes wounded (64 in total) [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters].
- **The air-bridge act** is a run-planning minigame:
  - route over land or sea, the frontline crossing time synchronised with diversionary artillery, payload against weapons, and minutes on the ground against fuel;
  - a detection or alertness meter rises with each flown approach, as surprise is lost and air defences thicken [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters] [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions];
  - the historical script is 7 missions (21 Mar – about 5 Apr), the first loss on mission 5 and a double loss on mission 7.
- **Civilian layer.** Civilians in the bunkers (up to about 1,000 claimed) are a liability that only UN/ICRC windows can resolve. Firing resumes as each window ends [src:abc-2022-ozdemir-inside-azovstal] [src:isw-2022-05-03-roca].
- **Russian AI behaviour.** Starve and blockade (21 Apr), then storm against the 9 May deadline (3–13 May), with tunnel exits blocked (12 May) [src:isw-2022-04-21-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-04-roca] [src:isw-2022-05-12-roca].
- **Debrief.** Show the contested numbers side by side: prisoners (2,439 RU, about 2,500 UA, ICRC "hundreds" as of 20 May), Olenivka dead (50 to 57), theatre dead (12 to 600). Then show the long tail of exchanges: 1,279 returned by May 2025 [src:kyivpost-2025-pehlman-azovstal-half-home].

## 11. Open questions/gaps

- **Air-bridge dates.** Only mission 1 (21 Mar), mission 3 (28 Mar) and the last flight (5 Apr, per Serbov) are dated. Missions 2, 4, 5 and 6 are not. TWZ's "around the middle of May" for the last sorties conflicts with the 5 Apr account and with GUR's figure of seven missions [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue] [src:rbc-2026-honcharova-serbov-interview].
- **Helicopter losses.**
  - Counts differ: 2 shot down plus a rescue helicopter (Budanov) [src:twz-2022-altman-mariupol-helicopter-missions]; 1 destroyed and 2 "missing" officially (Flint) [src:twz-2022-altman-flint-helicopter-rescue]. Time describes two Mi-8s damaged on mission 6 that crash-landed on friendly ground [src:time-2024-spencer-collins-mariupol-helicopters].
  - Crew deaths are unknown: Zelensky said 90% of the pilots did not return [src:pravda-2022-mazurenko-azovstal-weapons-delivery], while Time documents no crew deaths.
  - Wikipedia's "six helicopters shot down overall" is unsourced here [src:wikipedia-siege-of-mariupol].
- **Distance error.** Time's "82 km" from Dnipro to Mariupol is wrong; the straight-line distance is about 233 km [src:osm-nominatim-c03-places].
- **FAB-3000.** Use against Azovstal is reported but not confirmed by Ukrainian or Western sources fetched for this dossier.
- **Garrison numbers.** The strength at Azovstal by date, killed in action, and the size of each unit are not documented in Ukrainian primary sources reviewed here.
- **Civilians.** The number of civilians in the plant is uncertain ("hundreds" to about 1,000). The total civilians evacuated from Azovstal across both operations is unclear; the UP total of about 567 includes people from the wider city.
- **Russian losses** are only claimed (6,500 killed, per Ukraine).
- **Olenivka toll:** 50+ (UN, 2023), 51 (UN, Oct 2023), 54 (RFE/RL), 57 (Kyiv Independent) [src:un-news-2023-olenivka-justice] [src:kyivpost-2023-afp-olenivka-not-himars] [src:rferl-2025-peretiatko-azovstal-captivity] [src:kyivindependent-2024-shulzhenko-kozatskyi].
- **Drone footage.** Azov drone footage of the burning plant (early May) circulated widely, but no original upload or dated, stable URL was confirmed for this dossier.
- **Geography.** The helicopter landing spots, the bunker locations and the internal layout (which shops, which tunnels) are not mapped in public sources found.
- **Weather.** Values are reanalysis, not station records.
- **Not yet consulted.** Ukrainian parliamentary or journalistic investigations of why the city was encircled so fast (raised by Serbov) [src:rbc-2026-honcharova-serbov-interview].
