# 03 — Fires and air: artillery, glide bombs, helicopters, aviation, missiles, air defence

Accessed 2026-09-28. Sources are in `sources/03-fires-air.yaml`, cited inline as `[src:<id>]`. The player side is Ukraine. Russian capabilities are described as reported by Ukrainian and Western sources.

## 1. Overview

The fires war went through three overlapping regimes.

1. **Massed tube and rocket artillery (2022).** Russia fired about 20,000 rounds a day in Donbas, with peaks above 32,000. Ukraine rarely fired more than 6,000, and Russia held a 10–12:1 advantage by June 2022 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. HIMARS/M270 GMLRS from late June 2022 broke the Russian model, which depended on railheads and big depots. RUSI calls their arrival "the point where the Russian offensive on Donbas ended" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
2. **Precision and mass without air superiority (2023–24).** Russian shells ran short (about 12M rounds in 2022, trending to about 7M in 2023) [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Russia moved to Krasnopol laser-guided shells, Lancet for counter-battery, and above all mass UMPK glide bombs. These let the VKS (Russian Aerospace Forces) strike the front from 30–90 km without entering Ukrainian SAM coverage [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russian EW (electronic warfare) cut Ukraine's GPS-guided Excalibur hit rate from more than 50% to 6–7% [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming]. Ukraine rationed shells: the ratio was 1:10 in February 2024 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio].
3. **The drone-dominated kill zone and a deep-strike duel (2024–26).** Tactical UAVs now cause 60–70% of damaged and destroyed Russian systems [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year], and Latvian intelligence puts drones at 70–80% of all killed and wounded on both sides by early 2026 [src:politico-2026-hartog-latvian-drones]. Artillery survives by dispersing and digging in, and it works as a partner to drones rather than the main killer. The Ukrainian shell ratio recovered to about 1:1.8 by July 2025 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio], helped by the Czech initiative [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative]. Russian KAB use climbed from about 5,000 to almost 8,800 a month during 2026 [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs] [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-kabs-us-campaigns], and Iskander-M output to 60–70 a month [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. Russia launched 100–200 ballistic missiles and up to about 8,000 Shahed/Geran drones a month [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity] [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-july-strikes]. As PAC-3 ran short, partly because of the 2026 US–Iran war, Ukraine's ballistic-missile intercept rate collapsed to 10–15% [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks] [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot]. Ukraine answered with its own long-range weapons, above all mass mid-range and long-range drones, plus a small number of Flamingo and Neptune missiles.

**The air war stayed contested.** Since early March 2022, Ukrainian ground-based air defence has kept Russian crewed aircraft out of Ukrainian-held airspace except at very low altitude [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war]. Russia's layered IADS (integrated air defence system) keeps Ukraine's small air force (MiG-29, Su-27, Su-24M, Su-25, and from 2024–25 F-16 and Mirage 2000) to stand-off strikes and anti-missile and anti-drone patrols. Neither side has air superiority. Glide bombs are Russia's workaround: "Glide bombs gave the VKS teeth without the need to first achieve air superiority" [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

## 2. Per-era notes

### e1-invasion (Feb–Apr 2022)
- **The opening strike campaign fails to blind Ukraine.** About 75% of static Ukrainian SAM sites were engaged in the first 48 h. Mobile systems survived through dispersal and deception: dummy sites, printed "damage" sheets over hangars, and false radio reports of destroyed sites. Russian BDA (battle damage assessment) was weak [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. From early March the VKS lost the ability to operate over Ukrainian-held territory except at very low altitude [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war].
- **Pre-war Ukrainian air defence** had 35 S-300PS/PT divisions, 3 S-300V, 9 S-125 and 15 Buk-M1, with MANPADS (mostly modernised Igla-1) spread through units. SAMs forced aircraft down low, into the MANPADS envelope [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Missiles.** More than 200 Iskander strikes and about 180 Kh-22/32 and Kh-55/555 launches from February to early May 2022. By early April Russia had moved from salvos to single shots, probably because of stock shortages. Iskander-M output was estimated at about 6 a month. Russia fell back on Tochka-U, Bal and Bastion, with more civilian damage as a result [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Helicopters.** Russian air-assault helicopters reached Hostomel low along the Dnipro, but MANPADS downed 2 in the first wave [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Of the 34 helicopters in that assault Russia lost about 17%, and Oryx confirms 6 (4 Ka-52, 1 Mi-28, 1 Mi-8) [src:pravda-2026-dotsenko-helicopters-drones]. Ka-52 and Mi-28N crews began pitch-up lob firing of unguided S-8 rockets. This buys about 6–7 km of extra range and keeps the helicopter out of Stinger range, at the cost of very wide dispersion [src:twz-2022-newdick-helicopter-lob]. Ukrainian Mi-24 and Mi-8 crews adopted the same technique: they run in low, pitch up 15–30°, ripple S-8 or S-13 rockets at a pre-calculated area and break away firing flares. The salvo forces infantry to take cover but rarely kills armour or dug-in positions [src:pravda-2026-dotsenko-helicopters-drones].
- **Ukrainian aviation.** Su-24M, Su-25, Mi-24 and Mi-8 struck Russian convoys moving without air cover. MiG-29 pairs flew defensive counter-air, often outnumbered 6:2. Airspace was deconflicted from SAMs first by time, then by area [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Sensor-to-shooter.** Russia relied on pre-planned strikes and presumed success. Ukraine's pre-war Kropyva digital fire-control software had cut the time to open counter-battery fire by 90% and the time to engage unplanned targets by two-thirds [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

### e2-donbas-artillery (May–Aug 2022)
- **Russian fire superiority.** Russia averaged about 20,000 rounds a day, peaking above 32,000. More than 1,100 tube guns, in groups of 80–90 per brigade-sized grouping, gave a 12:1 advantage in May–June. A Ukrainian company position with about 3 km of frontage could take 6,000 rounds a day. Ukraine rarely exceeded 6,000 rounds a day in total, being short of both barrels and shells [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Meatgrinder gives 20,000–60,000 rounds a day and about 12M for 2022 as a whole [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **MLRS.** Uragan and Smerch were used at maximum range (35–70 km) and even against point targets. Russia fielded up to 10 EW complexes per 20 km of front, which degraded Ukrainian precision fires [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Russian kill chain.** With the unit's own UAV, fires landed 3–5 min after detection. Through a fire-control HQ it took 20–30 min, and about 48 h at operational level. Guns stood 100–150 m apart and rarely displaced unless fired upon [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **UAV spotters were used up fast.** About 90% of Ukrainian UAVs were lost, a quadcopter lasted about 3 flights and a fixed-wing about 6, and only about a third of missions succeeded. The Orlan-10 stood out on the Russian side [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **HIMARS.** From late June, GMLRS systematically hit depots that had been mapped from Russian dependence on rail. RUSI marks this as the end of the Russian Donbas offensive [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. After the July 2022 strikes on C2 and depots, Russia could no longer stockpile ammunition beside its guns [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].

### e3-counteroffensives-22 (Sep–Nov 2022)
- After the HIMARS strikes, Russia could no longer mass ammunition beside its guns and had to haul it from depots further back [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. This over-stretched logistics is the backdrop to Ukraine's Kharkiv and Kherson offensives (our inference; not a direct claim of the source).
- **The Shahed campaign begins.** Russia sent hundreds of Iranian Shahed-136 against substations, together with cruise and ballistic missiles. RUSI urged more MANPADS, radar-guided AA guns and Western fighters as affordable counters [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war].

### e4-bakhmut (Dec 2022–May 2023)
- **Russian shell economy.** In Q1 2023 Russia fired 12,000–38,000 rounds a day, and days above 24,000 became rare. Use shifted from 152 mm guns to 120 mm mortars. Annual consumption was trending to about 7M against Ukrainian estimates of about 2.5M a year in Russian production [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Russian reconnaissance-fire circuit (the Russian term for the kill chain).** Each axis commander keeps Orlan-10 orbits overhead, and the artillery brigade flies its own complex. Engagement takes about 3–5 min from UAV detection and 20–30 min from EW detection. The Strelets system links sensors to digital fire control. Krasnopol laser-guided shells and Lancet loitering munitions do counter-battery work and hunt guns [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Russian air defence** now intercepts a share of GMLRS [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Excalibur's confirmed hit rate fell from more than 55% early in 2023 to 7% in July and 6% in August 2023. JDAM-ER, delivered from February 2023, lost accuracy within weeks, and a patch followed in May 2023 [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming].
- **The VKS stays stand-off.** It uses FAB-500s with early glide kits from Su-35S out to about 70 km, in small but steady numbers, plus lofted S-8. Industry supplies about 40 long-range missiles a month. Tu-95 packages use Shaheds as pathfinders to find gaps in Ukrainian air defence [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].

### e5-counteroffensive-23 (Jun–Nov 2023)
- **Artillery.** Ukrainian M777 batteries normally had to displace 2–15 min after opening fire. Ammunition hoarded for the offensive allowed a short "freedom to fire" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Helicopter "artillery".** The Ka-52 firing Vikhr and Ataka ATGMs was the leading aviation threat. Mi-35M added Ataka and lofted S-8 salvos. Strikes came from 8–10 km, often with an EW-escort helicopter. Ukrainian troops learned that when Russian GPS jamming lifted, attack helicopters were coming [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. Around Robotyne the UK MoD called the Ka-52 "one of the most influential Russian weapons systems" against the offensive. It flew at 50–100 m and carried up to 12 Vikhr ATGMs (about 8 km). The new Ka-52M added the LMUR (Izdeliye 305), with about 15 km range and a 25 kg warhead, well beyond the roughly 6 km reach of a Stinger [src:elpais-2023-ka52-counteroffensive]. Ukrainian MANPADS teams still got some: the 47th Mechanised Brigade claimed its eighth Ka-52 near Robotyne on 17 Aug 2023, with a single launch [src:ukrinform-2023-ka52-robotyne]. Oryx has 69 Ka-52 losses confirmed over the whole war [src:oryx-russian-losses].
- **Russian doctrine change.** Russia dropped the Soviet norm of about 720 rounds to suppress a platoon position. It prioritised Krasnopol production and brought in Lancet-3M: "effect over volume" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Glide bombs** were already a significant factor on the Zaporizhzhia front in summer 2023 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs].
- **Ukrainian deep strike.** Storm Shadow, fired from Su-24M since May 2023, hit the Chonhar bridges and, on 22 Sep 2023, the Black Sea Fleet HQ in Sevastopol [src:wikipedia-storm-shadow]. The first ATACMS strikes (October 2023) destroyed helicopters on occupied airfields [src:wikipedia-atacms]. The GBU-39 SDB (small-diameter bomb), delivered from November 2023, hit about 90% of the time [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming].

### e6-avdiivka-attrition (Oct 2023–Jul 2024)
- **Shell famine.** The Ukraine:Russia shell ratio reached 1:10 in February 2024 while US aid was stalled [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. The Czech initiative was built to fill the gap and delivered 1.5M rounds in 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative].
- **The mass-KAB era starts.** Russia produced about 40,000 UMPK kits in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] and dropped about 40,000 KABs, around 109 a day [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs]. Glide bombs contributed to the fall of Avdiivka in February 2024 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs]. ISW judged Avdiivka the first time Russian aviation used glide bombs at scale as close support for advancing infantry. A Ukrainian brigade counted 60 KABs on Avdiivka in one day (17 Feb), and a soldier reported "up to 500" over several days. The Tavriisk grouping recorded a record 73 airstrikes on 14 Feb 2024 [src:isw-2024-02-17-assessment].
- **The 2024 rate.** Zelensky gave "more than 3,000" KABs a month, 3,200 in May 2024, and "up to 100" a day [src:pbs-2024-hinnant-glide-bombs]. During the Kharkiv/Vovchansk offensive, eight Russian air bases within 100 km of the border launched the strikes on Kharkiv, and glide bombs had hit the city more than 50 times in 2024 by mid-June [src:pbs-2024-hinnant-glide-bombs].
- **UMPB D-30SN.** It debuted in March 2024 with about 90 km range and a warhead of about 250 kg. Defense Express's serial-number analysis shows output rising from 110 in winter to 161 a month in spring and about 600 in May–June 2024 [src:defenseexpress-2024-umpb-d30-production].
- **FAB-3000 with UMPK** (about 1,200 kg of explosive) was first dropped by a Su-34 on Lyptsi, north of Kharkiv, in June 2024, during the Kharkiv/Vovchansk offensive [src:defenseexpress-2024-fab3000-first-use].
- **Long-range ATACMS** were in use from February 2024, for example against Dzhankoi air base [src:wikipedia-atacms].

### e7-kursk-pokrovsk (Aug 2024–Mar 2025)
- **Shells.** Syrskyi said Russia was firing more than 44,000 rounds a day in August 2024, a 1:3 ratio [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. He later credited Ukrainian strikes on depots with cutting the rate from about 40,000 to about 23,000, and put the ratio at 1.5:1 by late December 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2025-denisova-syrskyi-shell-rate]. Western open-source estimates run much lower (see e8), so treat these as Ukrainian official figures. Ukraine received about 1.6M 155 mm shells in 2023 and 1.5M in 2024. Brigades now had shells but lacked charges and spares, and could rarely get DPICM or BONUS rounds [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Drones overtake artillery.** Tactical UAVs account for 60–70% of damaged or destroyed Russian systems, even though 60–80% of FPVs fail to reach the target. Officers insist drones work best with artillery. One example: a drone located three tanks, and five BONUS shells knocked out all of them within two minutes [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Gun survival.** Both sides space guns more than 500 m apart, dig them in with overhead cover, and use hides with clusters of firing positions and decoys. Guns move mainly to escape glide bombs [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Glide bombs** are released 30–90 km from the target, and Ukraine had "no effective countermeasures" [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. About 50% of Ukrainian casualties are taken in the rear from FPVs, artillery and glide bombs [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Air defence.** Forward-pushed Ukrainian SAMs get found and killed with 9M723 Iskander. Helicopters and fighter CAPs are the most efficient killers of high-flying Gerans, with mobile fire groups, AA guns and MANPADS as point defence [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Syrskyi says Mi-8 and Mi-24 crews with machine guns downed more than 3,200 Shahed-type drones between August 2024 and August 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-zadorozhnyy-helicopters-shaheds]. Interceptor UAVs are thinning out the Orlan and Zala reconnaissance drones [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. In October 2024 Russia launched 1,900 Shaheds, 33 ballistic and 42 cruise missiles [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign].
- **Aviation.** F-16s arrived in August 2024, and the first was lost on 29 August [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force]. Oryx confirms 5 F-16s destroyed in total [src:oryx-ukrainian-losses]. Mirage 2000-5F arrived on 6 February 2025 with AASM Hammer and SCALP [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].
- **Deep strike.** ATACMS were first used inside Russia on 19 Nov 2024 (a Bryansk arsenal, then an S-400 in Kursk region) [src:wikipedia-atacms], and Storm Shadow on 20 Nov 2024 [src:wikipedia-storm-shadow]. Russia answered with the first Oreshnik IRBM (intermediate-range ballistic missile) strike, on Dnipro on 21 Nov 2024; Ukrainian officials say the warheads were inert [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].

### e8-drone-kill-zone (Mar 2025–Jan 2026)
- **Shell balance improves.** By July 2025 Russia was firing about 27,000 rounds a day and the ratio was 1:1.8 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. The Western counterpart is far lower: open-source estimates compiled by West Point's Modern War Institute put Russian expenditure at 10,000–15,000 rounds a day, against Russian production of about 19,000 rounds a day of all types (about 7M in 2025, including 3.4M howitzer shells). They put Ukrainian fire at 2,000–7,000 rounds a day [src:mwi-2026-meneguzzo-industrial-window].
- **Ammunition supply, 2025.** The Czech initiative gives different totals for the year: 1.8M [src:kyivindependent-2026-pakhnyuk-czech-withdraw], or "almost 2M" and 4.4M cumulative, according to Pavel in February 2026 [src:pravda-2026-czech-initiative-4-4m]. Pavel says it moved the ratio from 1:10 to 1:2 [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative]. The EU had delivered 80% of its 2M-round 2025 target by September [src:militarnyi-2025-kallas-2m-rounds], and by February 2026 Commissioner Kubilius claimed EU output of "more than two million shells a year" [src:interfax-2026-kubilius-2m-shells]. Ukrainian 155 mm production stayed marginal: the first 10,000 shells came in late 2024, and a planned 100,000 for 2025 did not materialise, for lack of a domestic propellant plant [src:mezha-2025-ukrainian-155mm]. Budanov says North Korea covers about 40% of Russia's artillery needs [src:militarnyi-2025-budanov-dprk-shells], and RUSI calls those shells low quality [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. North Korean deliveries had fallen by more than half by late 2025 [src:mwi-2026-meneguzzo-industrial-window].
- **How artillery is used now.** A fire mission is rarely more than 10 rounds before the gun is spotted, and 8–10 rounds are generally enough to destroy a soft target. Laser-guided rounds handle hard targets that UAVs cannot kill. Ukrainian analysts think about half of the Russian guns claimed as destroyed by UAVs are only damaged and later repaired [src:rusi-2025-watling-combined-arms]. By 2025, Ukraine's MoD counted 819,737 video-confirmed drone hits and credited drones with more than 80% of enemy targets destroyed. Zelensky: "artillery matters, but in a different way" [src:defensenews-2026-ruitenberg-epoints].
- **KAB surge.** About 3,500 a month in early 2025 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs]. A 2025 high of 5,328 in October, with about 40,000 in January–October [src:kyivindependent-2025-taradiuk-kab-october]. About 3,500 in November, for about 44,000 in January–November (130 a day) [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs].
- **HUR's glide-bomb figures.** HUR says 120,000 glide bombs were planned for 2025, with 200–250 launched a day. About 500 are extended-range (about 200 km), and work on 400 km versions is under way [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]. Ukrainian SAM units killed up to 100 KABs in September–November 2025, which is the first real anti-KAB capability [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs]. Pokrova jamming degrades KAB accuracy [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs]. Ukrainian officers see the newer powered bombs (Molniya type) as having a low success rate and being easy to intercept [src:rusi-2025-watling-combined-arms].
- **Anti-Shahed adaptation.** Interceptor drones took a growing share of Shahed kills from late 2025. File 01 puts them at about $2,000 each, and a Shahed-type drone costs roughly $20–50k [src:mwi-2026-meneguzzo-industrial-window]. A Shahed carrying an R-60 air-to-air missile, aimed at helicopters, appeared in December 2025 [src:pravda-2026-dotsenko-helicopters-drones].
- **Ukraine opens a SEAD/DEAD campaign** (suppression and destruction of enemy air defences) in late 2025. Oryx confirms 77 Russian SAM systems and 23 radars destroyed in 2025, and ISW counts at least 107 strikes on Russian air defences from November 2025 [src:isw-2026-barros-intermediate-range-strikes].
- **Missile production (HUR, June 2025, per month).** Iskander-M 60–70 (up from 40 in May 2024), Kinzhal 10–15, Kh-101 60–70, Kalibr 25–30, Iskander-K 20–30 and Onyx/Zircon 20–30. Russia also holds a stock of 60 North Korean KN-23 [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. RUSI cites Russian MoD plans for more than 750 9M723 and more than 560 Kh-101 in 2025, and notes the Kh-101 upgrades: better terminal visual navigation, cluster warheads and flares against MANPADS [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Strike tempo (October 2025).** About 5,300 Shaheds, 148 ballistic and 74 cruise missiles, roughly three times October 2024. The record was 26 ballistic missiles on 16 October. About 20% of Shaheds hit [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign]. Ballistic launches grew more than 700% from 2023 to 2025 [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks].
- **Ukrainian strike weapons.** Officials said Sapsan (about 300 km, >480 kg warhead) entered serial production in June 2025, and that Neptune production was scaled up [src:kyivindependent-2025-taradiuk-sapsan]. Zelensky dates Sapsan's combat use from 9 Dec 2025, but evidence of wide production "has remained elusive" [src:kyivindependent-2026-rushton-ballistic-gap]. An OSINT tally counts at least 31 Neptunes hitting 26 Russian sites from August 2023 to July 2026, with 2024 the busiest year. The R-360M reaches about 500 km with a 350 kg warhead; the Long Neptune (R-360L) has a 260 kg warhead [src:pravda-2026-harbuz-neptune-record]. The FP-5 Flamingo (about 3,000 km, about 1,150 kg payload) was first used on 30 Aug 2025, against an FSB post in Crimea [src:rbc-2026-vialko-flamingo]. Imagery of its September 2025 strike showed one of three missiles near the aimpoint, with the others 80–100 m and about 200 m off [src:missilematters-2026-hoffmann-flamingo-votkinsk]. A Gripen letter of intent followed in October 2025 [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].

### e9-counteroffensive-26 (Feb 2026–present)
- **Starlink cut-off (about 1 Feb 2026).** Russian units used Starlink to relay drone feeds (including strike drones reaching about 80 km) and Telegram to pass coordinates and coordinate air defence. Losing both makes their sensor-to-shooter chain less efficient but does not collapse C2 [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram]. This coincides with Ukraine's counteroffensive in the south.
- **Fires in the counteroffensive (from 29 Jan–9 Feb; see 00 §3 e9).** Ukraine's gains in the south rested less on tube artillery than on shaping fires. A SEAD/DEAD campaign opened Russian-held airspace. From spring 2026 an intermediate-range drone campaign hit fuel trucks, rail tankers and freight trains on the M-14, M-18 and T-0509 (H-20) highways and rail lines, 100–160+ km behind the front. Ukrainian armour could then be pushed 19 km past Russian lines near Oleksandrivka in March. FP-2 drones began firing unguided S-8 rockets at operational depth in May 2026, in effect drone-borne rocket artillery. The 7th Corps now tasks drone sorties specifically against Russian guns and drone launch sites. Russia's rate of advance fell to 2.9 km² a day in January–April 2026, excluding infiltrated areas, from 9.76 a year earlier [src:isw-2026-barros-intermediate-range-strikes]. ATACMS played almost no part: Ukraine's stock was "severely depleted" [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-atacms-iran].
- **Russian glide-bomb response.** KAB use rose throughout the counteroffensive. January is given as 5,171 (Air Force data via Kyiv Post) [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs] or more than 5,700 (MoD), with a record 316 on 18 January [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-january-kabs]. February was a record at the time, and March reached 7,987, more than 1,500 above it [src:ukrinform-2026-march-kabs]. April was nearly 7,000 and May about 7,500 [src:united24-2026-may-kabs]. June reached 8,266, with 391 bombs in 127 sorties (about 3 per sortie) on 13 June [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs]. July brought 8,300+ [src:defenseexpress-2026-syngaivska-july-kabs] and August 8,766, or 283 a day. That is more than the 211–237 guided bombs a day the US dropped on Iran in Operation Epic Fury [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-kabs-us-campaigns]. Kyiv Post projects about 100,000 more by mid-2027 [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs]. Targets included bridges on the Pokrovsk–Kostyantynivka H-20 highway, hit with FAB-3000 and Kh-38 (February), and the Kramatorsk and Slovyansk agglomeration [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment]. By late September, strikes in the Kramatorsk direction were intensifying, partly to leave ruins that Russian infiltrators use as cover [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **Russian tube and rocket fire (Ukrainian counts of strike events, not rounds).** More than 113,000 attacks and 2,400 MLRS strikes in January 2026 [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-january-kabs]. More than 115,000 artillery strikes, including 2,834 by MLRS, in March [src:ukrinform-2026-march-kabs]. Nearly 97,000 attacks and 1,300+ MLRS strikes in July [src:defenseexpress-2026-syngaivska-july-kabs]. Russian artillery brigades and regiments now field their own drone operators; ISW's order-of-battle notes name the 288th Artillery Brigade, for example [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment].
- **Ukrainian glide bombs.** A domestic 250 kg glide bomb with a range of about 40 km, carried by the Su-24, was cleared in May 2026. Ukraine receives only "a few dozen" Western glide bombs a month [src:euromaidanpress-2026-axe-ukrainian-glide-bomb]. ISW links its use to the SEAD campaign, which lets Ukrainian jets fly closer and higher [src:isw-2026-barros-intermediate-range-strikes].
- **Ballistic pressure and the Patriot gap.**
  - Launches: 91 ballistic missiles in January 2026, in salvos of up to 30, with at least 2 Iskander-M built a day [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-ballistic-salvo]. A record 121 followed in February [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity], then 195 in July [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-july-strikes]. Zelensky counts more than 2,100 missiles in 2026 to late September, over 950 of them ballistic [src:twz-2026-altman-vivaldi]. Ukrainian officials put Russian output at about 120 ballistic missiles a month, and the Atlantic Council expects more than 1,000 launches in 2026 [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot].
  - Intercept rates depend on the counter and the salvo. CSIS's 10.2% is its 2026 dataset share (to mid-July) [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks]. The MoD's monthly figures give about 40% in June, when 11 of 41 were downed on 2 June and about half on 15 June [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-ballistic-tripled], and 29 of 195 (15%) in July [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-july-strikes]. In the first week of July only 4 of 49 were stopped [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot], and none of 29 on the night of 6 July [src:euronews-2026-vakulina-zero-intercepts]. Air Force spokesman Ihnat: "We have enough systems, but what we need is a steady supply of missiles" [src:euronews-2026-vakulina-zero-intercepts]. Each ballistic missile typically takes 2–3 Patriot interceptors [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-ballistic-tripled].
  - **The US–Iran war**, from February 2026, reportedly consumed up to 1,430 Patriot rounds in 39 days [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot]. Gulf states fired more than 1,100, and nearly a third of global Patriot stocks were used [src:euronews-2026-vakulina-zero-intercepts]. US ATACMS and PrSM stocks were also "badly depleted" (Reuters, which the White House denies) [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-atacms-iran]. Lockheed makes about 620–650 PAC-3 MSE a year, rising towards 2,000 under a January 2026 contract [src:twz-2026-altman-vivaldi] [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot]. Ukraine bought 70 ATACMS from Turkey in August 2026 [src:kyivindependent-2026-rushton-ballistic-gap].
  - Oreshnik struck Lviv on 8 Jan 2026 and was used twice on 24 May 2026 [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].
- **Jet Gerans.** The Geran-5 debuted on 11 Jan 2026: about 1,000 km range, 90 kg warhead, air-launchable from the Su-25, with optional R-73 missiles [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-geran-5]. Geran-3 and Geran-4, at about 500 km/h, are beyond current interceptor drones and complicate the work of mobile fire groups [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. Against the jets, Ihnat says, "mobile fire groups and anti-drone interceptors can no longer be relied upon" and scarce missiles must be used [src:pravda-2026-ihnat-jet-drones]. Gun defence is also fragile: on 1 Apr 2026 three of the eight guns in two Skynex batteries failed within minutes, and a Shahed got through [src:militarnyi-2026-khomenko-skynex-issues]. Zelensky counts 63,000 attack drones in January–August 2026, more than 7,700 of them jets [src:twz-2026-altman-vivaldi]. ISIS reconstructs jets at 57–62% of genuine Shahed/Geran use in August 2026 [src:isis-2026-anokhin-shahed-monthly-sep]. Zelensky, citing HUR, says North Korea has begun building Shahed-type drones with Russian help (claim) [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-dprk-shaheds].
- **Shahed waves and who kills them.**
  - Volume and intercepts: a record 8,150 drones in May 2026, 35–40% of them decoys; the 2026 plan is about 60,000 attack UAVs plus 50,000 decoys [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. In July, 5,142 of 8,653 strike drones were downed (about 59%) [src:united24-2026-mykhailenko-july-strikes]. ISIS reconstructs a Shahed hit share of about 10–14% in July–August [src:isis-2026-anokhin-shahed-monthly-sep].
  - Intercept share: the MoD counted 7,476 of 8,150 Shahed-type launches intercepted in May 2026 (91.7%) [src:pravda-2026-sting-3000].
  - Who kills them, May 2026 (official claims): of more than 3,500 drones downed, Unmanned Systems Forces interceptors took more than 1,200 and army aviation helicopters more than 440 [src:kyivpost-2026-syrsky-may-interceptors]. The maker's claim for its Sting interceptor alone is more than 3,000 Shaheds and Gerberas in the same month [src:pravda-2026-sting-3000]. The counts cannot both be complete, so treat interceptor kill shares as a range. Beskrestnov ("Flash") says interceptors now kill about 50% of incoming Shahed-type drones, against about 10% in winter [src:kyivpost-2026-syrsky-may-interceptors]. Helicopters take up to 40% of drones in their designated zones, and one Navy Mi-8 crew downed 12 Shaheds in 3 h in March 2026 [src:pravda-2026-dotsenko-helicopters-drones].
  - Data caveat: from about 5 May 2026 the Air Force stopped consistently publishing Shahed counts, so monthly Shahed figures are reconstructions [src:isis-2026-anokhin-shahed-monthly-sep].
- **Ukrainian deep strike.** Flamingo hit the GRAU arsenal at Kotluban on 12 Feb 2026 [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment]. On 20–21 Feb it holed a workshop roof at the Votkinsk missile plant, about 1,400 km away, about 20–25 m from the likely aimpoint [src:missilematters-2026-hoffmann-flamingo-votkinsk]. Cheboksary followed on 5 May and 10 Jun 2026 [src:rbc-2026-vialko-flamingo]. The maker claims 30 a month, with 210 planned. Independent counts find about 10 documented strikes since August 2025, salvos of 3–6 missiles every 3–8 weeks, and only a few that caused serious damage; the supply of AI-25 engines is the bottleneck [src:meduza-2026-flamingo-production] [src:rbc-2026-vialko-flamingo]. A Gripen deal was signed in May 2026 [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].
- **Operation Vivaldi** (Lyman, May–Sep 2026). This was a targeting operation more than an artillery one. According to the 3rd Corps, reconnaissance mapped Russian command nodes, which were then hit by kamikaze UGVs that heavy bomber drones dropped more than 10 km behind the line. Strikes on both ends of a diesel pipeline from Voronezh Oblast, which carried about half of the sector's fuel, forced 400 km truck runs. The repair base for the 20th Army was pushed back to Voronezh Oblast, and the Rubikon unit withdrew after losses (unit claims) [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-vivaldi-targeting]. In the first phase, fires went into Russian ATV and motorcycle reinforcement runs and supply routes, stranding the infiltration groups, which were then cleared; EW and strikes on control posts degraded Russian C2 [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment]. No source quantifies artillery, HIMARS or Ukrainian glide-bomb use in Vivaldi.

## 5. Russia's side as reported

- **Doctrine.** Russia began with Soviet norm-based massed fires, for example about 720 rounds to suppress a platoon position. After HIMARS, barrel wear and shell shortages it judged this "non-viable" and moved to the reconnaissance-fire complex: Orlan-10 plus Strelets plus Krasnopol and Lancet, with effect prioritised over volume [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak] [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **The VKS as a "force in being".** It lost the struggle to suppress Ukrainian air defence in March 2022 [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war] and fell back on stand-off munitions: cruise missiles from Tu-95/Tu-160, lofted S-8, and then glide bombs [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. The glide bomb turned a latent air force into the main source of heavy firepower at the front [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Industrial scaling.** UMPK went from tens of thousands to more than 100,000 a year [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]. Iskander-M rose tenfold from 2022 to 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. Shahed/Geran output supports waves of thousands a month [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. CSIS reads the strike rhythm as "industrial rhythm more than battlefield design" [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign]. Russia makes up shell shortfalls with North Korean ammunition, launchers and KN-23 missiles [src:militarnyi-2025-budanov-dprk-shells] [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. HUR counts more than 7M North Korean rounds, 600+ guns and 100+ KN-23/KN-24 since June 2023 [src:kyivpost-2026-zakharchenko-dprk-supplies]. By 2026 KAB use (283 a day in August) exceeds the US rate over Iran [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-kabs-us-campaigns].
- **Adaptation of the strike campaign.** Missiles are routed around EW and SAM positions and arrive from several directions at once. Iskander carries decoys. The Kh-101 has better terminal guidance and flares. Drone waves map Ukrainian air defence and drain its interceptors [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. In 2026 Russia pairs jet Gerans with ballistic salvos to exploit the Patriot shortage [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **Glide bombs as siege tools.** KABs support infantry directly, as at Avdiivka [src:isw-2024-02-17-assessment]. They cut bridges and roads in the Ukrainian rear (FAB-3000 on the H-20, February 2026) [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment], and reduce near-rear towns to ruins that infiltrators then use as cover [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **Artillery and drones merge.** Russian artillery brigades and regiments field their own drone operators [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment]. Rocket minelayers (Zemledeliye, 5–15 km) seed minefields across breach lanes and supply routes [src:euromaidan-2026-mukhina-zemledeliye].
- **Newer tools.** Extended-range and powered glide bombs [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]; jet Gerans, including air-launched Geran-5 with optional R-73 missiles [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-geran-5]; and the Oreshnik IRBM used as a signalling weapon [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].
- **Vulnerabilities.** Rail and depot dependence (2022), exposed forward arming and refuelling points for helicopters close to the front (2023) [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak], dependence on Starlink and Telegram for drone feeds and coordinates (until February 2026) [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram], missile plants within reach of Flamingo, such as Votkinsk [src:missilematters-2026-hoffmann-flamingo-votkinsk], and an air-defence network thinned by Ukraine's SEAD campaign, which exposes GLOCs 100–160 km deep [src:isw-2026-barros-intermediate-range-strikes].

## 6. Game/sim relevance

- **Model fires as a kill chain, not a dice roll.** Each strike should have a sensor (UAV, EW direction-finding, observer), a link (organic, HQ queue, Starlink/Telegram, fibre), and a shooter with a response time that varies by era. The data supports 3–5 min for an organic UAV link against 20–30 min through an HQ (2022–23). In e9, degraded links (loss of Starlink) should visibly lengthen Russian response times [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder] [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram].
- **Ammunition as a strategic resource.** Russian daily fire should fall from about 20k–60k (2022) to about 27k (2025, Ukrainian figure) or 10–15k (Western open-source estimate); expose the range as a difficulty setting. Ukrainian fire of 2–7k a day is the plausible 2025–26 band. The Ukrainian:Russian ratio should swing from 1:12, to 1:10 in the early-2024 famine, to about 1:2 as a function of aid, the Czech initiative (whose funding halved in 2026) and North Korean deliveries (down by half in late 2025). Shells and charges should be separate items [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio] [src:mwi-2026-meneguzzo-industrial-window] [src:kyivindependent-2026-pakhnyuk-czech-withdraw] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Fire missions are short.** A gun gets about 10 rounds before it is spotted, 8–10 kill a soft target, and then it must move. Drones find the targets and guns or laser-guided rounds finish them. About half the guns "destroyed" by drones come back after repair [src:rusi-2025-watling-combined-arms]. By 2026 drones cause 70–80% of casualties, so artillery should be a finisher and a suppressor, not the main killer [src:politico-2026-hartog-latvian-drones].
- **Precision decays under EW.** GPS-guided munitions should have an accuracy modifier tied to local Russian EW density, with Excalibur and GMLRS degrading over time. GBU-39 and CRPA-equipped weapons should resist better [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming] [src:euromaidanpress-2026-axe-ukrainian-glide-bomb].
- **Glide bombs as an off-map, un-interceptable (mostly) area weapon.** Their volume should scale by era: about 100 a day in 2024, about 130 in 2025, and 170–285 a day in 2026, with a monthly series in §4. Local concentrations should be possible, such as 60 a day on one town (Avdiivka) [src:isw-2024-02-17-assessment]. They demolish fortifications and force guns and HQs to move. Counters should be indirect: jamming, dispersal, striking airfields and carriers, and pushing SAMs forward at the risk of an Iskander hunting them [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs] [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Contested air and helicopter artillery.** Neither side can fly deep. Helicopters work only as stand-off shooters: ATGMs at 8–10 km, LMUR at about 15 km, or lofted S-8/S-13 salvos as imprecise area suppression. A forward arming and refuelling point near the front is a lucrative target, and FPVs now reach helicopters [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak] [src:pravda-2026-dotsenko-helicopters-drones]. For the player, Ukrainian Mi-8/Mi-24 gunships are also an air-defence asset: they cover designated zones against slow Shaheds, but not jets [src:kyivpost-2026-syrsky-may-interceptors].
- **Interceptor stocks are a national pool.** PAC-3 for ballistic threats should be finite and shared between cities and the front. Each ballistic missile costs 2–3 interceptors, and resupply is an external event: the 2026 Iran war cut it [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity] [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-ballistic-tripled] [src:atlanticcouncil-2026-kuskevics-patriot]. Gun air defence needs a reliability roll [src:militarnyi-2026-khomenko-skynex-issues]. Ukraine's own Chaika VR trainers for mobile fire groups are a reference for how the Shahed-shooting minigame looks [src:euromaidanpress-2025-seagull-flyswatter].
- **Deep strike as the player's operational lever.** HIMARS (2022), Storm Shadow and ATACMS (2023–24), then mid-range drones, a few Flamingo and Neptune missiles, and (claimed) Sapsan (2025–26). They can hit depots, HQs, airfields, pipelines and missile plants. Target effects should be felt with a lag: depots displace, repair routes lengthen (250–400 km in Vivaldi), and output falls. Missiles should be scarce, arriving in salvos of 3–6 every few weeks, and miss by tens of metres [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-vivaldi-targeting] [src:meduza-2026-flamingo-production] [src:missilematters-2026-hoffmann-flamingo-votkinsk].
- **Shaping fires are the 2026 counteroffensive model.** The sequence is SEAD/DEAD, then interdiction of GLOCs at 100–160 km, then the strike on command nodes and reinforcement runs, then the ground attack. This is what made Ukrainian mechanised moves possible in the kill zone [src:isw-2026-barros-intermediate-range-strikes] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].

## 7. Terms

- **bavovna (бавовна)** — "cotton". Ironic Ukrainian slang for explosions on Russian-held targets, mocking the Russian euphemism "khlopok" ("pop", a homophone of "cotton").
- **KAB (керована авіаційна бомба, kerovana aviatsiina bomba)** — "guided aerial bomb". Ukrainian shorthand for any Russian glide bomb (UMPK-kitted FAB).
- **UMPK** — Russian "unified gliding and correction module", a wing-and-GNSS kit bolted onto FAB bombs.
- **FAB** — Russian general-purpose high-explosive bomb (FAB-250/500/1500/3000, by kg).
- **arta (арта)** — slang for artillery. "Nasha arta" means "our guns".
- **prylit (приліт)** — "arrival". An incoming hit or impact.
- **vylit (виліт)** — "departure". An outgoing launch, also a sortie.
- **PPO (ППО, protypovitriana oborona)** — air defence.
- **mobilni vohnevi hrupy (мобільні вогневі групи)** — mobile fire groups. Truck-mounted machine gun or AA-gun teams hunting Shaheds.
- **mopedy (мопеди)** — "mopeds". Slang for Shahed/Geran drones, from the moped-like buzz of their engines.
- **shakhedy (шахеди)** — Shaheds. Used generically for Geran one-way attack drones.
- **tryvoha (тривога)** — air-raid alert.
- **kontrbatareina borotba (контрбатарейна боротьба)** — counter-battery fight.
- **Kropyva (Кропива, "nettle")** — Ukrainian artillery C2 and mapping software.
- **Hrim-2 / Sapsan** — Ukrainian ballistic missile programme.
- **Bohdana** — Ukrainian domestic 155 mm wheeled self-propelled howitzer.
- **lob / pitch-up fire** — helicopter or jet climbs and releases unguided rockets on a ballistic arc to stay out of MANPADS range.
- **reconnaissance-fire complex / reconnaissance-strike complex (RFC/RSC)** — Soviet and Russian concept of a sensor-to-shooter network; RUSI renders it "Reconnaissance Fires Circuit".
- **Strelets** — Russian reconnaissance-and-fire-control data system.
- **CRPA** — controlled reception pattern antenna, an anti-jam GNSS antenna.
- **FARP** — forward arming and refuelling point.
- **IADS** — integrated air defence system.
- **SEAD / DEAD** — suppression / destruction of enemy air defences.
- **GLOC** — ground line of communication: a road or rail supply route.
- **LMUR (Izdeliye 305)** — Russian light multipurpose guided missile for Ka-52M and Mi-28NM, about 15 km range.
- **armiiska aviatsiia (армійська авіація)** — army aviation: the Ukrainian helicopter branch, now also an anti-Shahed force.
- **CAP** — combat air patrol.
- **stand-off** — releasing a weapon from outside the enemy's air-defence envelope.

## 8. Open questions and gaps

- **Artillery, HIMARS and glide bombs in Vivaldi and the southern counteroffensive.** No source quantifies them. Vivaldi was run under "maximum information silence", and the ISW special report and daily assessments (read via Wayback) describe drone-led shaping fires, not tube fire. A later RUSI fieldwork report is the likeliest source.
- **Per-town KAB counts.** Avdiivka (February 2024) is sourced. Vovchansk has only qualitative reporting, and Kharkiv city only a strike count (more than 50 by mid-June 2024). ISW assessments from May–June 2024 via Wayback had no per-town numbers.
- **KAB monthly series gaps.** February 2026 is derived (March minus "more than 1,500"). Most 2024 and 2025 months are missing; only April–May 2024, early 2025, October and November 2025 are sourced. A full Air Force/MoD series was not found in English.
- **Artillery's share of casualties.** Only the complement is sourced: drones cause 70–80% (Latvian SAB), and more than 80% of targets destroyed (UA MoD). No source splits the remaining 20–30% between artillery, glide bombs, mines and small arms.
- **Conflicting production figures.** RUSI's 70,000 UMPK ordered for 2025 against HUR's 120,000 planned. Actual drops (~44,000 in Jan–Nov 2025) suggest stockpiling or overstated plans. Flamingo's maker claims (30–210 a month) are contradicted by the strike record (~10 strikes). Sapsan's production is unverified.
- **Czech initiative totals.** Pavel's figures differ between February 2026 (4.4M cumulative, almost 2M in 2025) and May 2026 (just over 3M: 1.5M plus 1.8M). The difference may be calibre scope (122 mm included) or counting method.
- **Interceptor-drone kill share.** Official counts conflict (USF: over 1,200 in May 2026; Sting's maker: over 3,000), and the Air Force stopped publishing Shahed counts from May 2026. Cost per kill is only implied (a ~$2k interceptor against a $20–50k Shahed); file 01 has the detail.
- **Ballistic intercept methodology.** CSIS, the MoD monthly and Air Force per-salvo figures are now all sourced, but none separates PAC-3 from SAMP/T or counts interceptors fired per kill.
