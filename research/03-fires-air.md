# 03 — Fires and air: artillery, glide bombs, helicopters, aviation, missiles, air defence

Accessed 2026-09-28. Sources are in `sources/03-fires-air.yaml`, cited inline as `[src:<id>]`. The player side is Ukraine. Russian capabilities are described as reported by Ukrainian and Western sources.

## 1. Overview

The fires war went through three overlapping regimes.

1. **Massed tube and rocket artillery (2022).** Russia fired about 20,000 rounds a day in Donbas, with peaks above 32,000. Ukraine rarely fired more than 6,000, and Russia held a 10–12:1 advantage by June 2022 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. HIMARS/M270 GMLRS from late June 2022 broke the Russian model, which depended on railheads and big depots. RUSI calls their arrival "the point where the Russian offensive on Donbas ended" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
2. **Precision and mass without air superiority (2023–24).** Russian shells ran short (about 12M rounds in 2022, trending to about 7M in 2023) [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Russia moved to Krasnopol laser-guided shells, Lancet for counter-battery, and above all mass UMPK glide bombs. These let the VKS (Russian Aerospace Forces) strike the front from 30–90 km without entering Ukrainian SAM coverage [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russian EW (electronic warfare) cut Ukraine's GPS-guided Excalibur hit rate from more than 50% to 6–7% [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming]. Ukraine rationed shells: the ratio was 1:10 in February 2024 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio].
3. **The drone-dominated kill zone and a deep-strike duel (2024–26).** Tactical UAVs now cause 60–70% of damaged and destroyed Russian systems [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Artillery survives by dispersing and digging in, and it works as a partner to drones rather than the main killer. The Ukrainian shell ratio recovered to about 1:1.8 by July 2025 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio], helped by the Czech initiative [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative]. Russian production climbed to about 5,000–8,000 KABs a month in 2026 [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs] and 60–70 Iskander-M a month [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. Russia launched about 100+ ballistic missiles and up to about 8,000 Shahed/Geran drones a month [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. In the 2026 CSIS data, Ukraine's ballistic-missile intercept rate collapsed to about 10% [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks]. Ukraine answered with its own long-range weapons: Flamingo, Sapsan, Neptune and mass long-range drones.

**The air war stayed contested.** Since early March 2022, Ukrainian ground-based air defence has kept Russian crewed aircraft out of Ukrainian-held airspace except at very low altitude [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war]. Russia's layered IADS (integrated air defence system) keeps Ukraine's small air force (MiG-29, Su-27, Su-24M, Su-25, and from 2024–25 F-16 and Mirage 2000) to stand-off strikes and anti-missile and anti-drone patrols. Neither side has air superiority. Glide bombs are Russia's workaround: "Glide bombs gave the VKS teeth without the need to first achieve air superiority" [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

## 2. Per-era notes

### e1-invasion (Feb–Apr 2022)
- **The opening strike campaign fails to blind Ukraine.** About 75% of static Ukrainian SAM sites were engaged in the first 48 h. Mobile systems survived through dispersal and deception: dummy sites, printed "damage" sheets over hangars, and false radio reports of destroyed sites. Russian BDA (battle damage assessment) was weak [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. From early March the VKS lost the ability to operate over Ukrainian-held territory except at very low altitude [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war].
- **Pre-war Ukrainian air defence** had 35 S-300PS/PT divisions, 3 S-300V, 9 S-125 and 15 Buk-M1, with MANPADS (mostly modernised Igla-1) spread through units. SAMs forced aircraft down low, into the MANPADS envelope [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Missiles.** More than 200 Iskander strikes and about 180 Kh-22/32 and Kh-55/555 launches from February to early May 2022. By early April Russia had moved from salvos to single shots, probably because of stock shortages. Iskander-M output was estimated at about 6 a month. Russia fell back on Tochka-U, Bal and Bastion, with more civilian damage as a result [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Helicopters.** Russian air-assault helicopters reached Hostomel low along the Dnipro, but MANPADS downed 2 in the first wave [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Ka-52 and Mi-28N crews began pitch-up lob firing of unguided S-8 rockets. This buys about 6–7 km of extra range and keeps the helicopter out of Stinger range, at the cost of very wide dispersion [src:twz-2022-newdick-helicopter-lob].
- **Ukrainian aviation.** Su-24M, Su-25, Mi-24 and Mi-8 struck Russian convoys moving without air cover. MiG-29 pairs flew defensive counter-air, often outnumbered 6:2. Airspace was deconflicted from SAMs first by time, then by area [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Sensor-to-shooter.** Russia relied on pre-planned strikes and presumed success. Ukraine's pre-war Kropyva digital fire-control software had cut the time to open counter-battery fire by 90% and the time to engage unplanned targets by two-thirds [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

### e2-donbas-artillery (May–Aug 2022)
- **Russian fire superiority.** Russia averaged about 20,000 rounds a day, peaking above 32,000. More than 1,100 tube guns, in groups of 80–90 per brigade-sized grouping, gave a 12:1 advantage in May–June. A Ukrainian company position with about 3 km of frontage could take 6,000 rounds a day. Ukraine rarely exceeded 6,000 rounds a day in total, being short of both barrels and shells [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Meatgrinder gives 20,000–60,000 rounds a day and about 12M for 2022 as a whole [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **MLRS.** Uragan and Smerch were used at maximum range (35–70 km) and even against point targets. Russia fielded up to 10 EW complexes per 20 km of front, which degraded Ukrainian precision fires [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Russian kill chain.** With the unit's own UAV, fires landed 3–5 min after detection. Through a fire-control HQ it took 20–30 min, and about 48 h at operational level. Guns stood 100–150 m apart and rarely displaced unless fired upon [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **UAV spotters were used up fast.** About 90% of Ukrainian UAVs were lost, a quadcopter lasted about 3 flights and a fixed-wing about 6, and only about a third of missions succeeded. The Orlan-10 stood out on the Russian side [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **HIMARS.** From late June, GMLRS systematically hit depots that had been mapped from Russian dependence on rail. RUSI marks this as the end of the Russian Donbas offensive [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. After the July 2022 strikes on C2 and depots, Russia could no longer stockpile ammunition beside its guns [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].

### e3-counteroffensives-22 (Sep–Nov 2022)
- After the HIMARS strikes, Russia could no longer mass ammunition beside its guns and had to haul it from depots further back [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. This over-stretched logistics is the backdrop to Ukraine's Kharkiv and Kherson offensives (our inference; not a direct claim of the source).
- **The Shahed campaign begins.** Russia sent hundreds of Iranian Shahed-136 against substations, together with cruise and ballistic missiles. RUSI urged more MANPADS, radar-guided AA guns and Western fighters as affordable counters [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war].

### e4-bakhmut (Dec 2022–May 2023)
- **Russian shell economy.** In Q1 2023 Russia fired 12,000–38,000 rounds a day, and days above 24,000 became rare. Use shifted from 152 mm guns to 120 mm mortars. Annual consumption was trending to about 7M against Ukrainian estimates of about 2.5M a year in Russian production [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Russian reconnaissance-fire circuit (the Russian term for the kill chain).** Each axis commander keeps Orlan-10 orbits overhead, and the artillery brigade flies its own complex. Engagement takes about 3–5 min from UAV detection and 20–30 min from EW detection. The Strelets system links sensors to digital fire control. Krasnopol laser-guided shells and Lancet loitering munitions do counter-battery work and hunt guns [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **Russian air defence** now intercepts a share of GMLRS [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. Excalibur's confirmed hit rate fell from more than 55% early in 2023 to 7% in July and 6% in August 2023. JDAM-ER, delivered from February 2023, lost accuracy within weeks, and a patch followed in May 2023 [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming].
- **The VKS stays stand-off.** It uses FAB-500s with early glide kits from Su-35S out to about 70 km, in small but steady numbers, plus lofted S-8. Industry supplies about 40 long-range missiles a month. Tu-95 packages use Shaheds as pathfinders to find gaps in Ukrainian air defence [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].

### e5-counteroffensive-23 (Jun–Nov 2023)
- **Artillery.** Ukrainian M777 batteries normally had to displace 2–15 min after opening fire. Ammunition hoarded for the offensive allowed a short "freedom to fire" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Helicopter "artillery".** The Ka-52 firing Vikhr and Ataka ATGMs was the leading aviation threat. Mi-35M added Ataka and lofted S-8 salvos. Strikes came from 8–10 km, often with an EW-escort helicopter. Ukrainian troops learned that when Russian GPS jamming lifted, attack helicopters were coming [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak]. The Ka-52's role around Robotyne is widely reported but not separately sourced here (see Gaps). Oryx has 69 Ka-52 losses confirmed over the whole war [src:oryx-russian-losses].
- **Russian doctrine change.** Russia dropped the Soviet norm of about 720 rounds to suppress a platoon position. It prioritised Krasnopol production and brought in Lancet-3M: "effect over volume" [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak].
- **Glide bombs** were already a significant factor on the Zaporizhzhia front in summer 2023 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs].
- **Ukrainian deep strike.** Storm Shadow, fired from Su-24M since May 2023, hit the Chonhar bridges and, on 22 Sep 2023, the Black Sea Fleet HQ in Sevastopol [src:wikipedia-storm-shadow]. The first ATACMS strikes (October 2023) destroyed helicopters on occupied airfields [src:wikipedia-atacms]. The GBU-39 SDB (small-diameter bomb), delivered from November 2023, hit about 90% of the time [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming].

### e6-avdiivka-attrition (Oct 2023–Jul 2024)
- **Shell famine.** The Ukraine:Russia shell ratio reached 1:10 in February 2024 while US aid was stalled [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. The Czech initiative was built to fill the gap and delivered 1.5M rounds in 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative].
- **The mass-KAB era starts.** Russia produced about 40,000 UMPK kits in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] and dropped about 40,000 KABs, around 109 a day [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs]. Glide bombs contributed to the fall of Avdiivka in February 2024 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs].
- **UMPB D-30SN.** It debuted in March 2024 with about 90 km range and a warhead of about 250 kg. Defense Express's serial-number analysis shows output rising from 110 in winter to 161 a month in spring and about 600 in May–June 2024 [src:defenseexpress-2024-umpb-d30-production].
- **FAB-3000 with UMPK** (about 1,200 kg of explosive) was first dropped by a Su-34 on Lyptsi, north of Kharkiv, in June 2024, during the Kharkiv/Vovchansk offensive [src:defenseexpress-2024-fab3000-first-use].
- **Long-range ATACMS** were in use from February 2024, for example against Dzhankoi air base [src:wikipedia-atacms].

### e7-kursk-pokrovsk (Aug 2024–Mar 2025)
- **Shells.** Syrskyi said Russia was firing more than 44,000 rounds a day in August 2024, a 1:3 ratio [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. Western open-source estimates run much lower, so treat this as the Ukrainian official figure. Ukraine received about 1.6M 155 mm shells in 2023 and 1.5M in 2024. Brigades now had shells but lacked charges and spares, and could rarely get DPICM or BONUS rounds [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Drones overtake artillery.** Tactical UAVs account for 60–70% of damaged or destroyed Russian systems, even though 60–80% of FPVs fail to reach the target. Officers insist drones work best with artillery. One example: a drone located three tanks, and five BONUS shells knocked out all of them within two minutes [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Gun survival.** Both sides space guns more than 500 m apart, dig them in with overhead cover, and use hides with clusters of firing positions and decoys. Guns move mainly to escape glide bombs [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Glide bombs** are released 30–90 km from the target, and Ukraine had "no effective countermeasures" [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. About 50% of Ukrainian casualties are taken in the rear from FPVs, artillery and glide bombs [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Air defence.** Forward-pushed Ukrainian SAMs get found and killed with 9M723 Iskander. Helicopters and fighter CAPs are the most efficient killers of high-flying Gerans, with mobile fire groups, AA guns and MANPADS as point defence. Interceptor UAVs are thinning out the Orlan and Zala reconnaissance drones [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. In October 2024 Russia launched 1,900 Shaheds, 33 ballistic and 42 cruise missiles [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign].
- **Aviation.** F-16s arrived in August 2024, and the first was lost on 29 August [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force]. Oryx confirms 5 F-16s destroyed in total [src:oryx-ukrainian-losses]. Mirage 2000-5F arrived on 6 February 2025 with AASM Hammer and SCALP [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].
- **Deep strike.** ATACMS were first used inside Russia on 19 Nov 2024 (a Bryansk arsenal, then an S-400 in Kursk region) [src:wikipedia-atacms], and Storm Shadow on 20 Nov 2024 [src:wikipedia-storm-shadow]. Russia answered with the first Oreshnik IRBM (intermediate-range ballistic missile) strike, on Dnipro on 21 Nov 2024; Ukrainian officials say the warheads were inert [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].

### e8-drone-kill-zone (Mar 2025–Jan 2026)
- **Shell balance improves.** By July 2025 Russia was firing about 27,000 rounds a day and the ratio was 1:1.8 [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio]. The Czech initiative targeted up to 1.8M shells in 2025, and President Pavel credits it with moving the ratio from 1:10 to 1:2 [src:kyivindependent-2025-czech-initiative]. Budanov says North Korea covers about 40% of Russia's artillery needs [src:militarnyi-2025-budanov-dprk-shells], and RUSI calls those shells low quality [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **KAB surge.** About 3,500 a month in early 2025 [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs]. A 2025 high of 5,328 in October, with about 40,000 in January–October [src:kyivindependent-2025-taradiuk-kab-october]. About 3,500 in November, for about 44,000 in January–November (130 a day) [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs].
- **HUR's glide-bomb figures.** HUR says 120,000 glide bombs were planned for 2025, with 200–250 launched a day. About 500 are extended-range (about 200 km), and work on 400 km versions is under way [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]. Ukrainian SAM units killed up to 100 KABs in September–November 2025, which is the first real anti-KAB capability [src:euromaidanpress-2025-mukhina-84000-kabs]. Pokrova jamming degrades KAB accuracy [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs].
- **Missile production (HUR, June 2025, per month).** Iskander-M 60–70 (up from 40 in May 2024), Kinzhal 10–15, Kh-101 60–70, Kalibr 25–30, Iskander-K 20–30 and Onyx/Zircon 20–30. Russia also holds a stock of 60 North Korean KN-23 [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. RUSI cites Russian MoD plans for more than 750 9M723 and more than 560 Kh-101 in 2025, and notes the Kh-101 upgrades: better terminal visual navigation, cluster warheads and flares against MANPADS [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Strike tempo (October 2025).** About 5,300 Shaheds, 148 ballistic and 74 cruise missiles, roughly three times October 2024. The record was 26 ballistic missiles on 16 October. About 20% of Shaheds hit [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign]. Ballistic launches grew more than 700% from 2023 to 2025 [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks].
- **Ukrainian strike weapons.** Sapsan (about 300 km, >480 kg warhead) entered serial production in June 2025, and Neptune production was scaled up [src:kyivindependent-2025-taradiuk-sapsan]. The FP-5 Flamingo (about 3,000 km, about 1,150 kg) was first used in combat on 30 August 2025; its production claims are unverified [src:wikipedia-fp5-flamingo]. A Gripen letter of intent followed in October 2025 [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].

### e9-counteroffensive-26 (Feb 2026–present)
- **Starlink cut-off (about 1 Feb 2026).** Russian units used Starlink to relay drone feeds (including strike drones reaching about 80 km) and Telegram to pass coordinates and coordinate air defence. Losing both makes their sensor-to-shooter chain less efficient but does not collapse C2 [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram]. This coincides with Ukraine's counteroffensive in the south.
- **KABs at record levels.** 5,171 in January 2026 and 8,266 in June (+45%). On 13 June 2026, 391 bombs were dropped in 127 sorties, about 3 per sortie. About 100,000 more are expected by mid-2027 [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs].
- **Ukrainian glide bombs.** A domestic 250 kg, about 40 km glide bomb carried by the Su-24 was cleared in May 2026. Ukraine receives only "a few dozen" Western glide bombs a month [src:euromaidanpress-2026-axe-ukrainian-glide-bomb].
- **Ballistic pressure.** 91 ballistic missiles in January 2026, in salvos of up to 30, with at least 2 Iskander-M built a day [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-ballistic-salvo]. A record 121 followed in February 2026, with capacity of about 100 a month against PAC-3 output of about 52 a month [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. CSIS measures the 2026 ballistic intercept share at 10.2% and projects up to 840 ballistic launches in 2026 [src:csis-2026-jensen-two-strategic-attacks]. Oreshnik struck Lviv on 8 Jan 2026 and was used twice on 24 May 2026 [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].
- **Jet Gerans.** The Geran-5 debuted on 11 Jan 2026: about 1,000 km range, 90 kg warhead, air-launchable from Su-25, with optional R-73 missiles [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-geran-5]. Geran-3 and Geran-4 at about 500 km/h are beyond current interceptor drones and complicate the work of mobile fire groups [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity].
- **Shahed waves.** A record 8,150 drones in May 2026, 35–40% of them decoys. The 2026 plan is about 60,000 attack UAVs plus 50,000 decoys [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity].
- **Ukrainian deep strike.** Flamingo hit the Votkinsk plant, an Iskander producer about 1,400 km away, in February 2026, then targets in Cheboksary and Kirov [src:wikipedia-fp5-flamingo]. A Gripen deal was signed in May 2026 [src:wikipedia-ukrainian-air-force].
- **Operation Vivaldi** (Lyman, May–Sep 2026): no fires-specific source was found. See Gaps.

## 5. Russia's side as reported

- **Doctrine.** Russia began with Soviet norm-based massed fires, for example about 720 rounds to suppress a platoon position. After HIMARS, barrel wear and shell shortages it judged this "non-viable" and moved to the reconnaissance-fire complex: Orlan-10 plus Strelets plus Krasnopol and Lancet, with effect prioritised over volume [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak] [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder].
- **The VKS as a "force in being".** It lost the struggle to suppress Ukrainian air defence in March 2022 [src:rusi-2022-bronk-russian-air-war] and fell back on stand-off munitions: cruise missiles from Tu-95/Tu-160, lofted S-8, and then glide bombs [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder]. The glide bomb turned a latent air force into the main source of heavy firepower at the front [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Industrial scaling.** UMPK went from tens of thousands to more than 100,000 a year [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]. Iskander-M rose tenfold from 2022 to 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production]. Shahed/Geran output supports waves of thousands a month [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity]. CSIS reads the strike rhythm as "industrial rhythm more than battlefield design" [src:csis-2025-atalan-october-air-campaign]. Russia makes up shell shortfalls with North Korean ammunition, launchers and KN-23 missiles [src:militarnyi-2025-budanov-dprk-shells] [src:kyivindependent-2025-post-ballistic-production].
- **Adaptation of the strike campaign.** Missiles are routed around EW and SAM positions and arrive from several directions at once. Iskander carries decoys. The Kh-101 has better terminal guidance and flares. Drone waves map Ukrainian air defence and drain its interceptors [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].
- **Newer tools.** Extended-range and powered glide bombs [src:pravda-2025-hur-glide-bomb-production]; jet Gerans, including air-launched Geran-5 with optional R-73 missiles [src:militarnyi-2026-pryhodko-geran-5]; and the Oreshnik IRBM used as a signalling weapon [src:wikipedia-oreshnik].
- **Vulnerabilities.** Rail and depot dependence (2022), exposed forward arming and refuelling points for helicopters close to the front (2023) [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak], dependence on Starlink and Telegram for drone feeds and coordinates (until February 2026) [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram], and missile plants within reach of Flamingo, such as Votkinsk [src:wikipedia-fp5-flamingo].

## 6. Game/sim relevance

- **Model fires as a kill chain, not a dice roll.** Each strike should have a sensor (UAV, EW direction-finding, observer), a link (organic, HQ queue, Starlink/Telegram, fibre), and a shooter with a response time that varies by era. The data supports 3–5 min for an organic UAV link against 20–30 min through an HQ (2022–23). In e9, degraded links (loss of Starlink) should visibly lengthen Russian response times [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2023-watling-meatgrinder] [src:carnegie-2026-kolomychenko-starlink-telegram].
- **Ammunition as a strategic resource.** Russian daily fire should fall from about 20k–60k (2022) to about 27k (2025, Ukrainian figure). The Ukrainian:Russian ratio should swing 1:12 to 1:10 (early 2024 famine) to 1:2 as a function of aid, the Czech initiative and North Korean deliveries. Shells and charges should be separate items [src:liga-2025-shell-ratio] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Precision decays under EW.** GPS-guided munitions should have an accuracy modifier tied to local Russian EW density, with Excalibur and GMLRS degrading over time. GBU-39 and CRPA-equipped weapons should resist better [src:stripes-2024-khurshudyan-jamming] [src:euromaidanpress-2026-axe-ukrainian-glide-bomb].
- **Glide bombs as an off-map, un-interceptable (mostly) area weapon.** Their volume should scale by era (about 100 a day in 2024 to about 170–275 a day in 2026). They demolish fortifications and force guns and HQs to move. Counters should be indirect: jamming, dispersal, striking airfields and carriers, and pushing SAMs forward at the risk of an Iskander hunting them [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-glide-bombs] [src:japcc-2026-zanier-glide-bombs] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Contested air.** Neither side can fly deep. Helicopters work only as stand-off shooters at 8–10 km or with lob fire, and a forward arming and refuelling point near the front is a lucrative target. Air defence interceptor stocks (especially PAC-3 for ballistic threats) should be a finite national pool shared between cities and the front [src:rusi-2023-watling-stormbreak] [src:frontliner-2026-zvirynskyi-strike-intensity].
- **Deep strike as the player's operational lever.** HIMARS (2022), Storm Shadow and ATACMS (2023–24), then domestic Flamingo, Sapsan and Neptune plus drones (2025–26). They can hit depots, HQs, airfields and missile plants. Target effects should be felt with a lag (depots displace, and output falls) [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:wikipedia-fp5-flamingo].

## 7. Terms

- **bavovna (бавовна)** — "cotton". Ironic Ukrainian slang for explosions on Russian-held targets, mocking the Russian euphemism "khlopok" ("pop", a homophone of "cotton").
- **KAB (керована авіаційна бомба, kerovana aviatsiina bomba)** — "guided aerial bomb". Ukrainian shorthand for any Russian glide bomb (UMPK-kitted FAB).
- **UMPK** — Russian "unified gliding and correction module", a wing-and-GNSS kit bolted onto FAB bombs.
- **FAB** — Russian general-purpose high-explosive bomb (FAB-250/500/1500/3000, by kg).
- **arta (арта)** — slang for artillery. "Nasha arta" means "our guns".
- **prylit (приліт)** — "arrival". An incoming hit or impact.
- **vylit (виліт)** — "departure". An outgoing launch, also a sortie.
- **PPO (ППО, protypovitriana oborona)** — air defence.
- **mobilni vohnevi hrupy (мобільні вогневі групи)** — mobile fire groups. Truck-mounted machine gun or AA-gun teams hunting Shaheds.
- **mopedy (мопеди)** — "mopeds". Slang for Shahed/Geran drones, from the moped-like buzz of their engines.
- **shakhedy (шахеди)** — Shaheds. Used generically for Geran one-way attack drones.
- **tryvoha (тривога)** — air-raid alert.
- **kontrbatareina borotba (контрбатарейна боротьба)** — counter-battery fight.
- **Kropyva (Кропива, "nettle")** — Ukrainian artillery C2 and mapping software.
- **Hrim-2 / Sapsan** — Ukrainian ballistic missile programme.
- **Bohdana** — Ukrainian domestic 155 mm wheeled self-propelled howitzer.
- **lob / pitch-up fire** — helicopter or jet climbs and releases unguided rockets on a ballistic arc to stay out of MANPADS range.
- **reconnaissance-fire complex / reconnaissance-strike complex (RFC/RSC)** — Soviet and Russian concept of a sensor-to-shooter network; RUSI renders it "Reconnaissance Fires Circuit".
- **Strelets** — Russian reconnaissance-and-fire-control data system.
- **CRPA** — controlled reception pattern antenna, an anti-jam GNSS antenna.
- **FARP** — forward arming and refuelling point.
- **IADS** — integrated air defence system.
- **CAP** — combat air patrol.
- **stand-off** — releasing a weapon from outside the enemy's air-defence envelope.

## 8. Open questions and gaps

- **Operation Vivaldi (Lyman, May–Sep 2026)** and the Feb 2026 southern counteroffensive: no fires-specific source was found. How much did Ukrainian artillery, glide bombs and HIMARS contribute, and how did Russian KAB use near Lyman change? The web-search budget ran out before ISW daily assessments could be pulled, so ISW should be added.
- **Vovchansk and Kharkiv city KAB data.** Per-town glide-bomb counts for 2024 (Vovchansk, Kharkiv, Avdiivka daily rates) are missing beyond qualitative statements.
- **Current artillery share of casualties.** RUSI gives drones 60–70% of damaged systems in late 2024. A 2025–26 figure for artillery's share, and for Ukrainian rounds per day in 2025–26, is missing.
- **Conflicting glide-bomb production figures.** RUSI's 70,000 ordered for 2025 vs HUR's 120,000 planned. Actual drop counts (~44,000 in Jan–Nov 2025) suggest either stockpiling or overstated plans.
- **Contested Russian daily fire.** Ukrainian official figures (44k/day in Aug 2024, 27k in Jul 2025) run far above Western open-source estimates (~10k–15k/day). This needs a Western counterpart source.
- **Ballistic intercept rates.** CSIS's 10.2% for 2026 vs Ukrainian Air Force daily claims. The methodology differs (neutralised vs intercepted). The Air Force's own monthly statistics are needed.
- **Ukrainian helicopters.** No sourced account of Mi-8/Mi-24 lob-fire or anti-Shahed kill counts; RUSI only notes helicopters are efficient. The Ka-52 at Robotyne specifically lacks a dedicated source.
- **Interceptor drones.** Their share of Shahed kills in 2025–26 and their cost per kill are unquantified here. The drone topic may cover this.
- **EU shell production** (e.g. the EU's 2M/yr target) was not sourced. Neither were Ukrainian domestic 155 mm output or North Korean monthly deliveries in 2026.
- **Flamingo production and accuracy** rest mostly on maker claims and need independent verification.
