# 00: Timeline & eras of the Russo-Ukrainian war (2014 prelude, Feb 2022 to Sep 2026)

Research note for *bavovniatko*. The player is always the Ukrainian side. Accessed 2026-09-28. Citations are inline src-id tags in square brackets, resolved in `sources/00-timeline-eras.yaml`. Claims attributed to one side are marked as such, and contested numbers are given as ranges with who claims what.

## 1. Overview

The full-scale war (24 Feb 2022 onward) has gone through three broad movements:

1. **Manoeuvre and shock, 2022 (e1–e3).** Russia's plan for a quick decapitation fails near Kyiv. Russian massed artillery grinds forward in Donbas in summer. Ukraine then exploits an overstretched Russian force in Kharkiv Oblast and levers Russia out of right-bank Kherson. Russia answers with mobilization, annexation claims and a long-range strike campaign using Iranian-designed Shaheds [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
2. **Attrition and the failed breakthrough, 2023 to mid-2024 (e4–e6).** Wagner and convict "meat assaults" take Bakhmut. Ukraine's Western-equipped 2023 offensive stalls in about 30 km of prepared Russian defences. Then, while US aid is stalled, Russia uses its shell and glide-bomb superiority to take Avdiivka [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka].
3. **The drone-dominated war, mid-2024 to 2026 (e7–e9).** Tactical UAVs become the main killer, and the "kill zone" makes manoeuvre very costly [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russia buys slow gains at huge cost: about 3,600 km² in 2024 and about 4,300–4,800 km² in 2025, depending on the tracker [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025]. Ukraine raids Kursk, strikes deep (Spiderweb), and scales interceptor drones against about 50,000 Shaheds a year. In 2026 it turns local counteroffensives into real gains after Russia loses Starlink [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].

Across all eras, the aid picture shifts from US-led gifting (2022–2024), through the US drawdown and the March 2025 pause, to European-financed support and European purchases of US weapons via PURL (2025–2026) [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years] [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. As of late Sep 2026 there is no durable ceasefire. Russia still demands all of Donbas, neutrality and demilitarization, and Ukraine refuses [src:csmonitor-2026-weir-peace-talks].

**Occupied territory, rough anchors:** Crimea plus part of Donbas before 2022. Just over 19% (DeepState) to about 20% or 120,000 km² (CSIS) by end-2025 [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. Ukraine clawed back several hundred km² in 2026 [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive]. The peak occupied share in March 2022 is not sourced here; see §8. The live reference for frontline geometry is DeepStateMAP [src:deepstate-map].

**Losses, contested:**
- Russian casualties to Dec 2025: about 1.2m, of whom 275–325k killed (CSIS) [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- British intelligence: about 1.5m casualties and about 500k killed [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-confirmed-dead].
- Ukrainian General Staff: 1.52m Russians "liquidated" (killed or seriously wounded) [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-confirmed-dead].
- Mediazona/BBC Russian: 258,980 Russian dead confirmed by name as of Sep 2026, which is a hard lower bound [src:kyivpost-2026-korshak-confirmed-dead].
- Ukrainian casualties to Dec 2025: 500–600k, of whom 100–140k killed (CSIS) [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- Visually confirmed Russian equipment losses: 24,138 items, including 4,448 tanks [src:oryx-russian-equipment-losses].

## 2. Prelude 2014–2021 (background only)

- **Feb–Mar 2014:** The Revolution of Dignity (Euromaidan) ends with Yanukovych fleeing. Russian troops without insignia occupy Crimea, and Russia annexes it after an illegitimate "referendum" [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Apr–Sep 2014:** Russian-backed forces seize towns in Donbas, and the "DPR/LPR" are proclaimed. MH17 is shot down in July. At **Ilovaisk** (Aug–Sep) regular Russian troops intervene directly and defeat Ukrainian forces [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Feb 2015:** **Debaltseve** and the **Minsk II** agreements. The line then freezes into trench warfare, with ceasefire violations but little change in territory. About 14,000 people were killed in 2014–2021 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **2021:** Russian forces mass on Ukraine's borders. This sets up the Feb 2022 invasion [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Relevance for the sim** (background, not separately sourced): by 2022 Ukraine had eight years of combat-experienced brigades (ATO/JFO veterans) and a partly NATO-trained officer corps. Russia had an occupied Donbas base for its forces and had already set up the "DPR/LPR" militia corps.

## 3. Per-era notes

### e1-invasion (24 Feb – early Apr 2022)

**Key battles and operations.** A multi-axis invasion from Belarus, Russia and Crimea hits Kyiv (Hostomel, Bucha, Irpin), Chernihiv, Sumy, Kharkiv, and the south (Kherson taken, Mariupol besieged). Russia planned a roughly 10-day operation and expected occupation by August 2022. Its deception worked, but it had no contingency plan when speed failed [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. By early April Russia had abandoned the attempt on Kyiv and the north [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. The Mariupol siege ran on into May (e2).

**What Ukraine had.** Pre-war stocks, plus rapid Western light anti-armour and MANPADS deliveries (Javelin, NLAW, Stinger) and Bayraktar TB2s. Quantities are not sourced here (§8). Ukraine kept **artillery parity for about the first six weeks**, until its ammunition ran short [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. "From April, the West became Ukraine's strategic depth" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

**What Russia had.** A **12:1 force ratio north of Kyiv**. It began with 2,433 barrel artillery systems against Ukraine's 1,176, and 3,547 MLRS against 1,680. Its main manoeuvre unit was the battalion tactical group (BTG) [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

**Defining features.** Road-bound Russian columns, weak Russian logistics and C2, and a Ukrainian territorial-defence surge. UAS attrition was extreme: about 90% of drones used were lost [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Talks in Belarus and Antalya/Istanbul (from 28 Feb 2022) halted over disagreements and the Bucha massacre [src:wikipedia-peace-negotiations].

### e2-donbas-artillery (May – Aug 2022)

**Key battles and operations.** Mariupol falls (May). Russia's Donbas offensive takes **Sievierodonetsk** (June) and then Lysychansk [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. Ukraine's Kherson operation begins on **29 Aug 2022**, which is the hinge into e3 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Ukraine had.** By summer 2022 Ukraine had used up its own stocks of most critical munitions and depended on partners [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Western-supplied precision fires hit Russian logistics in **June–July 2022** and took away Russia's advantage of massed artillery supplied from rail-fed depots [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. This is the start of the HIMARS/GMLRS era; US HIMARS arrived in summer 2022, and the exact dates and quantities are still to be sourced (§8).

**What Russia had.** By June, a **10:1 advantage in volume of fire** [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Most Ukrainian losses in 2022 came during this Donbas offensive and the Kherson push, and they fell mainly on the professional brigades [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Defining features.** A slow, artillery-heavy Russian advance city by city, with very heavy Ukrainian casualties. The original invasion force proved too small to cover the whole front, which set up e3 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e3-counteroffensives-22 (Sep – Nov 2022)

**Key battles and operations.**
- **Kharkiv counteroffensive** from **6 Sep 2022**: Balakliia (8 Sep), Izium and Kupiansk (10 Sep), Lyman (1 Oct). Ukraine claimed about 12,000 km² and 500+ settlements. ISW assessed about 2,500 km² for the breakthrough phase alone [src:wikipedia-2022-kharkiv-counteroffensive]. The Russian Western Group of Forces collapsed [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Kherson:** Ukrainian fire control over the Dnipro crossings forced an orderly Russian withdrawal from the right bank in Oct–Nov 2022. Kherson city was liberated in November [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].

**What Ukraine had.** A small pool of ready reserve brigades. The rest of the force was fixed on the line of contact [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The success spurred the Ramstein pledging process, but agreeing what to gift took about three months, and heavy-armour pledges came only in Jan 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Partial **mobilization declared 21 Sep 2022**, with a target of 300,000, followed by a mass exodus of draft-age men [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization]. Russia claimed to **annex** four partly occupied oblasts on 30 Sep [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. The first Iranian Shahed-136/131 shipment arrived in mid-Aug 2022, and the first Shahed was recovered in Ukraine on 13 Sep 2022 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].

**Defining features.** The last large Ukrainian manoeuvre successes. Russia stabilised by trading space (the Dnipro as a barrier), mobilising, and building fortifications in Zaporizhzhia that would decide e5 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e4-bakhmut (Dec 2022 – May 2023)

**Key battles and operations.** A Russian winter offensive from late Jan 2023 began at **Vuhledar**, where Russia lost dozens of armoured vehicles (Reuters via RUSI), and spread along the eastern front with its main weight on **Bakhmut** [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Bakhmut fell in May 2023 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].

**What Ukraine had.** Western heavy armour was pledged in Jan 2023. Challengers arrived in Mar 2023, and Patriot crews were trained in early 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Around Bakhmut about 10,000 Ukrainian troops were killed or severely wounded. From February, Russia had artillery control of the city's supply roads [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Wagner plus mobilised convicts, with VDV support. **19,547 Wagner fighters killed in Bakhmut**, 88% of them convicts, and a similar number seriously wounded. Russia also held a **5:1 artillery advantage** [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. By Mar 2023, prisoners were the largest category in Mediazona's count of named Russian dead [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].

**Defining features.** Russia "traded lives for time" while it built its southern defences. Ukraine debated whether holding Bakhmut was worth it, the fear being that losing it early would expose Chasiv Yar and Kramatorsk [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Lancet loitering munitions emerge as a key Russian precision tool. By e5 they were "scouring" the narrow Ukrainian breaches [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e5-counteroffensive-23 (Jun – Nov 2023)

**Key battles and operations.** Supporting attacks at Bakhmut began in mid-May. The main **Orikhiv–Tokmak** axis opened on **3 Jun 2023**. Meteorit line charges were used to clear lanes through minefields [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The **Kakhovka dam** was destroyed in the first week, and the **Wagner mutiny** followed in late June [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. A breakthrough was likeliest in early August. By mid-September the goals were tactical, and **Krynky** (across the Dnipro) was the last attempt [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Ukraine had.** The plan called for 12 armoured and mechanised brigades on a 30 km frontage, Tokmak isolated in 7 days, then Melitopol. In the event only a couple of brigades were properly equipped, and most were newly raised and undertrained [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. At most 55 guns of 155 mm fired on the main axis, at about **70 rounds/gun/day at peak and as low as 10**. There were about **7 GMLRS a day** for the whole theatre. Over **300 Storm Shadow/SCALP** missiles arrived during the offensive. **ATACMS** were first approved and used in Oct 2023, against Russian helicopter bases. M117 demining vehicles arrived too late (usable only from Oct 2023) [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Three defence lines about **30 km deep**, held by the 58th CAA (the 7th VDV and 42nd MR divisions forward). Up to **105,000 troops, about 470 tanks, up to 1,410 AFVs, 720+ guns, up to 230 MLRS and 12 operational-tactical missile complexes** were available on the axis. There were 35 days of ammunition cached at the guns, about 60 attack helicopters, and "dozens of glide bombs per day" [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Defining features.** Minefields, drones and Lancets, and precision fires made breaching columns predictable and targetable. Poor operational security meant Russia knew the axis and the timing. RUSI concluded that renewed Ukrainian offensives would not be viable "in the foreseeable future" [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e6-avdiivka-attrition (Oct 2023 – Jul 2024)

*Overlaps e5 in Oct–Nov 2023: Avdiivka opened on 10 Oct 2023 while Krynky was still under way.*

**Key battles and operations.** **Avdiivka**, 10 Oct 2023 to 17 Feb 2024 (Ukrainian withdrawal) [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka]. After that, Russia began its slow drive west towards Pokrovsk, covering about 50 km from Feb 2024 to Jan 2026 at about **70 m/day** [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. The **northern Kharkiv offensive** from 10 May 2024 (Vovchansk, Lyptsi) stalled at about 10 km depth. On 30 May 2024 Washington allowed US weapons to be used against targets in Russia near Kharkiv [src:wikipedia-2024-kharkiv-offensive].

**What Ukraine had.** A shell famine. Troops reported a **5:1** Russian advantage in early Feb 2024, and 8–9:1 at worst, blamed on delayed US and EU packages [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka]. Air defence against Shaheds became very effective: **77% interception in Feb 2024 and 97% in May 2024** [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran]. FPV drones were expanding fast. By late 2024, tactical UAVs accounted for **60–70% of damaged or destroyed Russian systems**, even though 60–80% of FPVs missed [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**What Russia had.** Mass **UMPK glide bombs**. The UK MoD counted 30–50 guided munitions a day by 5 Feb 2024, or about 600 in four weeks [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka]. About **40,000 UMPK were produced in 2024**, launched from 30–90 km behind the line [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russia contracted **400,000+ troops in 2024** [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. North Korean shells began arriving: the OSC counted about 4 million shells shipped Sep 2023 to Apr 2025 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].

**Defining features.** Russia's firepower-driven attrition against a Ukraine short of ammunition. Glide bombs "gave the VKS teeth" without air superiority [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

### e7-kursk-pokrovsk (Aug 2024 – Mar 2025)

**Key battles and operations.**
- The **Kursk incursion** began on **6 Aug 2024**. Zelensky claimed 1,250 km² and 92 settlements on 19 Aug [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- The Russian Pokrovsk/Kurakhove drive continued. Russia took Velyka Novosilka by early 2025 and pushed towards the Dnipropetrovsk border [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- Ukraine withdrew from **Sudzha on 12 Mar 2025** [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **F-16s confirmed in Ukraine on 4 Aug 2024**, with at least 79 pledged [src:kyivindependent-2024-court-f16].
- **ATACMS strikes inside Russia** from 19 Nov 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2024-york-atacms-kursk].
- A force of about 800,000 personnel, of which under 25% was usable combat power. It held a 1,200 km line with local force ratios of 1:2 to 1:6 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- An expanding deep-strike campaign against Russian refineries and depots [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**What Russia had.**
- About **580,000 troops** in the operational group: about 70,000 in Kursk with about 10,000 North Koreans, 80,000 on the Kharkiv axis, 230,000 in Donetsk/Luhansk and about 140,000 in the south [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- Losses of **more than 200 killed a day in 2024, and over 1,200 casualties a day** by late 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- DPRK troops from Nov 2024 (about 11–12k at first). They fought in company or battalion-sized assaults, and Ukrainian troops described them as fit and cohesive [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- Shahed/Geran launches rose from about **75 to about 900 a week from Sep 2024** [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- **Fibre-optic FPVs** appear, which are immune to RF jamming [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**Defining features.** Ukraine traded a strategic gamble (Kursk as leverage for negotiations) against Russian gains in Donetsk [src:kyivindependent-2024-york-atacms-kursk]. North Korean casualty claims are contested: Ukraine says 4,500, South Korea 6,000 killed or wounded, and a BBC analysis 2,304 killed [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].

### e8-drone-kill-zone (Mar 2025 – Jan 2026)

**Key battles and operations.**
- The Kursk pocket is lost (Mar 2025).
- Russian summer 2025 gains peak at **130–150 km²/week** (Jun–Jul) [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- A Russian thrust towards Dobropillia is halted (Aug–Sep 2025) [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi].
- Over the winter of 2025–26 Ukraine loses most of **Pokrovsk and Myrnohrad** and withdraws in a "deliberate regrouping" [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- Russia makes first inroads into Dnipropetrovsk and Sumy oblasts [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025], and fighting reaches the Huliaipole front [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- **Operation Spiderweb (1 Jun 2025)**: the SBU flew 117 FPVs from trucks against five airbases. The SBU claims 41 aircraft hit, independent counts give 12–13 destroyed, and a US estimate says about 20 hit / 10 destroyed [src:wikipedia-operation-spiderweb].

**What Ukraine had.**
- A **US aid and intelligence pause** after the 28 Feb 2025 Oval Office row, lifted on 11 Mar after Jeddah [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-aid-resume].
- New US allocations effectively ended in early 2025. Deliveries continued through **PURL** (created Jul 2025) [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years].
- European military aid in 2025 was **+67%** on the 2022–24 average, but total military aid was **13% below** it [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years].
- **Interceptor drones** at scale: Wild Hornets' Sting from Apr 2025, which made the first Geran-3 kill in Dec 2025 [src:wikipedia-sting-drone].

**What Russia had.**
- **70,000 UMPK ordered for 2025** [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Over 50,000 Shaheds launched in 2025**, about 5 times the previous year [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. Domestic production of the jet Geran-3 began in early 2025 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- North Korea: Ukrainian intelligence says it supplied up to 50% of Russia's 122/152 mm needs by end-2025, plus 148 KN-23/24 missiles by early 2025 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].
- Coercive recruitment of migrants and raids [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].

**Defining features.**
- A deepening drone "kill zone". By early 2025 the battlefield was transparent within 3 km and watched out to 15 km [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Later deepening and the elite Russian drone centre **Rubikon** are left to the drone topic file (see §8).
- Russian assaults shift further towards small-group infantry infiltration. Section-size attacks were already the norm in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- 2025 gains were **4,336 km² (DeepState) or 4,831 km² (CSIS)**, under 1% of Ukraine [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- **Diplomacy:** Jeddah and the 30-day ceasefire offer (Mar 2025) [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-aid-resume]. The Trump–Putin **Anchorage summit on 15 Aug 2025** ended without agreement because Putin demanded Donbas [src:wikipedia-2025-alaska-summit]. The White House meeting with Zelensky and Europeans followed on 18 Aug, and a **28-point US draft plan** was reported in Nov 2025 [src:wikipedia-2025-alaska-summit]. The first Abu Dhabi trilateral round was on 23–24 Jan 2026 [src:wikipedia-2026-abu-dhabi-talks].

### e9-counteroffensive-26 (Feb 2026 – present, Sep 2026)

**Key battles and operations.**
- SpaceX cut Russian frontline **Starlink** access at Ukraine's request on **5 Feb 2026** [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive]. Russian **Telegram** restrictions followed from 9 Feb, and ISW judged the two together degraded Russian C2 [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-starlink].
- The **Ukrainian counteroffensive** began on **11 Feb 2026** on the Dnipropetrovsk–Zaporizhzhia–Donetsk boundary [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive]. Whether Starlink caused the gains is disputed (§8).
- Ukraine advanced up to 10 km on a ~45 km front on the Haichur near Huliaipole [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- Syrskyi's area claims rose: **400 km² (late Feb), 470 (27 Mar), 600 (8 Jun), then 745 km² and 26 settlements (12 Aug)** [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive].
- **Operation Vivaldi** near Lyman, run by the 3rd Army Corps under Biletskyi. The attacks were tracked from late spring and the operation was revealed on 15 Sep [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi]. It erased Russia's northern pincer on the "fortress belt". Claims: 125 km² including 50 km² previously occupied, and 3,000+ Russian losses (unverified); analysts estimate 200–240 km² [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi] [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi] [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive].
- Russia shifted south towards **Mykolaivka and Sloviansk** and claimed to have encircled Dobropillia (22 Sep), which ISW did not confirm [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi]. Russian attacks west of Huliaipole towards Orikhiv continued [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive].

**What Ukraine had.**
- A European-financed pipeline: the **EUR 90 bn EU Ukraine Support Loan (2026–27)** and about EUR 11 bn allocated in May–Jun 2026 [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026].
- Continued dependence on US systems. **30% of European military aid is bought from US firms**, over 90% of PURL comes from US stocks, and Patriot remains critical [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026].
- Interceptor drones at mass scale: **Sting at 10,000+/month (Mar 2026)** and **9,000+ Shaheds downed by Jun 2026** [src:wikipedia-sting-drone]. Four new interceptors are being tested against jet Gerans [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors].

**What Russia had.**
- **Jet Gerans:** about 180 launched in 2025 but about **1,400 by early Jun 2026**. 15–20% of launches in Apr 2026 had jet engines, and the Geran-4 cruises at 350–500 km/h [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors]. The Geran-3 was retired by Aug 2026 in favour of the Geran-4/5 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- Russian gains slowed to **33 km²/week** by mid-Feb 2026. Ukraine estimates Russian losses at 30–32k/month [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- Year-round conscription from 2026 [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].
- More DPRK missiles, including a reported new Hwasong-11C batch in Aug 2026 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].

**Peace talks and ceasefires.**
- **Abu Dhabi** second round on 4–5 Feb 2026, with a 314-person POW swap and no ceasefire. Donbas was the main sticking point [src:wikipedia-2026-abu-dhabi-talks].
- An **Orthodox Easter truce** on 11–13 Apr 2026. Ukraine logged 469 Russian violations, and fighting resumed on expiry [src:wikipedia-april-2026-truce].
- In **Sep 2026**, Witkoff and Kushner visited Moscow and Kyiv, and Zelensky rejected the plan. Russia demands all occupied land plus the rest of Donbas, neutrality and demilitarization. CSMonitor contrasts these terms with the early-2022 draft deal, which it describes as limited to Donbas and Crimea [src:csmonitor-2026-weir-peace-talks].

**Defining features.** For the first time since 2022, Ukraine regains the local initiative, but through drone-led, information-silent operations rather than mechanised breakthroughs [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi]. The strategic air war escalates in quality (jet Gerans) as much as in quantity.

## 5. Russia's side as reported (Ukrainian and Western sources)

- **e1:** A pre-war professional army of BTGs with contract soldiers, VDV spearheads and huge artillery parks (2,433 barrel systems and 3,547 MLRS). It planned a 10-day campaign. Logistics and C2 failed, and the force was too small for the frontage [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **e2:** Russia reverted to what it does well: rail-fed massed artillery, a 10:1 fire advantage and slow city-by-city advances. Volunteers took the brunt of Russian dead from mid-2022 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
- **e3:** After Kharkiv exposed the manpower shortage, Russia mobilised 300,000, pulled back behind the Dnipro and began building the Zaporizhzhia lines. It started a war economy from May 2022 [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The Iranian Shahed arrived in Aug–Sep 2022 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **e4:** **Wagner and the convict conveyor.** Prisoners were the largest category of named dead by Mar 2023, and 88% of Wagner's Bakhmut dead were convicts. Trained Wagner troops lost only about 40–60 a week, and convicts were treated as disposable fixing forces [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count]. The army moved back from BTGs to divisions and regiments, padded with Storm-Z units [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **e5:** A textbook prepared defence. Three belts, regiments rotated to stay fresh, 35 days of cached ammunition, Lancets and helicopters, and early glide bombs [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Wagner mutinied in June 2023 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]; its fate after the mutiny is left to the manpower file.
- **e6:** Industrial-scale glide bombs (about 40k UMPK in 2024). DPRK shells shored up ammunition supply, and Russia's shell advantage was 5:1 or more. Shahed volume grew but early interception rates were high [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement] [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **e7:** A **volunteer/contract model** replaces mobilization. Recruitment was 400k+ in 2024 and was expected to keep exceeding losses of 1,200+ casualties a day. Bonus structures are left to the manpower file. Volunteers are again the largest category of Russian dead from Sep 2024. North Korean troops appear in Kursk. Russia's armoured-vehicle stocks from storage were running down [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
- **e8:** Drone-centric, infantry-infiltration warfare. Fibre-optic FPVs, 50,000+ Shaheds a year and 70k UMPK ordered. Up to half of Russia's artillery ammunition may be North Korean (Ukrainian intel claim). Migrants and vulnerable groups are coerced into service [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement] [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].
- **e9:** The loss of Starlink and throttled Telegram hurt Russian C2 [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-starlink]. Russia's planned spring–summer offensive was pre-empted, according to Wikipedia's summary of reporting [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive]. Ukraine estimates Russian losses at 30–32k a month [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring]. The strike arsenal moves to jet Gerans (Geran-4/5) [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran], and conscription becomes year-round [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].

## 6. Game/sim relevance

- **Era loadouts as data.** Each era ID can carry an availability table of Ukrainian systems, unlocked at their historical dates:
  - e1: Javelin/NLAW/TB2.
  - e2: GMLRS.
  - e4/e5: Western MBTs, Patriot, Storm Shadow; ATACMS from Oct 2023.
  - e7: F-16; ATACMS strikes into Russia.
  - e8/e9: mass interceptor drones.

  Supply rates can follow Kiel-derived curves, with scripted **aid shocks**: the late-2023 to early-2024 drought and the Mar 2025 pause [src:kiel-ukraine-support-tracker] [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-aid-resume].
- **Fire-ratio and ammunition sliders.** An artillery-advantage parameter could run 10:1 (e2), 5:1 (e4/e6) and far lower once drones dominate. Ammunition could be tracked per gun per day, from 70 down to 10 rounds. Together these give a direct lever for "shell famine" periods [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka].
- **Russian manpower conveyor.** Model Russian force generation per era: BTGs (e1), then mobilization (e3), then the convict pipeline (e4), then contract recruitment (e7+) of about 33k a month, derived from 400k+ a year. Casualties are a cost the AI accepts, and units rotate at about 30% losses [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
- **Kill-zone depth as a battlefield-transparency variable.** Grow it over time, from near zero in e1 to 3 km of full transparency and 15 km of observation by early 2025. It changes the cost of movement, rotation and logistics (for example, 50% of Ukrainian casualties are taken in the rear) [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Strategic air-war layer.** Weekly Shahed volume (75 rising to 900 a week, 50k a year) against interception rates (77–97%). Interceptor-drone production (10k/month) is a Ukrainian investment. A jet-Geran share is a Russian tech upgrade that lowers interception in e9 [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran] [src:wikipedia-sting-drone] [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors].
- **Scripted political and C2 events.** Russian Starlink loss (a Russian C2 debuff from 5 Feb 2026), holiday truces, negotiation rounds, and PURL/EU-loan funding tranches can all be timeline events that change modifiers rather than hard-coded outcomes [src:wikipedia-2026-ukrainian-counteroffensive] [src:wikipedia-april-2026-truce] [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026].

## 7. Terms

- **BTG** — battalion tactical group; Russia's 2022 combined-arms manoeuvre unit, largely dropped for regiments and divisions by 2023.
- **ZSU (Zbroini Syly Ukrainy)** — Armed Forces of Ukraine (AFU).
- **TrO (Terytorialna oborona)** — Territorial Defence Forces.
- **SBU** — Security Service of Ukraine (ran Spiderweb).
- **HUR (Holovne upravlinnia rozvidky)** — Defence Intelligence of Ukraine.
- **Syly bezpilotnykh system (SBS)** — Unmanned Systems Forces.
- **LBZ (liniia boiovoho zitknennia)** — line of contact.
- **sira zona** — "grey zone", contested no-man's-land between positions.
- **kill zone / zona urazhennia** — the drone-saturated band near the front where any movement is observed and struck.
- **shturmovyky** — assault infantry.
- **miasni shturmy** — "meat assaults": Russian waves of expendable infantry (Wagner/Storm-Z style).
- **Storm-Z / Storm-V** — Russian penal assault units made up of convicts.
- **poias fortets** — "fortress belt": Sloviansk–Kramatorsk–Druzhkivka–Kostiantynivka.
- **KAB / UMPK** — Russian guided aerial bomb / the glide-and-correction kit that turns FAB dumb bombs into glide bombs.
- **Shahed / Geran-2 / Geran-3/4/5 / Gerbera** — Iranian-designed one-way attack drone and its Russian-built versions: piston engine, then jet; Gerbera is a decoy.
- **mopedy** — "mopeds": Ukrainian slang for Shaheds, after their engine noise.
- **perekhoplyuvach** — interceptor (drone), e.g. Sting.
- **FPV** — first-person-view kamikaze drone; **optovolokno** — fibre-optic-guided FPV, immune to RF jamming.
- **NRK (nazemni robotyzovani kompleksy)** — ground robotic systems / UGVs.
- **bavovna** — "cotton": ironic Ukrainian slang for explosions on Russian-held targets (from a Russian euphemism for blasts). This is the root of the game's name.
- **HIMARS / GMLRS** — US wheeled rocket launcher / its guided 227 mm rocket.
- **ATACMS** — US tactical ballistic missile fired from HIMARS/M270.
- **Ramstein / UDCG** — the Ukraine Defense Contact Group donor forum.
- **PURL** — NATO's Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List: Europeans pay for US weapons (from Jul 2025).
- **Rubikon** — Russian elite Centre for Advanced Unmanned Technologies (drone interdiction unit); detail left to the drones file.
