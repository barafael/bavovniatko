## 1. Overview

The full-scale war (24 Feb 2022 onward) has gone through three broad movements:

1. **Manoeuvre and shock, 2022 (e1–e3).** Russia's plan for a quick decapitation fails near Kyiv. Russian massed artillery grinds forward in Donbas in summer. Ukraine then exploits an overstretched Russian force in Kharkiv Oblast and levers Russia out of right-bank Kherson. Russia answers with mobilization, annexation claims and a long-range strike campaign using Iranian-designed Shaheds [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
2. **Attrition and the failed breakthrough, 2023 to mid-2024 (e4–e6).** Wagner and convict "meat assaults" take Bakhmut. Ukraine's Western-equipped 2023 offensive stalls in about 30 km of prepared Russian defences. Then, while US aid is stalled, Russia uses its shell and glide-bomb superiority to take Avdiivka [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:euromaidanpress-2024-tril-avdiivka].
3. **The drone-dominated war, mid-2024 to 2026 (e7–e9).** Tactical UAVs become the main killer, and the "kill zone" makes manoeuvre very costly [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russia buys slow gains at huge cost: about 3,600 km² in 2024 and about 4,300–4,800 km² in 2025, depending on the tracker [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025]. Ukraine raids Kursk, strikes deep (Spiderweb), and scales interceptor drones against about 50,000 Shaheds a year. From late January 2026 it turns local counterattacks into real gains, helped by Russia's loss of Starlink, and by September ISW assesses that Russia has made "near-zero net gains since March 2026" [src:pravda-2026-romanenko-dshv-745] [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].

Across all eras, the aid picture shifts from US-led gifting (2022–2024), through the US drawdown and the two brief 2025 pauses, to European-financed support and European purchases of US weapons via PURL (2025–2026) [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years] [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026] [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance]. From March 2026 the US–Iran war competes for the same Patriot and ATACMS stocks (e9) [src:cnn-2026-cohen-interceptors-depleted]. As of late Sep 2026 there is no durable ceasefire. Russia still demands all of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts, neutrality and demilitarization, and Ukraine refuses [src:csmonitor-2026-weir-peace-talks] [src:isw-2026-09-06-roca].

**Occupied territory, rough anchors:**

- End-2025: just over 19% (DeepState) to about 20% or 120,000 km² (CSIS) [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].

## 2. Prelude 2014–2021 (background only)

- **Feb–Mar 2014:** The Revolution of Dignity (Euromaidan) ends with Yanukovych fleeing. Russian troops without insignia occupy Crimea, and Russia annexes it after an illegitimate "referendum" [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Apr–Sep 2014:** Russian-backed forces seize towns in Donbas, and the "DPR/LPR" are proclaimed. MH17 is shot down in July. At **Ilovaisk** (Aug–Sep) regular Russian troops intervene directly and defeat Ukrainian forces [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Feb 2015:** **Debaltseve** and the **Minsk II** agreements. The line then freezes into trench warfare, with ceasefire violations but little change in territory. About 14,000 people were killed in 2014–2021 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **2018–2019:** the first US Javelin sales (210 missiles in 2018, 150 more in 2019) give Ukraine a modern anti-armour capability before the invasion [src:state-2025-us-security-cooperation-ukraine].
- **2021:** Russian forces mass on Ukraine's borders. This sets up the Feb 2022 invasion [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].
- **Relevance for the sim** (background, not separately sourced): by 2022 Ukraine had eight years of combat-experienced brigades (ATO/JFO veterans) and a partly NATO-trained officer corps. Russia had an occupied Donbas base for its forces and had already set up the "DPR/LPR" militia corps.

## 3. Per-era notes

### e1-invasion (24 Feb – early Apr 2022)

**Key battles and operations.** A multi-axis invasion from Belarus, Russia and Crimea hits Kyiv (Hostomel, Bucha, Irpin), Chernihiv, Sumy, Kharkiv, and the south (Kherson taken, Mariupol besieged). Russia planned a roughly 10-day operation and expected occupation by August 2022. Its deception worked, but it had no contingency plan when speed failed [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. By early April Russia had abandoned the attempt on Kyiv and the north [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. The Mariupol siege ran on into May (e2).

**What Ukraine had.** Pre-war stocks, plus rapid Western light anti-armour and MANPADS deliveries (Javelin, NLAW, Stinger) and Bayraktar TB2s. US deliveries over the whole war reached more than 10,000 Javelins, 10,000 TOWs and 3,000 Stingers, but the fact sheet gives totals rather than arrival dates [src:state-2025-us-security-cooperation-ukraine]. Ukraine kept **artillery parity for about the first six weeks**, until its ammunition ran short [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. "From April, the West became Ukraine's strategic depth" [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. The first Western 155 mm guns arrived at the very end of the era: Canada announced its M777s delivered on 23 Apr 2022, "in conjunction with our American Allies" [src:ukrinform-2022-canada-m777].

**What Russia had.** A **12:1 force ratio north of Kyiv**. It began with 2,433 barrel artillery systems against Ukraine's 1,176, and 3,547 MLRS against 1,680. Its main manoeuvre unit was the battalion tactical group (BTG) [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons].

**Defining features.** Road-bound Russian columns, weak Russian logistics and C2, and a Ukrainian territorial-defence surge. UAS attrition was extreme: about 90% of drones used were lost [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Talks in Belarus and Antalya/Istanbul (from 28 Feb 2022) halted over disagreements and the Bucha massacre [src:wikipedia-peace-negotiations].

### e2-donbas-artillery (May – Aug 2022)

**Key battles and operations.** Mariupol falls (May). Russia's Donbas offensive takes **Sievierodonetsk** (June) and then Lysychansk [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. Ukraine's Kherson operation begins on **29 Aug 2022**, which is the hinge into e3 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Ukraine had.** By summer 2022 Ukraine had used up its own stocks of most critical munitions and depended on partners [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **155 mm tube artillery.** The US delivered 187+ M777s from April 2022 onward, with Excalibur guided shells. Its total of 155 mm howitzers eventually passed 200 [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine] [src:state-2025-us-security-cooperation-ukraine].
- **HIMARS.** The first four were in Ukraine by 24 Jun 2022 and a second four were due by mid-July [src:kyivindependent-2022-himars-arrive]. British M270s followed from July [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].
- **Effect.** Western precision fires hit Russian logistics in **June–July 2022** and took away Russia's advantage of massed artillery supplied from rail-fed depots [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** By June, a **10:1 advantage in volume of fire** [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons]. Most Ukrainian losses in 2022 came during this Donbas offensive and the Kherson push, and they fell mainly on the professional brigades [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Defining features.** A slow, artillery-heavy Russian advance city by city, with very heavy Ukrainian casualties. The original invasion force proved too small to cover the whole front, which set up e3 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e3-counteroffensives-22 (Sep – Nov 2022)

**Key battles and operations.**
- **Kharkiv counteroffensive** from **6 Sep 2022**: Balakliia (8 Sep), Izium and Kupiansk (10 Sep), Lyman (1 Oct). Ukraine claimed about 12,000 km² and 500+ settlements. ISW assessed about 2,500 km² for the breakthrough phase alone [src:wikipedia-2022-kharkiv-counteroffensive]. The Russian Western Group of Forces collapsed [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Kherson:** Ukrainian fire control over the Dnipro crossings forced an orderly Russian withdrawal from the right bank in Oct–Nov 2022. Kherson city was liberated in November [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].

**What Ukraine had.** A small pool of ready reserve brigades. The rest of the force was fixed on the line of contact [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The success spurred the Ramstein pledging process, but agreeing what to gift took about three months, and heavy-armour pledges came only in Jan 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Partial **mobilization declared 21 Sep 2022**, with a target of 300,000, followed by a mass exodus of draft-age men [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization]. Russia claimed to **annex** four partly occupied oblasts on 30 Sep [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. The first Iranian Shahed-136/131 shipment arrived in mid-Aug 2022, and the first Shahed was recovered in Ukraine on 13 Sep 2022 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].

**Defining features.** The last large Ukrainian manoeuvre successes. Russia stabilised by trading space (the Dnipro as a barrier), mobilising, and building fortifications in Zaporizhzhia that would decide e5 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e4-bakhmut (Dec 2022 – May 2023)

**Key battles and operations.** A Russian winter offensive from late Jan 2023 began at **Vuhledar**, where Russia lost dozens of armoured vehicles (Reuters via RUSI), and spread along the eastern front with its main weight on **Bakhmut** [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Bakhmut fell in May 2023 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **Western armour** was pledged in Jan 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
  - Leopard 2A4s arrived in February–March 2023 and German 2A6s in March, with Challenger 2s from March [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
  - Bradleys arrived from April 2023. The US total passed 300 [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine] [src:state-2025-us-security-cooperation-ukraine].
- **Patriot.** Crews were trained in early 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The first US and German batteries arrived in April [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine], and the Air Force put a second battery on combat duty on 27 Apr 2023 [src:ukrinform-2023-second-patriot].
- **Storm Shadow** arrived before May 2023 [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].
- **Bakhmut costs.** Around Bakhmut about 10,000 Ukrainian troops were killed or severely wounded. From February, Russia had artillery control of the city's supply roads [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Wagner plus mobilised convicts, with VDV support. **19,547 Wagner fighters killed in Bakhmut**, 88% of them convicts, and a similar number seriously wounded. Russia also held a **5:1 artillery advantage** [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. By Mar 2023, prisoners were the largest category in Mediazona's count of named Russian dead [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].

**Defining features.** Russia "traded lives for time" while it built its southern defences. Ukraine debated whether holding Bakhmut was worth it, the fear being that losing it early would expose Chasiv Yar and Kramatorsk [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Lancet loitering munitions emerge as a key Russian precision tool. By e5 they were "scouring" the narrow Ukrainian breaches [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e5-counteroffensive-23 (Jun – Nov 2023)

**Key battles and operations.** Supporting attacks at Bakhmut began in mid-May. The main **Orikhiv–Tokmak** axis opened on **3 Jun 2023**. Meteorit line charges were used to clear lanes through minefields [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The **Kakhovka dam** was destroyed in the first week, and the **Wagner mutiny** followed in late June [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]. A breakthrough was likeliest in early August. By mid-September the goals were tactical, and **Krynky** (across the Dnipro) was the last attempt [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Over 2023 as a whole the line barely moved: Ukraine lost about 540 km² and regained about 430 km² (DeepState) [src:euromaidan-2025-hrudka-2024-3600].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **Force.** The plan called for 12 armoured and mechanised brigades on a 30 km frontage, Tokmak isolated in 7 days, then Melitopol. In the event only a couple of brigades were properly equipped, and most were newly raised and undertrained [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Fires.** At most 55 guns of 155 mm fired on the main axis, at about **70 rounds/gun/day at peak and as low as 10**. There were about **7 GMLRS a day** for the whole theatre [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **Missiles.**
  - Over **300 Storm Shadow/SCALP** arrived during the offensive, SCALP from July [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].
  - **ATACMS** were first used on **17 Oct 2023**: the 165 km cluster-munition variant, delivered secretly days earlier, against the Berdiansk and Luhansk airfields [src:isw-2023-10-17-roca].
- **Late arrivals.**
  - The first **Abrams** arrived on 23 Sep 2023 (confirmed by Zelensky on 25 Sep), out of 31 in total. They came too late for the offensive [src:isw-2023-09-25-roca] [src:state-2025-us-security-cooperation-ukraine].
  - M117 demining vehicles were usable only from Oct 2023 [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**What Russia had.** Three defence lines about **30 km deep**, held by the 58th CAA (the 7th VDV and 42nd MR divisions forward). Up to **105,000 troops, about 470 tanks, up to 1,410 AFVs, 720+ guns, up to 230 MLRS and 12 operational-tactical missile complexes** were available on the axis. There were 35 days of ammunition cached at the guns, about 60 attack helicopters, and "dozens of glide bombs per day" [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

**Defining features.** Minefields, drones and Lancets, and precision fires made breaching columns predictable and targetable. Poor operational security meant Russia knew the axis and the timing. RUSI concluded that renewed Ukrainian offensives would not be viable "in the foreseeable future" [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].

### e6-avdiivka-attrition (Oct 2023 – Jul 2024)

*Overlaps e5 in Oct–Nov 2023: Avdiivka opened on 10 Oct 2023 while Krynky was still under way.*

**Sub-phases.** The era splits cleanly at the US supplemental of April 2024. After that Congress made no significant new funding available, but the FY2024 drawdown authority of USD 7.8bn restarted deliveries [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance].
- **e6a, Oct 2023–Apr 2024: Avdiivka and the aid drought.** The shell famine is at its worst.
- **e6b, May–Jul 2024: post-supplemental.** The Kharkiv/Vovchansk push is contained, Patriot numbers grow (a third German battery was pledged in April and all three had arrived by July) and the Pokrovsk drive begins [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].

**Key battles and operations.**
- **Avdiivka**, from 10 Oct 2023 until Syrskyi announced the withdrawal on the evening of 17 Feb 2024. The Tavria commander claimed almost 50,000 Russian losses in the battle (Ukrainian claim) [src:euromaidanpress-2024-tril-avdiivka].
- After that, Russia began its slow drive west towards Pokrovsk, covering about 50 km from Feb 2024 to Jan 2026 at about **70 m/day** [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- The **northern Kharkiv offensive** from 10 May 2024 (Vovchansk, Lyptsi) stalled at about 10 km depth. On 30 May 2024 Washington allowed US weapons to be used against targets in Russia near Kharkiv [src:wikipedia-2024-kharkiv-offensive].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **A shell famine.** Troops reported a **5:1** Russian advantage in early Feb 2024, and 8–9:1 at worst, blamed on delayed US and EU packages [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka].
- **Effective air defence against Shaheds:** **77% interception in Feb 2024 and 97% in May 2024** [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **Fast-growing FPV use.** By late 2024, tactical UAVs accounted for **60–70% of damaged or destroyed Russian systems**, even though 60–80% of FPVs missed [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**What Russia had.**
- **Mass UMPK glide bombs.** The UK MoD counted 30–50 guided munitions a day by 5 Feb 2024, or about 600 in four weeks [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka]. About **40,000 UMPK were produced in 2024**, launched from 30–90 km behind the line [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Manpower.** Russia contracted **400,000+ troops in 2024** [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **North Korean shells.** The OSC counted about 4 million shells shipped Sep 2023 to Apr 2025 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].

**Defining features.** Russia's firepower-driven attrition against a Ukraine short of ammunition. Glide bombs "gave the VKS teeth" without air superiority [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

### e7-kursk-pokrovsk (Aug 2024 – Mar 2025)

**Key battles and operations.**
- The **Kursk incursion** began on **6 Aug 2024**. Zelensky claimed 1,250 km² and 92 settlements on 19 Aug [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].
- The Russian Pokrovsk/Kurakhove drive continued. Russia took Velyka Novosilka by early 2025 and pushed towards the Dnipropetrovsk border [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Russian gains in 2024 totalled about 3,600 km², up to 20 km² a day in November [src:euromaidan-2025-hrudka-2024-3600].
- Ukraine withdrew from **Sudzha on 12 Mar 2025** [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **F-16s**, confirmed in Ukraine on 4 Aug 2024, with at least 79 pledged [src:kyivindependent-2024-court-f16]. Oryx counts about 50 delivered from the Netherlands, Denmark and Norway, with 53 Belgian jets due from late 2026 to 2030 [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine]. The first Dutch F-16s arrived on 6 Feb 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-mirage-arrive].
- **Mirage 2000-5s.** The first arrived on 6 Feb 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-mirage-arrive] and they first flew against a Russian strike on 7 Mar 2025 [src:kyivindependent-2025-zadorozhnyy-mirage-combat]. France has not given the total, and Oryx counts 4+ delivered [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].
- **ATACMS strikes inside Russia** from 19 Nov 2024 [src:kyivindependent-2024-york-atacms-kursk].
- **More armour.** Australia pledged 49 M1A1 Abrams in Oct 2024, all delivered by Dec 2025 [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine].
- **Force size.** About 800,000 personnel, of which under 25% was usable combat power, holding a 1,200 km line with local force ratios of 1:2 to 1:6 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- An expanding deep-strike campaign against Russian refineries and depots [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**What Russia had.**
- About **580,000 troops** in the operational group: about 70,000 in Kursk with about 10,000 North Koreans, 80,000 on the Kharkiv axis, 230,000 in Donetsk/Luhansk and about 140,000 in the south [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- Losses of **more than 200 killed a day in 2024, and over 1,200 casualties a day** by late 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. ISW's figure for Sep–Nov 2024 works out at 53 casualties per km² gained [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- DPRK troops from Nov 2024 (about 11–12k at first). They fought in company or battalion-sized assaults, and Ukrainian troops described them as fit and cohesive [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive] [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- Shahed/Geran launches rose from about **75 to about 900 a week from Sep 2024** [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
- **Fibre-optic FPVs** appear, which are immune to RF jamming [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].

**Defining features.** Ukraine traded a strategic gamble (Kursk as leverage for negotiations) against Russian gains in Donetsk [src:kyivindependent-2024-york-atacms-kursk]. North Korean casualty claims are contested: Ukraine says 4,500, South Korea 6,000 killed or wounded, and a BBC analysis 2,304 killed [src:wikipedia-2024-kursk-offensive].

### e8-drone-kill-zone (Mar 2025 – Jan 2026)

**Sub-phases.** The proposed split at about Aug 2025 holds up.
- **e8a, Mar–Aug 2025:** the Kursk pocket is lost, Russia makes its peak summer gains, Spiderweb, and Anchorage.
- **e8b, Sep 2025–Jan 2026:** the urban fight for Pokrovsk and Myrnohrad, the loss of Huliaipole, the Kupiansk counterattack, the 28-point plan, and Russia's Starlink-enabled mid-range drone interdiction from late December.

ISW's own figure for the six months that bracket the split is 2,403.77 km² of net Russian advances between 20 May and 20 Nov 2025 [src:isw-2026-09-08-roca].

**Key battles and operations.**
- The Kursk pocket is lost (Mar 2025).
- Russian summer 2025 gains peak at **130–150 km²/week** (Jun–Jul) [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring], or 450–550 km² a month (DeepState via Radio Svoboda) [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- A Russian thrust towards Dobropillia is halted (Aug–Sep 2025) [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi].
- Over the winter of 2025–26 Ukraine loses most of **Pokrovsk and Myrnohrad** and withdraws in a "deliberate regrouping" [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- Russia makes first inroads into Dnipropetrovsk and Sumy oblasts [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025].
- **Huliaipole falls** in late December 2025. The Russian 57th Motor Rifle Brigade infiltrated its centre on 17 Dec, against under-manned territorial-defence brigades [src:euromaidanpress-2025-axe-huliaipole].
- Ukraine retakes much of **Kupiansk** and its surroundings, about 305 km², in late December [src:isw-2026-02-15-roca].
- Russian weekly gains peak again at 141 km² in the last week of January 2026 [src:isw-2026-02-15-roca].
- **Operation Spiderweb (1 Jun 2025)**: the SBU flew 117 FPVs from trucks against five airbases. Kyiv claims over 40 strategic bombers destroyed or damaged [src:euromaidanpress-2025-tril-spiderweb]. The SBU's own tally is 41 aircraft hit, independent counts give 12–13 destroyed, and a US estimate says about 20 hit and 10 destroyed [src:wikipedia-operation-spiderweb].

**What Ukraine had.**
- A **US aid and intelligence pause** after the 28 Feb 2025 Oval Office row, lifted on 11 Mar after Jeddah [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-aid-resume]. DOD paused and resumed deliveries twice in 2025 [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance].
- New US allocations effectively ended in early 2025. Deliveries continued through **PURL** (created Jul 2025) [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years].
- European military aid in 2025 was **+67%** on the 2022–24 average, but total military aid was **13% below** it [src:kiel-2026-trebesch-four-years].
- **Interceptor drones** at scale: Wild Hornets' Sting from Apr 2025, which made the first Geran-3 kill in Dec 2025 [src:wikipedia-sting-drone].

**What Russia had.**
- **70,000 UMPK ordered for 2025** [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Over 50,000 Shaheds launched in 2025**, about 5 times the previous year [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. Domestic production of the jet Geran-3 began in early 2025 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **Recruitment:** 403,000 recruited in 2025, meeting the plan in early December (HUR claim). The 2026 plan is 409,000 [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k].
- North Korea: Ukrainian intelligence says it supplied up to 50% of Russia's 122/152 mm needs by end-2025, plus 148 KN-23/24 missiles by early 2025 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].
- Coercive recruitment of migrants and raids [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].

**Defining features.**
- A deepening drone "kill zone". By early 2025 the battlefield was transparent within 3 km and watched out to 15 km [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Later deepening and the elite Russian drone centre **Rubikon** are left to the drone topic file (see §8).
- Russian assaults shift further towards small-group infantry infiltration. Section-size attacks were already the norm in 2024 [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- 2025 gains were **4,336 km² (DeepState) or 4,831 km² (CSIS)**, under 1% of Ukraine [src:kyivindependent-2026-basmat-deepstate-2025] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts]. Russia paid about 85 casualties per km² gained [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- **Diplomacy:**
  - Jeddah and the 30-day ceasefire offer (Mar 2025) [src:kyivindependent-2025-hodunova-aid-resume].
  - The Trump–Putin **Anchorage summit on 15 Aug 2025** produced no ceasefire. Trump dropped his ceasefire demand and pressed Kyiv to give up the Donbas "fortress belt" before Zelensky's 18 Aug White House visit [src:euromaidanpress-2025-shandra-alaska].
  - A **28-point US plan "very favorable to Russia"** followed in Nov 2025 (ISW's words), then a US–Ukrainian–European **20-point plan** in Dec 2025. The Kremlin rejected both as insufficient [src:isw-2026-09-08-roca] [src:isw-2026-02-05-roca].
  - The first Abu Dhabi trilateral round was on 23–24 Jan 2026 [src:wikipedia-peace-negotiations].

### e9-counteroffensive-26 (late Jan 2026 – present, Sep 2026)

**Sub-phases.** The best split is at about Jun/Jul 2026, rather than at Vivaldi, which began in late spring.
- **e9a, late Jan–May 2026: the southern counterattacks.** Russia loses Starlink, Ukraine retakes 400+ km² on the Oleksandrivka axis by March, Russian net gains drop to near zero, and there are two holiday truces.
- **e9b, Jun–Sep 2026: strike war and a new command.** The jet-Geran surge, the Patriot shortage caused by the Iran war, the replacement of the defence minister and the commander-in-chief (16–21 Jul), the 745 km² claim for the Oleksandrivka offensive (Aug), Operation Vivaldi made public (Sep), and envoy diplomacy.

**The Starlink cutoff and the southern counterattacks.**
- **The Starlink cutoff was phased, from late January to 5 Feb 2026.** Topic 05 §3 has the detail.
  - On 29 Jan the MoD contacted SpaceX [src:mod-2026-spacex-starlink-russian-uavs].
  - A speed cap of about 75–90 km/h hit Starlink-equipped Russian drones from late January [src:twz-2026-altman-rogoway-starlink-scramble].
  - On 1 Feb Musk said SpaceX had taken "effective" steps against Russian use at Ukraine's request. This is ISW's "1 Feb" [src:isw-2026-02-01-roca].
  - On 2 Feb a Cabinet resolution created the whitelist [src:kyivindependent-2026-myronyshena-starlink-whitelist].
  - Enforcement came at about 03:00 Kyiv time on 5 Feb, when Fedorov said Russian terminals could no longer operate [src:mod-2026-starlink-whitelist-operational] [src:isw-2026-02-05-roca].
  - Russian Starlink-guided mid-range drone strikes had begun in late Dec 2025, which probably explains United24's "Dec 2025" date for the cutoff [src:isw-2026-02-01-roca].
  - General Staff sources said Russia cut, and in places halted, its assaults afterwards [src:isw-2026-02-06-roca].
  - Russian **Telegram** throttling followed from 9 Feb, and ISW judged the two together degraded Russian C2 [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment] [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-starlink].
- **The Starlink cutoff helped the counterattacks but did not cause them.**
  - The Air Assault Forces (DShV) date their Oleksandrivka-axis offensive from **29 Jan 2026**. That is the day the MoD first contacted SpaceX, a week before the whitelist was enforced [src:pravda-2026-romanenko-dshv-745]. Zelensky says it was carried out "exactly as it was planned" [src:united24-2026-kohanets-745].
  - ISW first saw "limited and localized" counterattacks on the Dnipropetrovsk–Zaporizhzhia border on 9 Feb. It called them "opportunistic" and "likely" exploiting the Starlink and Telegram blocks. A senior NATO official credited the cutoff for "at least some" of the gains [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment].
  - Kostiantyn Mashovets dated the Russian stall from about 8 Feb, with Ukrainian push-backs of 9–9.5 km along the Yanchur and Haichur rivers. At that point Ukrainian officials still denied it was a "counteroffensive" [src:isw-2026-02-15-roca].
  - The start date therefore ranges from 29 Jan (DShV's own dating) to 9 Feb (the first open-source observation). The 11 Feb date came only from Wikipedia and is dropped.
- **Area claims.** How much ground was retaken depends on who is counting and on whether grey-zone clearing counts as "liberation". Syrskyi preferred "restored control" [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-syrskyi-march] [src:kyivindependent-2026-fenbert-300km].
  - 300 km² (Zelensky, 20 Feb) [src:kyivindependent-2026-fenbert-300km].
  - 435 km² (Zelensky, 8 Mar), with 285.6 km² for one Air Assault grouping alone (Syrskyi, 9 Mar). Syrskyi called it the first month since Kursk 2024 in which Ukraine regained more than it lost [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-syrskyi-march].
  - **745 km² and 26 settlements** by 12 Aug (12 in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, 10 in Donetsk and 4 in Zaporizhzhia), plus 300+ km² of rear areas cleared of infiltrators (DShV) [src:pravda-2026-romanenko-dshv-745] [src:united24-2026-kohanets-745].
  - Zelensky claims 9,550 Russians killed in the Jan–Aug operation [src:united24-2026-kohanets-745].
  - Smaller pushes such as 46 km² near Novoselivka (29 May, DeepState) continued through the spring [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-46km].
  - The Kyiv Independent cautioned that most advances visible in open sources were extended clearing operations in a wide grey zone rather than breaches of Russian defence lines [src:kyivindependent-2026-fenbert-300km].
  - ISW later called the Oleksandrivka effort a counteroffensive [src:isw-2026-09-08-roca].

**Operation Vivaldi, near Lyman.**
- **Who and how.** Run by the 3rd Army Corps under Biletskyi, whose units include the 60th Mechanised Brigade, the 125th Heavy Mechanised Brigade, and a battalion tactical group of the 3rd Assault Brigade [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi] [src:hromadske-2026-lisova-vivaldi-phase1]. Russian and OSINT sources tracked the attacks from late spring, and the corps revealed the operation only on 15 Sep [src:kyivindependent-2026-york-vivaldi-gains].
- **Phase 1, announced 15 Sep:**
  - 75 km² cleared of infiltrators plus 10 km² liberated (Novoselivka, Oleksandrivka, Yarova and Serednie).
  - Claimed losses against the Russian 20th and 25th armies: about 1,500 men and 12,000+ drones [src:hromadske-2026-lisova-vivaldi-phase1].
- **Phase 2, announced 20 Sep:**
  - A further 40 km²: Shandryholove and Derylove liberated and Drobysheve fully held.
  - The corps says the "northern pincer" on the fortress belt was "routed" (17 Sep) and Russia pulled Rubikon units out after heavy losses [src:kyivindependent-2026-york-vivaldi-gains].
- **Totals and estimates.** The corps claims 125 km², including 50 km² previously occupied, and 3,000+ Russian losses (unverified) [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi]. Analysts estimate more than 200 km², and mappers' estimates reach about 240 km² (see 05) [src:hromadske-2026-lisova-vivaldi-phase1] [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi]. ISW said its method underestimates the gains and declined to assess them on 8 Sep [src:isw-2026-09-08-roca].
- **Method (ISW, 26 Sep).**
  - Ukrainian forces first struck the ATV and motorcycle reinforcements sent to stranded Russian infiltrators, then destroyed the depleted groups.
  - They reached drone and EW positions deep in the Russian rear, helped by Russian commanders' exaggerated reports of their own advances.
  - Russian sources speak of withdrawals from southern Lyman to the Zherebets [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **Russia's response.** Russia shifted south towards **Mykolaivka and Sloviansk** and claimed to have encircled Dobropillia (22 Sep), which ISW did not confirm [src:euromaidanpress-2026-thomas-vivaldi].

**The front at the end of September.**
- **ISW's measure.** Between 8 Mar and 8 Sep 2026 Russia lost a net 195.19 km² of ground it had advanced into. Only if infiltration zones are counted does it show a net gain (361.47 km²) [src:isw-2026-09-08-roca]. ISW's summary is "near-zero net gains since March 2026" [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
- **DeepState's measure.** Russian gains fell to 36 km² in July 2026, which is DeepState's figure and 07's computation. Radio Svoboda's 27 km² looks wrong (see 07 §2.7) [src:deepstate-api-history] [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km]. DeepState publishes Ukrainian recaptures late, so Vivaldi's May–Jun gains show up only in its Aug–Sep versions.
- **Putin's deadline** for taking all of Donetsk Oblast is 31 Dec 2026, and ISW judges Russia to be failing against it [src:isw-2026-09-06-roca].

**Command change.** Defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov was sacked in mid-July, which set off street protests. Special-operations commander Yevhen Khmara became acting minister on 16 Jul [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-khmara]. On 21 Jul Maj Gen **Mykhailo Drapatyi** replaced Syrskyi as Commander-in-Chief. Zelensky said the mid- and long-range strike programmes would continue unchanged [src:guardian-2026-murray-drapatyi].

**What Ukraine had.**
- **Funding.** A European-financed pipeline: the **EUR 90 bn EU Ukraine Support Loan (2026–27)** and about EUR 11 bn allocated in May–Jun 2026 [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026]. PURL reached USD 6.3 bn announced and USD 4.8 bn received by June 2026 [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance].
- **Continued dependence on US systems.** **30% of European military aid is bought from US firms**, over 90% of PURL comes from US stocks, and Patriot remains critical [src:kiel-2026-nishikawa-h1-2026].
- **A Patriot shortage.**
  - Russia now fires about three times as many ballistic missiles a month as a year earlier, against a Patriot supply squeezed by the Iran war [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-khmara].
  - Ukraine is contracting about 1,000 Patriot missiles, but most arrive in later years. At Ramstein on 8 Sep it asked allies to release stocks now, and only Germany did [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-patriot-1000].
  - ISW expects Russia to exploit the gap over the winter [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
  - Trump raised Patriot missile coproduction in July 2026 and then hedged [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance].
- **Future fighters.** Sweden pledged 16 Gripen C/D and 20 Gripen E/F in May 2026, the E/F bought with the EU loan [src:oryx-heavy-weaponry-supplied-ukraine]. Kyiv wants Meteor missiles with them to push KAB carriers back [src:euromaidanpress-2026-mukhina-khmara].
- **Interceptor drones at mass scale.**
  - **Sting at 10,000+/month (Mar 2026)** and **9,000+ Shaheds downed by Jun 2026** [src:wikipedia-sting-drone].
  - Jet drones are harder: Zelensky puts interception at **57% for jet drones against 70% overall** (28 Sep), and new interceptors are in combat testing [src:united24-2026-popilnichenko-jet-57] [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors].

**What Russia had.**
- **Jet Gerans:** about 180 launched in 2025 but about **1,400 by early Jun 2026**. 15–20% of launches in Apr 2026 had jet engines, and the Geran-4 cruises at 350–500 km/h [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors]. The Geran-3 was retired by Aug 2026 in favour of the Geran-4/5 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **Slowing gains.** Russian gains slowed to **33 km²/week** by mid-Feb 2026 [src:ukrinform-2026-liskovych-front-spring].
- **Losses.** The General Staff counts about 32,800 Russian losses a month in Feb–Jun 2026, and about 290 per km² gained [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- **Manpower.**
  - Year-round conscription from 2026 [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].
  - A 409,000 recruitment plan (HUR) [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k].
  - Zelensky warns of a 300–500k mobilisation after the September Duma elections, which the Kremlin denies [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- More DPRK missiles, including a reported new Hwasong-11C batch in Aug 2026 [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].

**The US–Iran war and Ukraine.**
- **Timing.** US–Israeli strikes on Iran began at the end of February 2026 and the war was in full swing by early March [src:kyivpost-2026-struck-feshchenko-gulf].
- **Stocks.** By early August the US had used about half its Patriot interceptors, nearly 80% of THAAD and "virtually all" ATACMS and PrSM. CSIS estimated about 2,200 modern Patriot rounds before the war [src:cnn-2026-cohen-interceptors-depleted]. NATO and European officials fear procurement delays for Ukraine [src:crs-2026-if12040-us-security-assistance].
- **Ukraine's help.** CENTCOM asked for Ukrainian help, and about 200 specialists went to the Gulf to help defend against Iranian drones [src:kyivpost-2026-struck-feshchenko-gulf].
- **Oil money for Russia.** Higher oil prices from the Hormuz crisis gave Russia an estimated USD 1.3–1.9 bn in extra export-tax revenue within two weeks (FT via Kyiv Post) [src:kyivpost-2026-struck-feshchenko-oil-windfall].

**Peace talks and ceasefires.**
- **Abu Dhabi, second round (4–5 Feb 2026):** a 314-person exchange and no ceasefire. A short energy-strike moratorium was in force at the time [src:isw-2026-02-05-roca].
- **Geneva (17–18 Feb):** talks failed on Donbas [src:kyivindependent-2026-fenbert-300km].
- **Orthodox Easter truce (11–13 Apr 2026):** 32 hours from 16:00 on 11 Apr. The General Staff logged 10,721 Russian violations. Long-range strikes paused for about a day, but artillery, FPVs and assaults did not [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-easter-truce]. Pass 1's figure of 469 violations, taken from Wikipedia, is superseded.
- **Victory Day.** Ukraine declared a unilateral ceasefire from 6 May, which Russia ignored [src:isw-2026-05-06-roca]. Both sides then agreed a **9–11 May** ceasefire with a POW exchange. The Kremlin restated that Ukraine must withdraw from the rest of Donetsk Oblast before any ceasefire [src:isw-2026-05-08-roca]. Limited fighting went on, and Russia used the pause to rotate and resupply [src:isw-2026-05-09-roca].
- **The envoys' visit (5–8 Sep).**
  - Kyiv and Moscow halted long-range strikes on each other's capitals from 5 to 7 Sep.
  - Ukrainian brigades were ordered to hold fire along the front from 5 to 8 Sep, a truce Ukraine proposed and Putin rejected [src:isw-2026-09-05-roca].
  - On 6 Sep Zelensky and Witkoff/Kushner agreed "fundamental principles", including security and economic guarantees, while Putin restated maximalist aims [src:isw-2026-09-06-roca].
  - Sources differ on the outcome: CSMonitor reports that Zelensky rejected the plan's terms [src:csmonitor-2026-weir-peace-talks].
- **"Energy ceasefire" (14 Sep):** Trump announced a mutual halt to energy strikes. Zelensky offered one on condition of reciprocity, and Russia did not respond [src:isw-2026-09-14-roca]. By 25 Sep Putin was signalling a winter strike campaign instead [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].

**Defining features.** For the first time since 2022, Ukraine regains the local initiative, but through drone-led, information-silent operations against infiltrators and their supply lines rather than mechanised breakthroughs [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-vivaldi] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment]. The strategic air war escalates in quality (jet Gerans, ballistic missiles against a Patriot shortage) as much as in quantity.

## 5. Russia's side as reported (Ukrainian and Western sources)

- **e1:** A pre-war professional army of BTGs with contract soldiers, VDV spearheads and huge artillery parks (2,433 barrel systems and 3,547 MLRS). It planned a 10-day campaign. Logistics and C2 failed, and the force was too small for the frontage [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **e2:** Russia reverted to what it does well: rail-fed massed artillery, a 10:1 fire advantage and slow city-by-city advances. Volunteers took the brunt of Russian dead from mid-2022 [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
- **e3:** After Kharkiv exposed the manpower shortage, Russia mobilised 300,000, pulled back behind the Dnipro and began building the Zaporizhzhia lines. It started a war economy from May 2022 [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. The Iranian Shahed arrived in Aug–Sep 2022 [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **e4:** **Wagner and the convict conveyor.**
  - Prisoners were the largest category of named dead by Mar 2023, and 88% of Wagner's Bakhmut dead were convicts [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
  - Trained Wagner troops lost only about 40–60 a week, and convicts were treated as disposable fixing forces [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
  - The army moved back from BTGs to divisions and regiments, padded with Storm-Z units [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons].
- **e5:** A textbook prepared defence. Three belts, regiments rotated to stay fresh, 35 days of cached ammunition, Lancets and helicopters, and early glide bombs [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons]. Wagner mutinied in June 2023 [src:wikipedia-russo-ukrainian-war]; its fate after the mutiny is left to the manpower file.
- **e6:** Industrial-scale glide bombs (about 40k UMPK in 2024). DPRK shells shored up ammunition supply, and Russia's shell advantage was 5:1 or more. Shahed volume grew but early interception rates were high [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement] [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran].
- **e7:** A **volunteer/contract model** replaces mobilization.
  - Recruitment was 400k+ in 2024, and was expected to keep exceeding losses of 1,200+ casualties a day [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year]. Bonus structures are left to the manpower file.
  - Volunteers are again the largest category of Russian dead from Sep 2024 [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
  - North Korean troops appear in Kursk, and Russia's armoured-vehicle stocks from storage were running down [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **e8:** Drone-centric, infantry-infiltration warfare.
  - Fibre-optic FPVs, 50,000+ Shaheds a year and 70k UMPK ordered [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts].
  - Recruitment met its 403,000 plan (HUR) [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k].
  - Up to half of Russia's artillery ammunition may be North Korean (Ukrainian intelligence claim) [src:wikipedia-north-korean-involvement].
  - Migrants and vulnerable groups are coerced into service [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].
  - Late in the era, Russian drone units used Starlink to extend mid-range strikes on the Ukrainian rear [src:isw-2026-02-01-roca].
- **e9:**
  - **C2.** The loss of Starlink and throttled Telegram hurt Russian C2 [src:isw-2026-02-12-assessment] [src:united24-2026-litnarovych-starlink].
  - **Summer offensive.** In early February Russia was struggling to seize the start lines for its planned summer 2026 offensive, towards Sloviansk–Kramatorsk and/or Orikhiv–Zaporizhzhia. The southern counterattacks set it back further [src:isw-2026-02-15-roca].
  - **Losses exceed recruitment.** Losses stay at about 32,800 a month (General Staff) while gains shrink to about 290 casualties per km² [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km]. Syrskyi and the unmanned-systems commander Brovdi say losses now outrun recruitment [src:kyivindependent-2026-farrell-syrskyi-march].
  - **"Beautiful reports."** ISW and Russian milbloggers describe a culture of inflated reports that misleads Putin and set up Vivaldi's surprise [src:isw-2026-09-06-roca] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
  - **Strike arsenal.** It moves to jet Gerans (Geran-4/5), with ballistic missiles aimed at the Patriot gap [src:militarnyi-2026-jet-geran-interceptors] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran] [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment].
  - **Conscription** becomes year-round [src:wikipedia-2022-russian-mobilization].
  - **Oil revenue.** The Iran war's oil spike cushions Russia's budget [src:kyivpost-2026-struck-feshchenko-oil-windfall].

## 6. Game/sim relevance

- **Era loadouts as data.** Each era ID can carry an availability table of Ukrainian systems, unlocked at the historical dates in the first table in §4:
  - e1: Javelin/NLAW/TB2; M777 from late April.
  - e2: HIMARS/GMLRS from late June.
  - e4/e5: Western MBTs, Bradleys, Patriot, Storm Shadow; Abrams and ATACMS from Sep–Oct 2023.
  - e7: F-16, Mirage; ATACMS strikes into Russia.
  - e8/e9: mass interceptor drones; Gripen as a pledged, not yet delivered, unlock.

  Supply rates can follow Kiel-derived curves [src:kiel-ukraine-support-tracker]. Scripted **aid shocks** would be:
  - the late-2023 to early-2024 drought, which ends with the April 2024 supplemental;
  - the two 2025 US pauses;
  - the 2026 Iran-war drain on Patriot and ATACMS.

- **Fire-ratio and ammunition sliders.** An artillery-advantage parameter could run 10:1 (e2), 5:1 (e4/e6) and far lower once drones dominate. Ammunition could be tracked per gun per day, from 70 down to 10 rounds. Together these give a direct lever for "shell famine" periods [src:rusi-2022-zabrodskyi-preliminary-lessons] [src:rusi-2024-watling-offensive-lessons] [src:wikipedia-battle-of-avdiivka].
- **Russian manpower conveyor.**
  - Force generation per era: BTGs (e1), then mobilization (e3), then the convict pipeline (e4), then contract recruitment (e7+) of about 33k a month, which matches 400–409k a year [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:euromaidan-2025-murdoch-budanov-403k].
  - Casualties are a cost the AI accepts, and units rotate at about 30% losses [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year] [src:mediazona-2026-casualties-count].
  - A **losses-per-km²** metric (53 → 85 → 290) is a ready-made calibration target for how the drone kill zone taxes the attacker [src:euromaidan-2026-tril-cost-per-km].
- **Kill-zone depth as a battlefield-transparency variable.** Grow it over time, from near zero in e1 to 3 km of full transparency and 15 km of observation by early 2025. It changes the cost of movement, rotation and logistics (for example, 50% of Ukrainian casualties are taken in the rear) [src:rusi-2025-watling-third-year].
- **Counter-infiltration as a Ukrainian mission type (e9).** Vivaldi shows the pattern: interdict the reinforcement flow of ATVs and motorcycles to infiltrators, then clear the starving groups, and treat grey-zone clearing as distinct from "liberation" when scoring [src:isw-2026-09-26-assessment] [src:kyivindependent-2026-fenbert-300km].
- **Strategic air-war layer.** Weekly Shahed volume (75 rising to 900 a week, 50k a year) against interception rates (77–97% for piston drones, 57% for jet drones in Sep 2026). Interceptor-drone production (10k/month) is a Ukrainian investment. A jet-Geran share is a Russian tech upgrade that lowers interception in e9. A Patriot stock that depletes against ballistic missiles adds a second resource race [src:csis-2026-jones-10-charts] [src:csis-2026-bondar-shahed-geran] [src:wikipedia-sting-drone] [src:united24-2026-popilnichenko-jet-57] [src:euromaidanpress-2026-zoria-patriot-1000].
- **Scripted political and C2 events.** These can all be timeline events that change modifiers rather than hard-coded outcomes:
  - the Russian Starlink loss, a Russian C2 debuff from 1–5 Feb 2026, stacked with the Telegram throttle from 9 Feb;
  - holiday truces that pause long-range strikes but not the front;
  - negotiation rounds;
  - the July 2026 command change;
  - PURL and EU-loan funding tranches.

## 7. Terms

- **BTG** — battalion tactical group; Russia's 2022 combined-arms manoeuvre unit, largely dropped for regiments and divisions by 2023.
- **ZSU (Zbroini Syly Ukrainy)** — Armed Forces of Ukraine (AFU).
- **TrO (Terytorialna oborona)** — Territorial Defence Forces.
- **DShV (Desantno-shturmovi viiska)** — Air Assault Forces; the core of the 2026 Oleksandrivka offensive.
- **SBU** — Security Service of Ukraine (ran Spiderweb).
- **HUR (Holovne upravlinnia rozvidky)** — Defence Intelligence of Ukraine.
- **Syly bezpilotnykh system (SBS)** — Unmanned Systems Forces.
- **LBZ (liniia boiovoho zitknennia)** — line of contact.
- **sira zona** — "grey zone", contested no-man's-land between positions.
- **kill zone / zona urazhennia** — the drone-saturated band near the front where any movement is observed and struck.
- **counterattack vs counteroffensive** — ISW's usage: a counterattack is a tactical action, while a counteroffensive is an operational-level undertaking like Kharkiv or Kherson in 2022. Ukrainian officials also avoided "counteroffensive" in Feb 2026.
- **"liberated" vs "restored control"** — Ukrainian official wording: the second covers grey-zone areas cleared of infiltrators rather than taken from full Russian control.
- **shturmovyky** — assault infantry.
- **miasni shturmy** — "meat assaults": Russian waves of expendable infantry (Wagner/Storm-Z style).
- **Storm-Z / Storm-V** — Russian penal assault units made up of convicts.
- **"beautiful reports" (Russian, as reported)** — Russian milblogger term for inflated reports of advances up the chain of command.
- **poias fortets** — "fortress belt": Sloviansk–Kramatorsk–Druzhkivka–Kostiantynivka.
- **KAB / UMPK** — Russian guided aerial bomb / the glide-and-correction kit that turns FAB dumb bombs into glide bombs.
- **Shahed / Geran-2 / Geran-3/4/5 / Gerbera** — Iranian-designed one-way attack drone and its Russian-built versions: piston engine, then jet; Gerbera is a decoy.
- **mopedy** — "mopeds": Ukrainian slang for Shaheds, after their engine noise.
- **perekhoplyuvach** — interceptor (drone), e.g. Sting.
- **FPV** — first-person-view kamikaze drone; **optovolokno** — fibre-optic-guided FPV, immune to RF jamming.
- **NRK (nazemni robotyzovani kompleksy)** — ground robotic systems / UGVs.
- **bavovna** — "cotton": ironic Ukrainian slang for explosions on Russian-held targets (from a Russian euphemism for blasts). This is the root of the game's name.
- **HIMARS / GMLRS** — US wheeled rocket launcher / its guided 227 mm rocket.
- **ATACMS** — US tactical ballistic missile fired from HIMARS/M270.
- **PAC-3** — the Patriot interceptor variant Ukraine relies on against Russian ballistic missiles.
- **Ramstein / UDCG** — the Ukraine Defense Contact Group donor forum.
- **PURL** — NATO's Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List: Europeans pay for US weapons (from Jul 2025).
- **Rubikon** — Russian elite Centre for Advanced Unmanned Technologies (drone interdiction unit); detail left to the drones file.
