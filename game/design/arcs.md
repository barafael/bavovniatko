# The arcs: story planning (2026-10-15)

Status: planning on paper (roadmap step 9). Nothing here is built, and nothing changes `campaign.md`'s order
until the owner decides. This document plans four arcs from the dossiers' set pieces. An arc is a run of chapters
that tell one story and share a place, a cast or a weapon. It then gives a recommendation for each of
`campaign.md`'s open questions, and follows the drones through the war, since the player's own drones are the
game's next build (roadmap step 7).

The rules from `chapters.md` hold throughout. Each chapter is a diorama that runs by itself. Winning means
reproducing what happened. The player matters at the margin, and the ledger is theirs. No god's-eye camera.

## 1. Donbas: holding, and the cost of holding (2023–2026)

**Chapters:** Bakhmut (c07), Vuhledar (c08, built), Avdiivka (c11) with the Stepove Bradley (v02), Pokrovsk and
Myrnohrad (c15).

**The story.** Four towns on the same arc of steppe, each held for months against an attacker who pays in men
for metres. Each ends in a withdrawal, the last one with no order to withdraw. Vuhledar is the exception that
proves the rule: the defence holds, and in October 2024 the town falls anyway.

**The player.**
- Bakhmut: a driver on the "road of life", then the brigade's rotation officer (`campaign.md` act 7).
- Vuhledar: the 72nd's drone operator and fire controller (built).
- Avdiivka: the gunner with no shells, the Stepove Bradley crew, the Zenit company.
- Pokrovsk: the drone line, then the road into Myrnohrad.

The cast changes. The verb stays the same: watch, decide what to spend, count what it cost.

**Beats, from the set pieces:**
1. Bakhmut, the salt town (c07 §6.2). Wagner street by street through Soledar in hard frost, and the quarrel over
   who took it. The player learns that the map lies: the town falls in the communiqués before it falls.
2. Vuhledar, the same road (c08). Columns down the same dirt roads past their own wrecks. The player learns that
   the defence works, and that it does not end the war.
3. Avdiivka, the first wave (c11 §6.1): columns of 20–40 vehicles at the salient's shoulders, 70 in a day. Then
   the terrikon (§6.2), the coke plant that never falls (§6.3), the promka ground away (§6.4), the pipe (§6.5),
   the Bradley duels (§6.6, v02), and cutting the neck in fog (§6.7). The player learns that holding is a
   resource that runs out.
4. Pokrovsk, the fourteen-day walk (c15 §6.1). Infiltrators who walk 600 m a day under cloaks. The drone line
   sees some and misses others. "The brigade ran out of infantry" (§6.2), Mad Max in the fog (§6.8), and no
   order to withdraw. The player learns that the war became drones against men on foot.

**Recovery beat.** The Stepove Bradley (v02): one crew, one tank, a decision to switch from AP to blinding fire.
It is small, clean and theirs. It sits inside Avdiivka's hold-then-withdraw.

**Levers.** These grow across the arc:
- Bakhmut: rotation and ammunition.
- Vuhledar: fires and the ATGM teams.
- Avdiivka: the shell famine, then a Bradley.
- Pokrovsk: the drone line itself.

**What it needs from the engine.**
- Towns: Bakhmut's blocks, Avdiivka's coke plant and promka. Vuhledar's panel blocks are the start.
- Infantry fighting infantry.
- A terrikon (a terrain cut).
- Fog.
- Cars and motorbikes.
- FPVs as weapons on both sides, by 2025.

## 2. Snake Island: the sea war's thesis (2022)

**Chapters:** Channel 16 and the island (c02 act 1), the blip under the clouds and the Moskva (c02 act 2), the TB2
hunts and the island given up (c02's later set pieces). The Black Sea drone war (c13) is its sequel.

**The story.** A rock, thirteen border guards and a radio. Then a cruiser that shouldn't have been seen, two
missiles, and six minutes of not knowing. Then a summer of drones and Harpoons, until Russia leaves the island
under fire. The arc is the war's first proof that the weaker side can win at sea by asymmetry.

**The player.**
- A border guard on Zmiinyi (act 1, `campaign.md` chapter 0).
- A Neptune battery's radar operator (act 2, chapter 2).
- A TB2 operator over the island (the later set pieces, an interlude).

**Beats:**
1. Channel 16, all day (c02 §6.1). A horizon that fills with ships, surrender calls every twenty minutes, then
   shells and three hours of air strikes, then the reply. The player cannot win this. They can only answer.
2. The Sapfir trap (§6.2). The rescue that sails into captivity. This is a consequence card, not something
   played.
3. The blip under the clouds (§6.3). Minutes of pondering, two Neptunes, six minutes of not knowing.
4. Fire, tow and the night roll (§6.4). Told from the other side's footage, not played.
5. The dawn hunt and the landing ramp (§6.5–6). A TB2 against Raptors and a Serna, a Tor still turning.
6. Two Harpoons on the Vasily Bekh (§6.8), and the coda: Russia leaves on 30 June.

**Recovery beat.** The Moskva itself, after Channel 16's defeat.

**Levers.**
- Act 1: none but the reply and how the men are split between shelters.
- Act 2: when to fire, and the decoy.
- The hunts: which target, and when.

**What it needs from the engine.**
- The sea: water, a swell, ships.
- A rock island.
- A radar scope as the picture instead of a drone feed.
- Long-range missiles.
- A TB2's view (a drone the player already knows how to read).

The Moskva model is catalogued (`db/research/assets/`). Of the four arcs, this one uses the least of the existing
engine.

## 3. Kursk: the other tempo (2024–2025)

**Chapter:** Kursk (c14), in two tempos.

**The story.**
- The dash: across the border at dawn, the gas station, the checkpoint, the HIMARS on the reserve column, the
  Seim's bridges dropped one by one.
- The long hold: North Koreans in the snow, fibre-optic drones on the road out, and the road itself becoming the
  trap.
- It ends in withdrawal, but it is the only chapter where the player attacks into Russia.

**The player.** The 80th's lead company in the dash. Then an SSO team on the road out.

**Beats:**
1. Dawn at the Sudzha checkpoint (c14 §6.1).
2. The gas station that keeps the gas flowing (§6.2): a beat about what does not stop.
3. The Oktyabrskoye column (§6.3): fourteen trucks of reserves burning on the 38K-017.
4. Flags and prisoners (§6.4): conscripts surrendering by the hundred.
5. Breaking the Seim (§6.5): three bridges, then drones hunting the pontoons.
6. The siege of Malaya Loknya (§6.6).
7. North Koreans in the snow (§6.7), and the road out under fibre-optic drones.

**Recovery beat.** The first days. This is the campaign's one advance that works, and it should feel like it
before the hold turns it back.

**Levers.**
- The dash: speed, and what to bypass.
- The bridges: which, and when.
- The hold: what to keep open.

**What it needs from the engine.**
- Summer roads and columns (Robotyne's kit).
- Trucks.
- Rivers and bridges that fall.
- Snow (Vuhledar's).
- Fibre-optic drones that EW cannot stop (late 2024).

## 4. Azov: Mariupol and the plant (2022)

**Chapter:** Azovstal (c03). Read as Mariupol and Azovstal; if the owner meant the Azov brigade's later fights
(Pokrovsk's Dobropillia salient, c15 §6.3), those belong to the Donbas arc.

**The story.** An encircled city, then a steel plant's tunnels, then a ledger that only goes down until the order
to surrender comes. The player cannot save the city. They can bring wounded out on night flights, ration what is
left, and decide who is evacuated first.

**The player.**
- A Mi-8 crew on the night runs.
- Then the plant's quartermaster (`campaign.md` act 3).

**Beats:**
1. The drama theatre (c03 §6.1): a prelude the player cannot prevent, told without gamifying it.
2. The first night run (§6.2): treetop height for 60 miles, a tree clipped, sixteen wounded out.
3. Fog over the sea (§6.3): guns stripped for 200 kg more medicine, the plant found by its stacks.
4. The Illich breakout (§6.4).
5. Blockade, not storm (§6.5): the map goes quiet and the bombing grows heavier.
6. The field hospital (§6.6).
7. The convoy (§6.7): civilians out, firing resumed the moment the pause ends.
8. "Orest's" light (§6.8): the photographs that leave the plant.

**Recovery beat.** The night runs. Seven flights, and each one that lands is a win.

**Levers.**
- The runs: routes, loads, which wounded.
- The plant: rationing, and the order of evacuation.

**What it needs from the engine.**
- Night and thermal.
- A flown Mi-8 (catalogued).
- Ruins at plant scale.
- Tunnels as a map table, not a 3D space.

This is the hardest material, and the arc where the ethics guidance in `chapters.md` matters most.

## 5. The campaign's open questions: recommendations

These are recommendations for `campaign.md` §"Open questions"; the decisions are the owner's.

1. **Refusing the historical outcome.**
   - Let it happen: the quiet card ends the chapter, the ledger shows what the refusal changed, and nothing comments
     on it.
   - Locking the outcome makes the player a passenger; a counterfactual ledger invites "winning" the war.
2. **Interludes.** Make them separate and skippable, placed by date between the chapters. Coupling them to a ground
   chapter's days is truer, but it makes both harder to build and to replay.
3. **How much of hold-then-withdraw each chapter keeps.**
   - Keep the full shape for Avdiivka only.
   - Cut Bakhmut to the road and the rotation, and Pokrovsk to its last two acts, as `campaign.md` proposes.
   - Three full sieges in a row would teach helplessness, not cost.
4. **Hub or linear.** Linear on first play; a calendar hub afterwards, to replay a chapter and compare ledgers.
5. **Callsigns across chapters.**
   - No: each diorama has its own roster in its file, as Vuhledar and Robotyne do now.
   - A persistent cast would need deaths that persist, and the record does not support inventing them.
   - The player's own callsign could persist as the one thread, if the owner wants one.
6. **Which night closes.**
   - Keep 1–2 July 2026 (c18).
   - Rule: a newer night replaces it only if a dossier exists for it and it is worse by the record's own count.
7. **Where Robotyne sits.** After Bakhmut, as `campaign.md` proposes: the mirror reads better after a hold than
   straight after Vuhledar.
8. **Bilohorivka.** Promote it, but only once it has a dossier; until then it stays in the reserve.
9. **The energy winter.**
   - Commission the dossier first.
   - It would make Kyiv a triptych, and it is the war's civilian centre, but it is a map-table chapter, which is
     the least proven form.
10. **Table chapters as a person's view.** Prototype one first: Kherson's fires cell, on the belief-state map, with
    late reports. Decide after playing it.
11. **Civilians in the finale.** Test the civil-defence meter for tone with someone outside the project before it
    ships.
12. **Difficulty.**
    - History sets the ration; the player sets only how much of the ledger they see.
    - The shell famine is meant to be the hardest act.

## 6. The drones through the arcs

The new research (db/staging/research/, 2026-10-15) says the player's drones should change with the date, and that
change is the war's own competence curve. In outline:

| when | where | the player flies | what flies against them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb–Apr 2022 | Kyiv, Mariupol, Snake Island | a few commercial quadcopters and a TB2 | little; EW in places |
| Feb 2023 | Vuhledar | a Mavic 3 and a Matrice 30T: spot and adjust; drops minor; no FPVs | Orlans cueing guns in 3–5 minutes; DJI operators located in 5–6 |
| Jun 2023 | Robotyne | Mavic 3/3T recon by day; night bombers only when Russian EW lapses; FPVs scarce | Lancets near their peak; EW at the line and 7 km back, switched on and off |
| late 2023 – 2024 | Avdiivka, Krynky | FPVs as a daily stock (about 20 a day per strike company by September 2023) | more EW; Russian FPVs outnumbering ours; video jamming from summer 2024 |
| 2024–2025 | Kursk, Pokrovsk | FPVs by the hundred thousand, heavy bombers, interceptors, terminal autonomy | fibre-optic FPVs immune to jamming; Rubikon hunting drone teams |

So the first two dioramas teach the drone as eyes, and the FPV arrives with Avdiivka. For roadmap step 7 that
means the owner's first call: whether the two dioramas built so far keep to their dates (no FPV at Vuhledar, a rare
one at Robotyne), or give the player the later kit early.
