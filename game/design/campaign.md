# The campaign

Status: in progress (2026-10-11). This is a proposal for the order the chapters play in and the arc they
make together. It sits on top of `chapters.md` (the slate and its principles) and `next.md` (the
Vuhledar and Robotyne build plan). Nothing here is settled; the open questions are at the end.

## The arc in one paragraph

Stay chronological. Run eight acts. Bracket the whole thing with Kyiv: the city held in 2022 and the city
that still pays every night in 2026. Alternate the ground chapters with the sea, air and covert chapters,
because the war itself alternates that way. On the ground Ukraine loses slowly to mass. At sea, in the air
and in the deep it wins cleanly by asymmetry. That heartbeat is the arc's engine and it needs no invention.

The psychological spine is a competence arc: the player gets better at a war that gets worse. Fixed
outcomes risk learned helplessness. The antidote is not softer outcomes. It is marginal control that
visibly matters, a cost ledger the player owns, and a recovery beat after each bleak chapter. The target
feeling is poignancy, not fun and not despair.

## The acts and what each does to the player

- **Act I, Survive (0 to 3).** Three outcomes the player cannot change. Kyiv holds, the Moskva sinks,
  Azovstal walks out. The player learns the contract early: the outcome is history, the price is yours.
  Bucha and Olenivka are end cards, never play.
- **Act II, The tide (Bilohorivka, 4, 5, the truck).** The only act where the map moves the player's way.
  Both main chapters are table chapters, not field dioramas. Kharkiv is what Kherson's pressure bought, and
  the player should feel that the slow chapter paid for the fast one.
- **Act III, The grind (6, 7).** Vuhledar then Bakhmut. The clean defence, then the dirty one. The player
  is good at the kill zone by now. Bakhmut teaches that competence does not settle the hold-or-leave
  argument.
- **Act IV, The attempt (8, the fleet, 9).** Robotyne is the mirror of Vuhledar. Krynky is its epilogue
  and the campaign's harshest chapter. It stays in full. The sea interlude between them is the bright
  counterpoint the summer actually had.
- **Act V, The famine (10, Kyrylivka, the ships).** Avdiivka without shells, while the A-50s and three
  ships die. January to March 2024 is the war's clearest statement of the asymmetry thesis. The interludes
  should land between Avdiivka's days by date.
- **Act VI, The gamble (11, Tarkhankut).** Audacity, then the bill. The ally pauses intelligence on the
  day the road is cut. The pipe surfaces in Sudzha, and the player who did not watch it at Avdiivka
  recognises it.
- **Act VII, The system (Spiderweb, 12, 13).** Pokrovsk is the system beating you at 30 km depth.
  Kupiansk is you learning it. Spiderweb opens the act so the player enters 2025 knowing what Ukraine can
  do far away before seeing what it cannot do close in.
- **Act VIII, Now (14, 15).** Vivaldi is a corps that fights as a system and wins something limited. The
  night over Kyiv is the price the city still pays. Chapter 1 and chapter 15 are the same city, the same
  outcome, and a completely different war. That rhyme is the arc in one image.

### Where harsh was chosen over something else

- The finale ends with 31 dead and 4 of 28 ballistic missiles stopped.
- Krynky and Zenit stay in the campaign, at full weight.
- Pokrovsk ends with no order to withdraw ever given.
- The win band in every hold-then-withdraw chapter forces the player to leave as late as the command did.
- Channel 16 corrects the "13 dead" story in its own debrief, so the honesty contract is set in the first
  ten minutes.

### Recovery beats

Each bleak chapter is followed by something short and clean before the next bleak one. Azovstal is
followed by Bilohorivka. Bakhmut by Robotyne's first morning. Krynky by Avdiivka's radar interludes.
Kursk by Tarkhankut and Spiderweb. Pokrovsk by Kupiansk. This is deliberate pacing, not a softening of
the outcomes.

## Alternatives considered

1. **End on Vivaldi instead of the night.** Vivaldi ends on competence and "only the first of the
   seasons". Honest and open. The night is preferred because it closes the Kyiv frame, returns the player
   to civilians, and is harsher. A third option nests the night inside Vivaldi by date and ends on the
   season cards.
2. **A different prologue.** The Hostomel helicopter stream is the cinematic opening. Brovary is the
   mechanical one. Channel 16 is the thesis, costs almost nothing to build, and sets the honesty contract
   in the first debrief. Hostomel and Brovary then live inside chapter 1.
3. **The hold-then-withdraw cluster.** Bakhmut, Krynky, Avdiivka, Kursk and Pokrovsk share one strategic
   shape. Five full-length versions of it will exhaust players. The proposal gives the full treatment to
   Avdiivka only, because it is the tightest and has the clearest three tempos, and gives each of the
   others a different body and verb (the driver, the coxswain, the SSO team on the road, the drone line).
   The alternative is to keep all five at full length as the slate implies.
4. **Split the multi-year chapters or keep them whole.** Kerch, the Black Sea and Snake Island span
   years. As single thematic chapters they break the chronology and the alternation rhythm. The proposal
   splits them into dated interludes. Keeping them whole is the current slate.
5. **Act V as three parallel clocks.** Avdiivka on the ground, the A-50 in the air, the ships at sea,
   switched between in one winter. Truer to how it felt. More to build.
6. **Pokrovsk before Kupiansk or after.** They overlap in time. Kupiansk second makes the act rise into
   Vivaldi. Pokrovsk second ends the act on the harsher note.
7. **A shorter spine.** If effort forces it: Irpin, Azovstal, the bridges, Vuhledar, Robotyne, Avdiivka,
   Kursk, Pokrovsk, the night. Everything else becomes an interlude. The cheap chapters are the radar and
   feed ones: Channel 16, the Moskva, the A-50, Spiderweb's strike.
8. **Thematic tracks instead of chronology.** Field, sea, deep, city, each chronological, player's choice
   of track. More variety, no arc. Not recommended.

## Observations

### On the design docs

- **The two design docs disagree on the debrief.** `chapters.md` says contested outcomes show who claims
  what in the debrief. `next.md` says the record never appears in the interface and the ledger speaks in
  the day's own terms. The newer plan should win, and the line in `chapters.md` needs changing. The end
  card remains the one place the record narrates.
- **What is built is what was not picked.** Vuhledar and Robotyne are both italic proposals in the slate,
  chosen first for engineering reasons (one field, two seasons, two sides of the same mechanics). Good
  reasons, but the campaign order should not inherit the build order.
- **Two engines, not one.** Kharkiv, Kherson, Kursk's dash, Spiderweb's long game and Vivaldi's build-up
  are too big for a field diorama. The map table already planned for the night phase is their engine. It
  does not break the no-god's-eye rule if the map is a belief state with late, noisy reports, which the
  prior-art research already recommends.

### On the material

- **The cast is already in the record.** The 72nd fights at Moshchun, Brovary and Vuhledar. The 47th at
  Robotyne, Stepove and Pokrovsk. The marines at Zmiinyi, Illich and Krynky. Azov and the 3rd Assault from
  Azovstal through Bakhmut's flanks to Dobropillia and Vivaldi. Fictional call signs tied to those real
  formations can recur, age and thin out. The last ledger can show who from chapter 1 is still on it.
- **Motifs recur without being written.** The pipe three times: it hits you at Avdiivka, again at Sudzha,
  and you beat it at Kupiansk. The mined field twice, from each side. The river crossing twice, Bilohorivka
  and Krynky, from each side. The flag war everywhere, from Prigozhin's and Shoigu's to the one destroyed
  within an hour on the Pokrovsk sign.
- **Silence is a campaign-long lever.** Kherson was signalled, Kharkiv silent, Krynky announced, Kupiansk
  silent, Vivaldi silent. Its historical setting alternates, so the player feels both sides of it.
- **The ally is weather.** HIMARS arriving, the supplemental stalling, the intelligence pause, Starlink
  refused over Crimea and later cut for the Russians. These stay scripted and visible only as what came up
  the road. That is harsher and truer than a diplomacy screen.
- **Every withdrawal in the record is late.** RUSI on Bakhmut, the soldiers on Avdiivka, the officers on
  Krynky, Oryx on Kursk, and Pokrovsk with no order at all. The win band makes the player repeat that.
- **Capture is the humane verb.** The ambush's prisoners, the North Korean two, the 250 at Nove, the 983
  at Kursk. The real points economy scores it. It is the one win the war offers that is palatable without
  softening anything. The prisoners vignette stops at "taken alive", as `next.md` decided.
- **Gaps in the slate.** The summer 2022 artillery war has no chapter, which Bilohorivka fixes. The winter
  of 2022 to 2023 energy strikes would make the Kyiv frame a triptych. The Kakhovka flood and Chasiv Yar
  are absent. None is urgent.
- **The last chapter is a slot.** The night dossier already lists alternatives from 2025 and 2026. As the
  war goes on, the final chapter will be replaced, and the design should expect that.

### On psychology

- **Learned helplessness is the real risk.** Fixed outcomes plus visible cost can produce "nothing I do
  matters". Three things prevent it: the margin must be legible (the ledger shows what the player's
  choices changed), the player must be allowed to be good at something (the kill zone, the radar, the
  boat), and bleak chapters must not run back to back.
- **Agency must be local, never strategic.** The player never decides whether to hold Bakhmut. They decide
  who drives the road tonight. This keeps the fixed outcome from feeling like the game's choice.
- **Debrief tone matters more than content.** The ledger must never congratulate and never scold. Numbers,
  names that are call signs, what came up the road, what is left. The silence after the last line is
  where the feeling happens.
- **The player will test the frame.** Someone will refuse to withdraw at Avdiivka to see what happens. What
  the game does then is a design decision, not a bug (see open questions).

## Open questions

1. **What happens when the player refuses the historical outcome?** Holding past the withdrawal date,
   pulling Azovstal's surrender forward, not launching the Neptunes. Proposal: let the quiet card end the
   chapter, with a ledger that shows fewer dead and less Russian cost, and no comment. Alternative: lock
   the outcome mechanically so refusing is impossible. Alternative: a counterfactual ledger that is
   explicitly marked as not the record.
2. **Interludes: separate chapters or scenes inside the neighbouring ground chapter?** Dating the A-50
   ambushes and the three ships between Avdiivka's days is truest but couples the chapters. Separate
   interludes are simpler to build and to skip.
3. **How much of the hold-then-withdraw shape does each chapter keep?** The proposal cuts Bakhmut to the
   road and the rotation, and Pokrovsk to its last two acts. The slate implies full chapters. Dossiers
   c07 and c15 have the material for either.
4. **Does the campaign have a hub, or is it strictly linear?** A linear campaign gives the arc. A hub
   (a calendar, a map of the country, a notebook) lets the player replay and compare ledgers. Both can
   coexist: linear first play, hub afterwards.
5. **Do call signs persist across chapters?** If yes, who owns the roster, how do they age, and does the
   player see them die on the ledger or only find them missing? If no, each chapter is self-contained and
   the cast motif is lost.
6. **Which chapter closes, and when does it get replaced?** The night over Kyiv is the current proposal.
   The dossier for c18 lists 7 Sep 2025 and 19 Jul 2026 as alternatives. There should be a rule for when a
   newer night replaces it.
7. **Where does Robotyne sit?** `next.md` defers this. The proposal places it as chapter 8, straight
   after Bakhmut, as the mirror of Vuhledar. The alternative is to keep it adjacent to Vuhledar (same
   field, back to back) and let chronology bend once.
8. **Bilohorivka's status.** It is in the slate's reserve. The proposal promotes it as the single clean
   win of the 2022 artillery war and the first recovery beat. It has no dossier yet.
9. **The energy winter.** A third Kyiv chapter (winter 2022 to 2023, the grid) would make the frame a
   triptych. No dossier, no slot. Worth a dossier before deciding.
10. **Can the table chapters stay a person's view?** Kherson, Kharkiv and Kursk's dash are played at a
    map. The rule is no god's-eye camera. The proposal says a belief-state map with late reports keeps the
    rule. This needs a prototype to confirm it feels like a person and not a general.
11. **How are civilians present in the finale?** The night over Kyiv is the only chapter where the player
    defends civilians directly and fails most of them. The ethics guidance says civilians are present
    without gamifying their harm. The civil-defence meter in c18 is the proposed answer. It needs testing
    for tone.
12. **Difficulty.** Rationing is the slider in `next.md`. Across a campaign, does the ration follow
    history (the shell famine is the hardest act by design) or the player's choice? Proposal: history sets
    the ration, the player sets only how much of the ledger they see.
